The question

MORE BODY.
SAME RUNNER.

The goal is not to turn a marathoner into a bodybuilder. It is to develop the body around an already proven running engine.

We want chest that projects, sleeves that fit, thighs that occupy the shorts, and calves and hamstrings that look capable of producing force — while the waist stays narrow and the running stays light, elastic and fast. Added tissue has to earn its place.

Development map

WHERE THE
TISSUE GOES.

01Chest depth

Build the front of the torso so the athlete no longer reads flat from profile or inside ordinary clothing.

02Upper-arm girth

Bias triceps long head and brachialis alongside biceps so the arm gains thickness, not only a peak.

03Medial thigh

Adductors and knee-dominant work create inner-thigh density while contributing to a stronger hip system.

04Hamstrings

Keep both hip-extension and knee-flexion strength. The posterior chain should become stronger without becoming expensive to run on.

05Soleus + calf

Straight-knee and bent-knee work build the lower leg where visible development and running capacity can overlap.

06Upper back

Add torso depth and shoulder support through rows and pulldowns with minimal cost to the legs and spine.

Glutes are not the growth priority. Adrian already carries comparatively developed glutes; they move to maintenance so recovery can be spent elsewhere.

Evidence standard

TWO STORIES.
ONE ATHLETE.

Every update asks two questions: did the body become more developed, and did the runner remain Adrian?

Body

Did tissue change?

Standardized photos, weekly-average body mass, waist, chest, relaxed upper arm, mid-thigh and calf.

Strength

Did capacity change?

Actual load and rep progression on representative lifts. No invented maxes and no need for maximal testing.

Running

Did the engine stay intact?

Normal / flat / heavy next-run report, key-run execution, gait-changing soreness, persistent fatigue and race evidence.

Athlete

Will he keep doing it?

Adherence, appetite, sleep, enjoyment and friction. A plan that an athlete repeatedly abandons is not a successful intervention.

Current interpretation · 18 Sep 2026

The early signal is not “hypertrophy achieved.” It is that Adrian is tolerating the work, engaging with it, and giving us enough adherence to become more precise.

What changed

Week 02 adds cable access and redirects volume toward chest depth, arm girth, medial thigh, hamstrings and lower-leg development.

What did not

The primary constraint remains the same: strength loses priority when it repeatedly makes the running worse.

What is missing

No measured body-mass trend, circumference change or longitudinal photo comparison exists yet. Those claims stay open.

What comes next

Confirm Week 02 inside a rising run week, then enter Density + Force only if the running response remains normal.

Running anchors

RACE WEEKS
CHANGE THE GYM.

The running schedule belongs to Adrian and his running coach. FORM strength does not replace it. Race weeks reduce lower-body volume and eccentric cost first.
