02 / External speed versus internal cost

When does 7:42–7:20 become low aerobic?

The goal is not enough cumulative minutes alone. It is repeatable running at a similar pace with lower internal effort, stable heart rate and normal recovery. We need comparable conditions and a defensible individual low-aerobic boundary before calling this pace easy.

What changed with speed?

From the mile to the first three 400 m repetitions, measured step length increased from 1.50 m to about 1.61 m; cadence increased from 178 to about 182 steps/min; contact time shortened from 218 to about 203 ms. This is a speed-dependent change, not proof of an economy improvement. October 22 work-only averages cannot be compared directly as a trend with October 18 whole-session averages, which include recovery/idle periods.

To judge adaptation, repeat similar pace bands on comparable surfaces with similar shoes, fatigue, temperature and recording method. Read internal load (HR and subjective effort) alongside cadence, stride, ground contact and next-day lower-leg response. 04 / Making speed last

Access is not yet ownership.

A quick 400 m at 6:00 pace and two uninterrupted miles at 6:00 pace are different accomplishments. Duration is a useful exposure measure; longer continuous holds, rising HR cost, perceived effort and next-day lower-leg response determine whether the speed is becoming durable.

Nov 2 · Controlled speed, new arm sensation

Twenty 200 m reps, 1:15 recoveries, and a reported steady 6–7/10 effort. The last five were nearly identical in time while Brice matched runners' rhythm, pulled his arms wider and farther back, and felt more propulsion. This is a credible report of what he felt, not a measurement of arm-generated drive. The next test is recognizing that rhythm without an external pacer and confirming normal lower-leg response after same-day Glute A hinge strength.

Oct 26 · Company and attention

Brice began conservatively with another runner helping set a steady rhythm. His cue was “Relax. It's marathon pace,” meaning controlled effort, not a measured marathon prescription. He felt strong after morning strength work. A negative-splitting group passed in the final loop; he joined and focused on moving lightly rather than pushing. Company, leg strength and attention are working variables. The slight middle dip could reflect relaxed pacing as well as attention drifting. Without the group, the next lap's pace is unknowable. Practice regaining fluid rhythm at useful moments, not every run.

Oct 24 · Fluid, still consciously held

After roughly three social miles and a longer self-selected continuation, the last two complete miles were 7:35.1 and 7:23.6 (about 7:29/mi combined). The athlete felt fluid, but maintaining rhythm and symmetry took attention. He sensed that falling off beat would make it harder to regain the stride. At faster lap pace, step length rose to 1.25–1.27 m, cadence to 170 spm and contact shortened to 249–252 ms. These changes are speed-dependent and do not prove economy or automatic coordination. Last two lap HR averages were 178 and 174 bpm, above the watch's recent estimated threshold HR. Recovery after this longer run is pending.

The retirement rule for 7:42–7:20

When the corridor becomes reliably low-aerobic in comparable conditions, with relaxed perceived effort, stable control and acceptable recovery, we can stop tracking it as a special speed milestone and retain it under ordinary aerobic mileage. There is no preset number of minutes at which this happens.
