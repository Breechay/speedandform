02 / CHOICE ARCHITECTURE

One purpose per session.

No chasing all objectives every day. Choose A for upper strength, B for lower strength, C for a short physique-focused accessory session, and D for elastic preparation. A, B, and C are complete voluntary strength options, not mandatory weekly appointments. Elastic work is preserved for a later phase and is not part of the current workout bank.

Start resistance work with five to ten minutes of general preparation and unloaded/reduced-load practice sets. Working loads should allow about two to three good reps in reserve; avoid grinders and maximal efforts. Keep running and lifting recovery costs visible together.

    Why speed may become more natural · three mechanisms

    Coordination: Repetition gives the nervous system practice at the stride and timing required. Evidence: pace, cadence and movement consistency.

    Aerobic capacity: Appropriate training develops the ability to sustain a speed with lower relative effort. Evidence: matched-pace HR, perceived effort and Garmin's VO₂ estimate as a secondary marker.

    Strength + elastic response: Loading builds useful force capacity. Heavy strength is in the current bank; impact-oriented elastic drills remain a later, clearance-gated phase. Evidence: actual lifting performance and future running comparison.

    Read the measurements as exposure → cost → movement → durability → recovery. Fifteen-second pace bins are recording categories, not separate muscle-fiber thresholds. A trend does not by itself prove what caused it.
