# Rebuilt Athlete: velocity automaticity observation

**Filed:** October 13, 2026  
**Subject:** Brice Ikouebe  
**Study:** FORM Study 002 · The Rebuilt Athlete  
**Status:** athlete report plus device evidence and working coaching interpretation. This is not a new training prescription.

## Question

Is faster running becoming easier to access because the movement is becoming more automatic again, or did the October 13 session simply cost less because it was shorter, cooler and more broken up?

Keep both possibilities alive. Athlete report is evidence. It is not a diagnosis.

## October 12 · faster running felt available but busy

### Purpose

The intended session was a 20-minute warm-up followed by 2 x 10 minutes of faster running.

### Execution

The warm-up was 20:00, 2.67 miles at 7:29/mi.

During the first faster block, Brice reported that the pace required more breathing and more conscious attention than he wanted. Roughly five minutes into the work he decided not to force the original structure. He broke the remaining faster running into shorter 400 m and 800 m pieces and used the resets to return to fluid movement.

The complete activity was 5.27 miles in 36:53 moving time, 52:35 elapsed, at 7:00/mi average moving pace.

### Athlete report

Brice did not describe the pace as mechanically impossible. The problem was that the speed still required too much processing: breathing, attention and the effort of staying relaxed.

He finished feeling strong and without an immediate sense that the session had damaged the legs. He later decided not to recreate a next-day performance test and to let sleep, food and recovery determine the next move.

## October 13 · the pace appeared before the plan

### Purpose

Brice started expecting an easy run. He did not plan to begin near 7:00/mi.

### Execution

His legs naturally settled near that pace and he reported that it felt easy and fluid. The outing became 3 x 1 mile with 2:30 recovery.

Recorded mile splits:

| Mile | Pace | Average HR | Brice's read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 7:05/mi | 141 bpm | The pace appeared without being planned. |
| 2 | 6:40/mi | 169 bpm | Faster than necessary, probably excitement. |
| 3 | 6:48/mi | 168 bpm | Deliberately relaxed to stay comfortable and complete the third mile. He would have stopped at two rather than force it. |

Total: 3.00 miles in 20:36 at 6:51/mi average pace. Elapsed time was 24:18.

### Athlete report

Brice's main observation was leg fluidity. He described the faster pace as something the legs found rather than something he had to manufacture.

He believes he is retraining automaticity at faster velocities: the body becoming reacquainted with the pace so that less conscious management is required.

The 6:48 third mile was not a fade. It was a deliberate reduction in pace to stay relaxed.

## Working interpretation

**Moderate confidence:** faster pace is becoming easier to access without forcing it.

The strongest evidence is Brice's report that he started expecting an easy run, did not choose 7:00/mi, and noticed the legs moving there comfortably. The second and third mile repetitions then remained close in pace, while the third was intentionally relaxed rather than involuntarily slower.

This supports an automaticity hypothesis: repeated exposure may be helping the nervous system and movement pattern become reacquainted with faster running so that the pace requires less conscious control.

It does **not** establish that 6:40/mi is now easy, sustainable continuously, equivalent to threshold, or evidence of half-marathon readiness.

## Competing explanations

October 13 was also:

- shorter;
- cooler;
- broken into one-mile repetitions;
- separated by 2:30 recoveries;
- performed after sleep, food and a night of recovery.

Those differences can reduce physiological cost independently of motor familiarity. The study cannot isolate weather, workout structure, recovery state or automaticity from these two outings.

## Recovery boundary

The October 11 follow-up remains the latest confirmed lower-limb recovery report: no lower-limb issues since the preceding runs, with muscular soreness easing toward normal.

The immediate October 13 report is that the legs felt fluid and the third mile stayed comfortable when deliberately relaxed. The 24–48-hour lower-limb response after the October 12–13 faster exposures is still pending.

## Next smallest useful question

Do not prove fitness the next morning.

First see whether the legs remain quiet after the two faster exposures. Then observe whether the same relaxed rhythm appears during ordinary continuous running without assigning a fast finish.

Later, a longer faster bout can test whether the pace stays automatic when the recovery breaks disappear.

## What this changes

The study now distinguishes **access to speed** from **ownership of speed**.

Access is returning quickly. Ownership means the pace can be held with relaxed breathing, low attentional cost, stable movement and normal recovery.

No new mileage quota, canonical FORM assignment, race prediction or medical conclusion is created by this observation.
