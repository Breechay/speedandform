## The proposition

**Train speed first, not exhaustion first.** The first target is the ability to produce and repeat fast, relaxed, mechanically controlled running without the first stride's muscling/forcing. Only then ask for longer sustained running at those speeds. Easy aerobic mileage, long runs and strength are supporting inputs, not a weekly competition.

Three long-range outcomes, **not January 2027 guarantees**:

| Outcome | Exact race demand | Why it belongs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 mile under 5:00 | under 74.6 seconds per 400 m | speed ceiling and coordination |
| 5K in 16:35 | about 5:20/mi; about 79.6 seconds per 400 m | speed endurance and eventual primary racing focus |
| 10K around 6:00 to 5:50/mi | about 37:17 to 36:15 overall | durability / aerobic strength; deliberately less aggressive than a theoretically race-equivalent 10K for a 16:35 5K |

The eventual mile and 5K race abilities are more closely related on standard endurance race-equivalence calculations than the stated 10K goal. The 10K acts as the durable aerobic bridge, not a separate speed ceiling. Race equivalents are modelling assumptions, not proof of current readiness.

## Architecture: 12 weeks, three four-week acts

| Act / dates | Priority | Working weekly miles | What the gate measures |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| **Act I: Access** · Oct 26–Nov 22 | 200/400/600 rhythm; relaxed mechanics; deliberate recovery between fast exposures | around 20–24, with a lower first/recovery week acceptable | Can short work repeat without grinding, focal tibial symptoms or next-day deterioration? |
| **Act II: Repeat** · Nov 23–Dec 20 | Hold smooth movement over 600–800 m; retain brisk but restrained 200s | around 22–27 | Are 600s/800s repeatable at similar cost, on matched sessions, with two days of workable recovery? |
| **Act III: Extend** · Dec 21–Jan 17 | Bridge to 800–1200 m and controlled longer aerobic support | around 24–30 | Does an even, non-maximal 1-mile/3K reference and regular easy running justify a true 5K-specific season? |

Mileage bands are **planning envelopes, not minimums or weekly targets**. A 16–20-mile absorption week can be better than pushing to reach the table. No sudden automatic increase to 40–50 miles, no required 9/10-mile long-run ratchet, and no “make-up mileage” after a rest day. Higher volume belongs after proof of repeatable recovery.

### Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Role | Training example (private coach planning, not an app prescription) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monday evening · iRUN** | Shared group outing, mainly easy initially | Structured easy time and a few relaxed strides. Any future speed sessions are separately coach-authorized, not inherited from a group workout |
| Tuesday | Strength and/or easy aerobic support | Upper strength emphasis; optional 30–45 min easy HR-guided run |
| Wednesday | Recovery / short easy / lower-body support | Yoga and mobility; lower-body lifting planned so Thursday track mechanics are not compromised |
| **Thursday morning · FORM Track** | Weekly quality-session anchor | Short-to-long speed-development sequence, one primary hard run in the week. Different targets for each athlete sharing the same track |
| Friday | Strength / no run | Upper-body strength or mobility |
| Saturday | Social easy / aerobic extension | Comfortable 5–8 miles as part of the week's real load; no forced long-run target |
| Sunday | Flexible recovery and strength | Planned lower-body training or rest, distributed to avoid soreness before Monday quality |

The goal is roughly **3–5 runs each week**, normally **one genuine high-intensity session** initially. The two group gatherings need not both be demanding. Later controlled aerobic development can sometimes take the second quality slot after a coach review; not automatically every Thursday. Keep the existing Forge physique program as the strength source, and arrange **two upper / two lower or lower-accessory doses** around actual run load; do not add a second high-fatigue leg day solely to fill a schedule.

### Running intensity boundaries

- Easy support: HR and conversational effort first, flat and familiar route when practical, no faster finish. A pace is not automatically Zone 2 because it feels smooth.
- Speed skill: relaxed, full movement control, no muscling through the first repetitions. Walk/jog recoveries are intentional.
- Continuous support: easy volume first; later controlled steady running by effort, not a forced 6:00–6:15 pace prescription.
- Protect speed consistency over cumulative speed minutes. Reduce demand when leg response, mechanics or post-session recovery conflicts with the intent. These contingencies stay in coach notes; each published athlete Week should have one clear prescribed session.

### Volume distribution examples, not prescriptions

- At **20 miles**: two group outings around 4–5 miles each (warm-up/cooldown included), one 3–4-mile recovery jog and a 6–7-mile comfortable Saturday.
- At **25 miles**: two group outings around 5 miles each, two easy outings totaling 8 miles, one 7-mile Saturday.
- At **30 miles**: only after stable response, similar quality slots with a little additional easy time on separate days; not more fast reps simply to make 30.

Do not force the examples to exactly 20/25/30 through arbitrary distances. Use actual total training, not just the session “work distance”; warm-ups, cooldowns and jog recoveries contribute impact.

## What happens after January 17?

**Gate outcome A:** Speed feels available and the 600–1000 m work is controlled, but aerobic recovery/volume or tibial response is inconsistent. Repeat the most productive act with smaller load. Do not launch a race-specific block because the calendar changed.

**Gate outcome B:** Sustained, repeatable 800–1200 m quality, stable next-day leg response, three to five weeks of comfortable base mileage, no mechanical grinding. Start an **8–12-week 5K-specific extension** with fewer but longer repetitions, controlled aerobic strength and a benchmark 5K when justified. The 16:35 goal remains a long-range aspiration.

**Mile specialist block:** After maintaining 5K-strength and bone tolerance, a separate **6–8-week mile-specific sharpening** period can selectively introduce more recovered 200/300/400 m work near sub-5 race velocity. Sub-5 pace is not obligatory in the present twelve weeks.

**10K:** Continue easy aerobic support now. Train specifically for 5:50–6:00 pace across 10K after a reliable continuous aerobic base has been re-established; those goals should not all be chased simultaneously in one 12-week period.

## Evidence measurements and stop/go authority

Follow [the Run Evidence Review Standard](RUN-EVIDENCE-REVIEW-STANDARD.md). For useful workouts review:
- Work-only time, distance and lap-average paces; sample-derived time in 15-second speed bands when original FIT samples are present.
- Step length, cadence, ground-contact time, vertical oscillation and ratio **at comparable pace and measured scope**.
- HR at matched pace, session context, self-reported exertion, recovery between reps and next-day lower-leg response.
- No speculative stride changes or muscle-fiber conclusions from a single day. No invented medical clearance.

**Coach gate, private:** Immediate focal tibial pain, new swelling, altered gait, or increasing symptoms after or the next morning means no fast progression and should prompt clinical review. This athlete's April injury was a traumatic shaft fracture, not automatically a tibial stress fracture; published return-to-run bone-stress injury guidance is contextual evidence, not a personalized clearance protocol. Coordinate with orthopedic/PT recommendations where applicable.
