There is no blank space after Orlando. The first two weeks have a job: absorb the race, restore normal movement and frequency, then arrive ready to train again. No performance test belongs here.

RECOVER

Unload Orlando and the 15-week accumulation. Absence of intensity is intentional, not missing work.

  • 4–5 running days maximum
  • ~24–30 miles or less if response asks for less
  • Easy only · no pace requirement
  • Race debrief captured
  • No long-run achievement to prove

RETURN

Restore rhythm without creating a new performance question.

  • 5–6 running days
  • ~34–40 miles
  • Easy frequency returns
  • Short strides or hills if comfortable
  • Relaxed 9–10 mile long run · no test

06 · Hypotheses

WHAT WE THINK.
BEFORE WE KNOW.

These are working claims. They are allowed to be wrong. The value of the study is that the work can change the theory rather than being forced to confirm it.
H01 · Late access

Move the same capability later.

If Act I establishes the band continuously, then progressively moving it deeper into longer runs should improve access after accumulated load.

H02 · Ceiling

Do not trade speed for distance.

Small doses of faster work should preserve speed reserve and keep marathon rhythm below the athlete's available ceiling.

H03 · Restraint

The closing 10K starts before mile 20.

What the athlete spends early changes what can still be produced late. Pacing restraint is part of durability, not separate from it.

H04 · Cost

Progress is not always a bigger ask.

After the major closing-10K rehearsal, a smaller late-run task should be repeatable with lower visible cost.

H05 · Individual response

One method can become two assignments.

Hope and José can begin from the same theory without being forced to remain symmetrical when their evidence diverges.

H06 · Fueling

Late pace needs interpretable conditions.

Fueling, hydration and environment are part of the long-run record. A late slowdown cannot automatically be called a durability failure.
