01 · PrimerKnow what comes next.

Eight runs.
Eight stations.

HYROX Singles is eight 1 km runs interrupted by eight fixed stations in a fixed order. Open vs Pro changes load; Pro is a division, not an earned professional rank.

Run. Complete the station. Find your running rhythm again. That is the FORM lens: train the whole race, including what happens between tasks.

Open, Pro and Elite

Open and Pro use different prescribed loads. Pro entry is not an earned professional rank. Elite racing has a separate qualification system. Confirm the athlete’s event and division, then check the official rulebook and qualification rules before prescribing. This page does not maintain current ranking cutoffs.

02 · DoctrineFour things to remember.

Four rules
to race by.

A faster station helps only if the seconds saved outweigh the cost elsewhere in the race.

01 · Clear

Leave ready to run.

Use smooth movements and controlled effort. Check whether pushing harder saves time once you include the next run.

02 · Return

Return to running immediately.

Leave the station and settle back into a sustainable rhythm. Watch how long breathing, posture and pace take to recover.

03 · Own

Choose a pace you can repeat.

Choose a pace you can return to across all eight runs. The first kilometer should support the finish.

04 · Spend

Push when you can hold it.

Near the finish, increase your effort if your movements stay clean. On wall balls, keep completing good reps and take short breaks when needed.

01 · Total race time

Where can you save time?

Look at running, stations and Roxzone separately. Choose one place where you can realistically save time.

Try this: Long transitions? Check your route and stops. One slow station? Review your breaks and missed reps.

See the calculation

Runs + stations + Roxzone + penalties

31:20 + 24:00 + 4:00 = 59:20

Add a penalty only if it is not already counted in your splits.

02 · Station + next run

Did pushing harder help?

Time the station and the run after it together. A faster station helps only if the time saved is greater than the time lost afterward.

Try this: Try the station slightly slower, then repeat the same run. Compare both totals.

See the calculation

Station time + next run time

Harder: 2:30 + 4:15 = 6:45
Controlled: 2:40 + 3:55 = 6:35

The controlled pair saves 10 seconds. Repeat with the same load, distance and rest. Wall balls have no next run.

03 · Run gap

Why is your race running slower?

Compare your race pace with your current threshold pace. The gap shows how much slower you ran; it cannot tell you why.

Try this: If the gap keeps growing in similar sessions, try a calmer opening or a more controlled station. Change one thing at a time.

See the calculation

Race pace − current threshold pace

4:20 − 4:00 = +0:20/km

Use known running distances and a recent threshold test. An old personal best is not your current benchmark.

04 · Speed retained

Is your running fitness showing up?

This percentage compares your race running speed with your current threshold speed. Read it alongside your actual pace.

Try this: Threshold improves but race pace stays the same? Test station effort and how quickly you settle back into running.

See the calculation

Threshold pace ÷ race pace × 100

240 ÷ 260 × 100 = 92.3%

Use seconds per kilometre for both paces. A higher percentage is not progress if it only rose because your threshold got slower.

05 · Late change

Can you hold your pace at the end?

Compare your first three runs with your last three. A slower finish is a reason to review how you spent your effort earlier.

Try this: Try a calmer opening and controlled early stations. Keep the change if your later runs and total time improve.

See the calculation

Late pace − early pace

4:25 − 4:10 = +0:15/km

Divide total run time by total distance for Runs 1–3 and Runs 6–8. Check that the splits cover comparable parts of the course.

Start with the runner you are now.

Use a recent running test to choose a starting pace. Then practise it after stations. Adjust it from what you can repeat, rather than adding a fixed number of seconds to your 10K pace.

04 · PacingProtect the race that still remains.

Pace the
whole race.

Choose your effort for each station and how much race you have left. One effort level will not suit everything.

Stations 1–3

Stay controlled.

Controlled enough that you can return to pace. You still have most of the race ahead of you.

Stations 4–6

Efficient.

Keep your movements steady and your breathing under control. Aim to leave each station ready to run.

Station 7 + Run 8

Controlled finish.

Choose an effort that lets you complete the final run and wall balls well. More aggression is useful only when it reduces the combined time.

Station 8

Finish.

There is no run afterward. Use what you have left while keeping good reps. Take short breaks before you start missing reps or need a long rest.

Check the total time

Choose the effort that improves the total race. A small loss on the next task can be worthwhile if the saving is larger. Test the trade-off.

05 · StationsWhat to do. What to change.

A clear plan
for each station.

Keep your movements smooth. Use practice to find an effort you can repeat, then check how you run afterward.

01 · SkiErgStart controlled

Leave ready to run.

Keep your pulls smooth and your breathing steady. Resist chasing a faster screen time in the opening minutes.

Try in practice: If Run 2 feels rushed or heavy, ease off the SkiErg next time. Compare the station and run together.

02 · Sled pushPace your legs

Keep the sled moving smoothly.

Start patiently. Try short pushes with brief breaks before your steps become a grind. Push farther when you can keep the same rhythm.

Try in practice: If you have to walk after the sled, try shorter pushes. Compare the push and Run 3 on the same sled and surface.

03 · Sled pullFeet and rope

Find a rhythm you can repeat.

Keep the rope clear of your feet. Use your legs and body as well as your arms. Take a brief reset if your pulls become rushed or messy.

Try in practice: If you keep stopping, practise your footwork and rope handling first. Then try a faster rhythm and check Run 4.

04 · Burpee broad jumpsSteady reps

Make the last rep look like the first.

Choose a jump length you can repeat. Keep moving at a steady rhythm instead of making a few huge jumps and stopping.

Try in practice: If you rush, then stall, try a brief pause on the floor between reps. Keep it only if the station and next run improve.

05 · RowStrong pull, easy return

Push, pull, then return smoothly.

Push with your legs, then finish the pull. Let your arms move forward before you slide back in. Give yourself time to return instead of rushing each stroke.

Try in practice: Try slightly fewer strokes per minute. Keep the change if you hold a similar split with less effort and can run well afterward.

06 · Farmers carryGrip and steps

Stand steady. Keep your feet moving.

Hold your middle firm and look along your route. Take quick, controlled steps. If your grip starts to slip, set the weights down safely before you lose control.

Try in practice: If repeated stops cost time, compare one planned short break with waiting until your grip fails.

07 · Sandbag lungesSame step each time

Keep each lunge clean.

Use a steady step length and rhythm. Meet the required depth on every rep. Speed up only while you can keep those same movements.

Try in practice: If Run 8 falls apart, try steadier lunges. Compare the lunges, final run and wall balls together.

08 · Wall ballsPlan your breaks

Finish with good reps.

Choose a set size you can repeat. Take short planned breaks before you start missing the target or losing depth. Increase your effort near the finish if your reps stay clean.

Try in practice: Count good reps, missed reps and rest time. Try smaller sets if they reduce missed reps and long stops.

06 · BuildBuild what the race needs.

Build the
athlete.

Train your running, strength and station technique. Then practise putting them together.

01

Run with less effort.

Build consistent easy running and suitable faster sessions. Your planned race pace should leave you able to handle the stations.

02

Make the loads easier.

Build strength in the movements you need: squatting, lifting, pushing, pulling and carrying. Check whether the race loads become easier to move.

03

Practise running afterward.

Complete a controlled station, then run a measured distance. Add more work when you can repeat the pace with good movement.

How your training changes near race day

Specific work should test transfer, not replace development. With enough time before the race, build real strength while keeping a small HYROX dose. As the race approaches, increase station-specific expression and full-sequence work.

Build first. Then put it together.

Build

Running + strength

Develop the running and train station technique on their own. Use fresh station work to learn load, rhythm and breaks.

Connect

One controlled station–run session a week can be a starting point. Add more only if you recover well and your other training stays on track.

Rehearse

The final four weeks can emphasize race order, transitions and the finish. Adapt to readiness and race date; keep recovery before race day.

01 · One station. Two approaches.

Question: does a more controlled station improve the combined station + next run?

  1. Warm up. Choose one station, a repeatable load/distance and a measured 800 m run. Write down the recovery you will use between trials.
  2. Trial A: complete the station at your usual effort, then run 800 m at your working target. Record both times, breaks and effort.
  3. Trial B: repeat with a more controlled station effort. Keep load, distance and recovery the same.
  4. Compare the pair totals below. Repeat on another day with the order reversed before deciding that one approach is better.

Keep: the approach that repeatedly saves combined time with acceptable technique and effort. Change: one variable if the result is unclear.

Keep the effort controlled from run to station and back.

Before you start

Warm up. Choose the sled/carrying loads, wall-ball reps and breaks, and recovery between blocks. Use the reference pace only if you can hold it with control. The original threshold example was 3:55/km.

The cue

Steady pressure. No repeated surges.

Aim to keep effort controlled and regain running rhythm promptly. Review heart rate alongside breathing, pace, technique and perceived effort. A perfectly flat HR trace is not the target; station demands differ.

How to read the comparison

Fresh time already exceeds the race budget: revisit the target or develop the station. Fresh time is below it: there is time allowance, but no proof the target will survive the race.

Example: a 2:40 fresh station against a 3:00 budget leaves 0:20 allowance. It does not establish a universal 20-second fatigue cost. Repeat under controlled station–run conditions, then confirm in a longer sequence.

Keep sled and turf conditions especially consistent. A faster isolated time is useful only if its technique and effort transfer to the race.

07 · ObserveCoach notes · watching a session

Watch what
changes.

Watch how the athlete moves early in the session. Record what changes later and ask what they felt.

Runs 1–8 — split, measured distance/timing boundary, first 200–300 m after each station.
Stations 1–8 — split, planned vs involuntary breaks, no-reps, technique and load.
Roxzone — walking, setup, hesitation, congestion and avoidable dead time.
Early movement — cadence, posture, arm carriage, stride length, breathing, head position.
First visible change — what changed first, not just when pace slowed.
Where it changed — record where a meaningful change appeared, if one did. Do not force a turning point into every race.

Measurement and race rules

Use current benchmarks and measured distances. A split points to a question; it does not prove a cause. Count penalties once.

Check the official rulebook for loads and movement standards, and Elite qualification for current entry criteria. FORM is an independent coaching framework.

HYROX pacing questions
What pace should I use for my first HYROX?
Start with current running evidence, then test a pace you can repeatedly regain after stations. Use the budget calculator to check the full-race cost.
How much slower is HYROX than fresh running?
There is no fixed offset. Compare current benchmarks with measured race or simulation running under known conditions.
What is the Roxzone?
The transition area between the running course and stations. Include its time in your race budget.
Does this shortened simulation predict my finish?
No direct conversion is established. Use it to test control and transitions; estimate all full-distance race components separately.
