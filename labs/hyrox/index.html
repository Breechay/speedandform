02 · RacingFour things to remember.

Four rules
to race by.

Save more time than you lose elsewhere. Test that across the whole race.

01 · Clear

Leave ready to run.

Early stations: keep your movements smooth and leave enough to run again.

Check it: Try a slightly calmer station with the same load and run. Compare both times, then check later work too.

02 · Return

Find your rhythm again.

After each station, settle your breathing and take controlled steps. Avoid forcing a sprint out of the exit.

Check it: Notice your first 200 m: how long until your stride and breathing settle? A change tells you what to investigate, not what caused it.

03 · Own

Start at a pace you can repeat.

Your first run should leave room for the other seven and the stations.

Check it: Compare early and late runs over known distances. If you fade, test a calmer opening before assuming you need more speed.

04 · Spend

Finish with good reps.

Late in the race, increase effort while movement stays clean. Run 8 still has wall balls afterward.

Check it: Compare the final run, wall-ball time, missed reps and breaks. Keep the approach that improves the whole finish.

Change one thing. Check again.

Keep distance, load and recovery comparable. Repeat on another day with the order reversed: fatigue, warm-up and conditions can favor either trial. If results disagree, keep the question open.

02 · Run gap

Why is your race running slower?

Compare your race pace with your current threshold pace. The gap shows how much slower you ran; it cannot tell you why.

Try this: If the gap keeps growing in similar sessions, try a calmer opening or a more controlled station. Change one thing at a time.

See the calculation

Race pace − current threshold pace

4:20 − 4:00 = +0:20/km

Use known running distances and a recent threshold test. An old personal best is not your current benchmark.

Speed retained · another view

Threshold pace ÷ race pace × 100

240 ÷ 260 × 100 = 92.3%

Use seconds per kilometre for both paces. A higher percentage is not progress if it only rose because your threshold got slower.

03 · Late change

Can you hold your pace at the end?

Compare your first three runs with your last three. A slower finish is a reason to review how you spent your effort earlier.

Try this: Try a calmer opening and controlled early stations. Keep the change if your later runs and total time improve.

See the calculation

Late pace − early pace

4:25 − 4:10 = +0:15/km

Divide total run time by total distance for Runs 1–3 and Runs 6–8. Check that the splits cover comparable parts of the course.

01 · One station, two ways

Question: does a calmer station help you finish the station and run sooner?

  1. Warm up: easy walking or running, then a few light practice reps. You should be able to say a full sentence while running easily.
  2. Set the test: choose a station dose you already handle and a measured 800 m run. Write down the load, distance and recovery. Choose enough recovery to repeat the work with control.
  3. A: use your usual station effort, then run at a controlled effort you aim to repeat. Record both times and any breaks.
  4. B: after your planned recovery, do the same station more calmly, then aim for the same running effort. Record both times. If you are not ready after the rest, stop the comparison rather than forcing it.
  5. Compare: add station and run for each trial. A close result is inconclusive; it is not permission to push harder.
  6. Repeat another day: reverse the order. Keep the approach that repeatedly saves time with good movement. Check later fatigue too before choosing a race strategy.

If you have a current target pace, use it for both runs. Otherwise record effort and time; you can learn without inventing a threshold number.

How to read the comparison

Fresh time already exceeds the race budget: revisit the target or develop the station. Fresh time is below it: there is time allowance, but no proof the target will survive the race.

Example: a 2:40 fresh station against a 3:00 budget leaves 0:20 allowance. It does not establish a universal 20-second fatigue cost. Repeat under controlled station–run conditions, then confirm in a longer sequence.

Keep sled and turf conditions especially consistent. A faster isolated time is useful only if its technique and effort transfer to the race.

Race day

Arrive with a plan.

Read this during race week. Keep the plan familiar, short and easy to follow.

This week

Read your event instructions. Confirm start time, division, laps, station loads and the route through Roxzone. Write your opening pace and station break plan. Shorten training so you arrive rested.

Pack familiar kit

Bring your ID, entry confirmation, tested shoes and clothing, and planned nutrition. Check permitted equipment. At check-in, secure your timing chip and leave time for bag drop.

Eat and drink as practiced

Use a familiar pre-race meal and timing. Rehearse any gels or drinks in training, including how you carry them. Sip according to your practiced needs and thirst; do not force large amounts at once.

Personal nutrition must be carried from the start; accept drinks only from official aid stations. Race rules ↗

Warm up without wearing yourself out

Example: 8–12 minutes easy movement, a few light station reps, then 2–3 short runs toward your opening pace with easy recovery. Adjust for heat and how you feel. Finish ready, with time to reach your assigned start.

Have a fallback

Sled feels heavier than expected? Use shorter pushes and planned pauses. Grip fading? Take a brief carry break before losing control. Missing wall balls? Correct depth or target contact and shorten the next set. Keep counting valid reps.

Race the finish you can complete

Use Run 8 to arrive ready for wall balls. Follow your tested sets, then increase effort if your reps stay good. Afterward, record what changed—not just the finish time.

Warm-up and pacing are coaching examples. Check the current official rules and your event’s instructions.

HYROX pacing questions
What pace should I use for my first HYROX?
Start with current running evidence, then test a pace you can repeatedly regain after stations. Use the budget calculator to check the full-race cost.
How much slower is HYROX than fresh running?
There is no fixed offset. Compare current benchmarks with measured race or simulation running under known conditions.
What is the Roxzone?
The transition area between the running course and stations. Include its time in your race budget.
Does this shortened simulation predict my finish?
No direct conversion is established. Use it to test control and transitions; estimate all full-distance race components separately.
