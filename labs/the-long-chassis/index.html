The new direction

He knows distance. Now build speed.

Jacob reports an experienced ultra-running background, a recent 30 km run that became miserable late, and current knee/hip problems that feel more limiting than his aerobic fitness. He also reports a Garmin-estimated VO₂ max around 50 and says his historical device estimate never exceeded roughly 55.

The next development target is a faster mile and 5K. We will borrow the structure of Brice’s Speed Reserve program, while setting Jacob’s own pace targets and training load from his results and current capacity.

These are athlete-reported observations, not diagnoses. PT/physio instructions and persistent pain outrank an experiment.

The progression

Short speed first. Longer speed next.

The fourteen-week draft moves from relaxed 200 m and 400 m repeats toward 600–1200 m 5K work, with threshold sessions in between. It ends with separate mile and 5K references. Easy running preserves endurance without an automatic long-run ladder.

The mile and 5K remain the focus whether or not he runs Palermo in November. That race changes the training calendar, not the goal. Exact workout dates and paces are still being set.

Next gate

Get his numbers. Then set the pace.

We need recent weekly running, the longest normal run, a few race results, current knee and hip response, PT guidance, and his available training days. Jacob trains remotely, so his calendar will contain independent sessions, not Miami iRUN or FORM Thursday events.
