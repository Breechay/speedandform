The opening read

He can still go long. The body is charging too much for it.

Jacob reports an experienced ultra-running background, a recent 30 km run that became miserable late, and current knee/hip problems that feel more limiting than his aerobic fitness. He also reports a Garmin-estimated VO₂ max around 50 and says his historical device estimate never exceeded roughly 55.

That does not prove the engine is complete. It changes the opening question: before adding more cardiovascular punishment, can we make the body strong and stable enough to use the fitness he already has?

These are athlete-reported observations, not diagnoses. PT/physio instructions and persistent pain outrank an experiment.
Two systems

FORM carries the engine. FORGE rebuilds the chassis.

FORM

Run + aerobic

Running and low-impact aerobic work are prescribed for purpose, not because more cardio is automatically better.

FORGE

Strength + stability

Unilateral leg work, posterior chain, hip capacity and trunk control become consistent rather than occasional.

Study 005

Choose the limiter

The emphasis moves according to the athlete response. The study does not force both systems upward at the same time.

Long horizon

Two years is permission not to rush.

The 100-mile goal is a direction, not a current mileage target. Palermo Marathon in November is currently described by the athlete as a finish rather than a competition target. The first block is expected to be a 4–6 week chassis phase, but it will not be frozen until the PT/physio work, symptom pattern, ultra history and equipment are actually known.

The first success condition is smaller than 100 miles: can he train consistently without the chassis becoming the reason the engine cannot be used?

Next gate

Next intake captures the knee/hip pattern, PT/physio exercises and restrictions, past ultra results, normal weekly volume, current strength access, recent run frequency, recovery, and what the Palermo month should actually look like.
