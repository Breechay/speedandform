THE PLAN IS
NOT THE POINT.

A training plan is a tool. The question is whether it is the right tool for the athlete standing in front of you.

FORM authors methods so they can be repeated, inspected and learned from. But a season is composed around a person. Sometimes you need the whole progression. Sometimes you enter halfway through. Sometimes the calendar forces two ideas to overlap. Evidence decides what happens next.

TWO QUESTIONS.
ONE SYSTEM.

One study expands capacity. The other asks how much of an owned pace survives as fatigue rises.

The emphasis changes. The runner does not. Faster athletes still need durability; durable athletes still need speed. Evidence decides what gets emphasized next.

The practice

RUNNING IS
A SKILL.

A golfer practices the swing.

The movement itself is worthy of attention, not merely the score at the end.

A swimmer practices the stroke.

Fitness matters. So does how the body organizes itself to express that fitness.

A runner should practice running.

Rhythm, posture, relaxation, awareness and control can be observed and refined while the athlete trains.

Performance does not mean there is nothing left to learn.

An already fast athlete can become more organized. An already strong athlete can waste less. An athlete with plenty of intensity can learn to channel it. FORM is interested in the numbers, but also in the runner producing them.
