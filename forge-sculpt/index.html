The problem

Effort doesn't make a shape. Allocation does.

You are not short on effort. You have four years of evidence that effort was never the problem. You're short on a plan for where it goes.

01

You assembled your training from fragments — a split here, a video there, whatever felt right that month.

02

The fragments have a bias. You train what you're already good at, and you're already good at what you train.

03

So the shape compounds in one direction. Chest and arms. A frame that stops at the waist.

04

You didn't choose this body. You accumulated it.

Every set you have ever done was a decision about what you would eventually look like. You just weren't the one making it deliberately.

The mechanism

Every set is spent on purpose.

A training week is a fixed budget. Most programs spend it by convention — chest day, back day — or by preference. Breechay Sculpt spends it on what the silhouette is actually missing, then moves that bias across three campaigns so the emphasis compounds instead of averaging out.

The part your program skipped

Men's training stops at the waist.

Not for physiological reasons. For conventional ones. Men train what they can see in a mirror, so men have spent thirty years building the front of a body and calling it a physique.

Meanwhile the rest of the industry spent a decade actually solving glute development — the mechanics, the volume, the loading. That work exists. It's good. It has simply never been pointed at a man's silhouette.

Breechay Sculpt points it there. Your glutes are the largest muscle you own and the reason a body has a base, a side profile, and a back worth looking at. Build the Glute Base exists because nothing else in men's training was ever going to give you one.

This is the work that will feel unfamiliar. That is the evidence it's worth running.

Before you ask

The honest answers.

Can I swap exercises?+
No. The allocation is the product. If a movement conflicts with an injury, stop and get qualified guidance. FORGE does not improvise substitutions that change the program’s allocation.
What gym do I need?+
A commercial gym with cables, machines, and a full rack. This is not a home-dumbbell program and we'd rather lose the download than take your money and waste your week.
How many days a week, honestly?+
Four to seven — authored per phase, not flattened to one number. Across forty-nine training weeks the year runs 267 sessions. Some weeks are denser; some are leaner. The commitment is your time. It is never your attention.
Is this just a PDF with a timer?+
The written curriculum is the product; the app is how you execute it without thinking. You get Today, Focus, logging, resume protection, and a Record. What you don't get is a blank page.
What happens after The Frame?+
The year continues. The Depth opens (phases 05–08). Then The Base (phases 09–12). Then a three-week Hold — then return to The Frame. Three campaigns · twelve phases · one 52-week year. Not four campaigns. Not a dead end.
I'm a beginner. Is this for me?+
If you can perform basic barbell, dumbbell and cable movements safely, yes. The loads are yours to select. The allocation, progression and sequencing are not — and that's the part that was never going to work out on its own.
Who owns my data?+
You do. Stored on this device. Shared only by you. Export and deletion remain under your control.
