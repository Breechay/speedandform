01 · Governing question

HOW LONG IS THE PACE AVAILABLE AFTER THE RUN GETS EXPENSIVE?

This is not a beginner marathon-completion plan. It is for a runner who already owns the distance and wants to preserve useful speed under prolonged fatigue.

Mileage is the environment, not the claim. The plan progresses where the specific work happens inside that mileage.

Specific contact

Remember the faster capability Act I established. Tuesday maintains access; it does not need to prove durability again.

Thursday

Protect the ceiling

Threshold, aerobic power and fixed FORM speed work stay in the program. Hard Saturday means quiet Thursday; easy Saturday leaves room to raise the ceiling.

Saturday

Test what remains

The reserve does not need to grow every week. The progression is increasingly the amount of running that happens before the same capability is requested.

Fueling becomes prescriptionFrom the first reserve long run onward, use the strategy you intend to race with.

Record product, amount, timing and tolerance. The plan does not impose one universal carbohydrate target; it requires a deliberate, rehearsed strategy so late-run evidence is interpretable.

Long-run law

A 22-MILE RUN IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY BETTER THAN AN 18-MILE RUN.

The principal rehearsal deliberately stops short of recreating the race-day 20-mile prelude. Training asks for the capability after enough prior running to make the signal meaningful without turning the long run into a full-marathon simulation. Race day is the full test.

Specific reserve

The reserve is the faster capability supported by current evidence. It is not automatically marathon race-day pace. The exact band is athlete-relative and can change when race or training evidence changes the coach read.

Marathon rhythm

Marathon rhythm is what can actually be distributed across 26.2. It appears deliberately after the reserve has been protected; it does not replace the ceiling or the faster capability above race demand.

03 · Coming from a race

BETWEEN BLOCKS
HAS A JOB.

If this plan follows a hard race or a long build, do not force Week 1 of Marathon Durability onto a body that has not absorbed the previous work. FORM uses a separate two-week transition method before the next performance question begins.
Transition 01

Recover

4–5 easy running days · roughly 24–30 miles or less · no pace demand · no required long-run achievement.

Transition 02

Return

5–6 running days · roughly 34–40 miles · easy running · relaxed 9–10 mile long run · one small strides or hill touch if comfortable.

04 · Before you use it

KNOW WHAT THIS PLAN ASSUMES.

This is a demanding plan. The right entry point matters more than the appeal of the goal race.

Good fit

  • You already have marathon experience or comparable long-run durability.
  • ~50 miles per week is close to normal, not an emergency jump.
  • You can handle six running days and meaningful long runs.
  • You have a specific pace supported by current evidence.
  • You want to preserve speed while extending marathon durability.

Choose another starting point if

  • You are training to finish your first marathon.
  • You are returning from injury or need rehab guidance.
  • 50–66 miles per week is far beyond your recent training.
  • Your target pace exists only because of a desired finish time.
  • You need individualized weekly changes rather than a self-guided plan.
