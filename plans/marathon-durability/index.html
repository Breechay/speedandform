01 · Who this is for

YOUR SHORTER-RACE FITNESS SAYS
THE MARATHON SHOULD BE BETTER.

This is for runners who already know how to train. The problem is not finishing 26.2. The problem is keeping more of your ability available late.

This is probably you

  • Your 5K, 10K or half-marathon fitness looks better than your marathon finish.
  • ~50 miles per week is already normal for you.
  • Six running days and 16–18 mile long runs are familiar.
  • You have paces supported by recent racing or workouts.
  • You want to approach the marathon as more than a mileage contest.

Start somewhere else if

  • This is your first marathon build.
  • You are returning from injury or rebuilding basic mileage.
  • 50–66 miles per week would be a major jump.
  • Your target pace only exists because of a finish-time goal.
  • You need individualized weekly changes from a coach.
02 · Race pacing

THE FIRST 20 MILES
WRITE THE LAST 10K.

The marathon rewards restraint before it rewards courage. FORM uses a simple race line: 9 · 20 · 54 · 17.

Those numbers are proportions of the race, not four new pace zones. They tell you what your job is as the race changes.

9% · ~2.4 mi

START

Do less than adrenaline wants. Get organized. Start fueling. Let everyone else make decisions.

20% · ~5.2 mi

SETTLE

Find marathon rhythm. Breathing, cadence and effort should feel almost boring.

54% · ~14.1 mi

HOLD

This is the marathon. Fuel. Protect rhythm. Do not bank time. Good running should still look unremarkable.

17% · ~4.5 mi

FINISH

Now read the body. If the rhythm is still there, race. If it is not, solve the problem you actually have.

Race-day law

FEELING GOOD AT MILE 10 IS NOT A REASON TO SPEED UP.

That is the feeling the first half of the race is supposed to produce. Protect it.

03 · Why you faded

SOMETHING HAPPENED. NOT WHAT.

04 · A week has three jobs

KEEP THE WEEK SIMPLE.
LET SATURDAY OWN THE QUESTION.

Marathon training gets complicated quickly. The weekly structure should not.

Tuesday · Ceiling / contact

Remember what faster feels like.

Threshold, reserve work and controlled faster running keep range above marathon demand.

Thursday · Support

Raise or protect the ceiling.

When Saturday is easy, Thursday can carry more work. When Saturday is the big ask, Thursday gets quiet.

Saturday · Access

Ask for the ability later.

The long run creates the context. Access and re-access decide whether useful pace still exists inside it.

THE SHIFTThe pace is not the progression. The depth is.

06 · Set your paces

GOAL TIME DOES NOT CREATE CAPABILITY.
EVIDENCE DOES.

Do not choose every training pace by reverse-engineering a dream finish time. Start with what the athlete actually owns.

01 · Ceiling

What can you do faster than marathon demand?

Use current threshold / controlled faster evidence. This keeps room above the race.

02 · Reserve

What faster pace do you demonstrably own now?

This is the capability you can touch, preserve and later ask to re-appear under fatigue.

03 · Marathon rhythm

What can actually be distributed across 26.2?

Long-run evidence, fueling, current endurance and race response matter more than the number you want on the clock.

04 · Update

Let new evidence change the assignment.

A workout or race can keep the current band, narrow it, reduce it or create a different question.

ACCESSCan you reach the capability after prior running?
RE-ACCESSCan you leave it, recover, then find it again?
ABSORBCan you recover from the work without needing another bigger test?

08 · Fueling + adjustment rules

MAKE THE TEST
INTERPRETABLE.

Marathon training only teaches you something when the conditions are clear enough to read.

Fueling

Practice what you intend to race with.

From the first marathon-specific long run, record product, amount, timing and tolerance. A late fade under improvised fueling does not cleanly answer the durability question.

Recovery

Do not force the next Ask.

If recovery is materially abnormal, reduce, replace or delay the next major session until normal easy-running function returns.

Conditions

Heat, wind and terrain change the cost.

Record them. Do not pretend the same pace means the same thing in every environment.

Decision

The response decides the next move.

Continue, repeat, reduce or change the question. The plan is a method, not a command to ignore evidence.

09 · Coming from a race

Use a two-week on-ramp.

Week 1 · Recover: 4–5 easy days · ~24–30 miles or less · no pace demand. Week 2 · Return: 5–6 days · ~34–40 miles · relaxed 9–10 mile long run · one small stride/hill touch if comfortable.

10 · Coach FAQ

THREE QUESTIONS
THAT CHANGE THE RACE.

Keep the answers simple enough to remember when you are tired.
Won't all the easy running make me slow?

No. Easy running builds the endurance underneath the week; the faster work remains. The goal is not to replace speed with mileage. It is to make useful pace easier to carry.

Why isn't the mileage even higher?

More mileage can help if you can absorb it. The plan uses enough volume to build the marathon without making the biggest weekly number the goal.

What if I feel amazing early on race day?

Good. Keep it that way. Feeling easy at mile 10 is evidence that the opening is doing its job — not permission to spend what you will need at mile 22.
