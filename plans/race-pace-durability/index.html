About this plan

Your pace is individual. The durability demand is not.

You do not need to prove your target pace for one mile. You need it available for 13.1.

Race Pace Durability is athlete-relative. Set a race-pace band that fits your current fitness, then keep that band stable while uninterrupted distance grows and the same demand moves later into the long run.

Before you start

Not a beginner plan.

You should already be comfortable training six days per week, running around 45 miles per week, and completing a 12-mile long run. This block develops race-pace durability. It does not build the base from zero.

Your race-pace band

The method is shared. The pace is athlete-relative.

A 1:15 runner, a 1:30 runner and a different athlete can use the same progression at different bands. The band must be supported by current fitness, not chosen only from the finish time you want.

In the current public study, José is working at 6:30–6:45/mi while Hope is working at 6:45–7:00/mi. Those are athlete assignments, not universal plan constants.

The pace does not automatically get faster every week. The distance you can hold continuously grows across the block.

5 → 6 → 8 → 12 continuous miles → race.
