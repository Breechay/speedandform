The goal

Run the pace.
Hold it longer.

Running your goal pace for a mile or two is not the same as holding it for a half marathon. This plan builds how long you can stay at a pace your current fitness supports.

Early workouts use shorter efforts with recovery. Later workouts ask you to hold race pace for longer, including late in long runs.

What changes over 15 weeks

The race-pace work
gets longer.

Shorter efforts5 miles6 miles8 miles12 miles lateRace

The final 12 race-pace miles come after four easier miles in a 16-mile long run.

Your pace, not ours.

The plan does not require one universal pace. A faster or slower runner can use the same 15-week progression when the chosen race pace matches current fitness.

This is a demanding plan.

This version starts around 45 miles per week and peaks around 60. If your normal training is far below or above that, this exact plan may not be the right fit.

Before you buy

Is this plan
right for you?

Good fit

  • You already run around 45 miles per week or close to it.
  • Running six days and a 12-mile long run are already normal for you.
  • Your recent races or workouts support the pace you want to train.
  • You are comfortable following a plan without weekly coaching.
  • You want to run a stronger, faster half marathon.

Not yet

  • You are still building toward your first half marathon.
  • You are returning from injury or need medical or rehab guidance.
  • Your target pace comes only from the finish time you want, not from current fitness.
  • You need your training adjusted for you every week.
  • Your normal mileage is far above or below this 45→60 mile-per-week plan.
