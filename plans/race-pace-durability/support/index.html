The problem

Touching pace is not
the same as owning it.

Short reps can prove that the speed exists while still hiding the actual half-marathon problem: how much of that pace remains available after sustained work.

This block does not keep chasing a faster number every week. It keeps the race-pace demand recognizable, then removes the rests and extends how long you have to stay there.

The progression

The resets disappear.
The distance grows.

Continuous miles at the athlete’s assigned race-pace band. The final 12 follow four easier miles in a 16-mile long run.

Same method. Different bands.

Race Pace Durability is not a 6:30–6:45/mi plan. That is one current study assignment. A faster or slower runner can use the same durability architecture at a different band when current fitness supports it.

This published block still has a workload.

The pace is athlete-relative; the weekly structure is not infinitely elastic. This version begins around 45 miles per week and peaks around 60. If your normal workload is materially different, the method may fit while this exact prescription may not.

Before you buy

Know whether this block
fits where you are now.

Good fit

  • You already run around 45 miles per week or close to it.
  • Six running days and a 12-mile long run are already normal.
  • You have recent race, threshold or training evidence for the pace you want to develop.
  • You are comfortable following a self-guided plan without weekly coaching.
  • Your goal is to make an ambitious half-marathon pace last, not merely touch it once.

Not yet

  • You are building toward your first half marathon or still building basic run volume.
  • You are returning from injury or need medical / rehab guidance.
  • Your target pace comes only from the finish time you want, without current fitness evidence.
  • You need your mileage, workouts or pace band adjusted for you every week.
  • Your normal training volume is far above or below this published 45→60 mpw block.
