What the plan is

The structure behind the sessions.

If you have seen parts of this block on Instagram or followed Hope and José, this is the complete 15-week progression behind those workouts.

The plan does not try to make every week faster. It changes the shape and amount of the work so a pace supported by your current fitness becomes sustainable for longer.

Early sessions use recovery between efforts. Later sessions remove those resets. The long runs add another question: can you still access the pace when you are no longer fresh?

How the 15 weeks build

The bigger sessions are separated by build or absorb weeks. The aim is not to prove yourself every Tuesday. It is to arrive at the next ask ready to answer a slightly different question.

Tuesday · Practice race pace

Repeated efforts build time at your race-pace band. At key points those become continuous runs of 5, 6 and 8 miles.

Thursday · Support the work

Threshold running, hills, controlled faster work or strides keep support above race pace without turning every day into another race.

Saturday · Extend the long run

Early long runs stay easy. Later ones add race pace after prior mileage, including 12 race-pace miles after 4 easier miles inside a 16-mile run.

Your pace

Use a pace your current running supports.

The method is shared; the pace is athlete-relative. Recent races and workouts should support the band you use. A faster or slower runner can follow the same progression at different paces.

Success does not automatically make the target faster the next week. The progression first asks you to hold the pace for longer.

The commitment

This is a demanding half-marathon plan.

It begins around 45 miles per week, peaks around 60, uses six running days, and reaches an 18-mile long run. If that is far outside your normal training, this exact version may not be the right starting point.

Before you buy

Know whether the shape fits you.

Good fit

  • You already run around 45 miles per week, or close to it.
  • Six running days and a 12-mile long run are already normal for you.
  • Recent races or workouts support the race pace you want to train.
  • You are comfortable following a plan without weekly adjustments.
  • You want a stronger half-marathon build rather than a first-finish plan.

Choose a different starting point if

  • You are still building toward your first half marathon.
  • You are returning from injury or need medical or rehab guidance.
  • Your target pace comes only from the finish time you want.
  • You need your training changed around you each week.
  • Your normal mileage is far above or below this plan's 45 to 60 mile range.
