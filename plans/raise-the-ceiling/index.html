SIX WEEKS. ONE 10K.

THE SIX WEEKS

ABOUT THIS PLAN

Can a good runner become a better runner without simply running harder?

Make a fast pace ordinary before asking for a faster one. Tuesday raises what they can sustain. Thursday keeps faster running available above it. Saturday keeps the endurance underneath.

Simon and Lisa run the same method and they do not run the same Tuesday. Lisa raced five days before week two, so she repeats instead of stepping up, and takes her first twenty-five continuous minutes a week later than he does. Both arrive at the test with two twenty-five minute efforts filed at their own band. Different routes. Same destination.

The numbers belong to Simon and Lisa. The question may belong to you.

TUESDAY

Warm up 15–20 min easy, then 4 × 20s relaxed strides. Cool down 10 min easy. Recovery between reps is 3 min float in weeks two and three, and 2 min from week four.

Prefer a session that finishes as strong as it starts. A hot first rep is a warning that the workout is being spent too early. The band is not a target to beat.

THURSDAY AND SATURDAY

Thursday is a fixed FORM track standard. It does not scale to the athlete — how close each one gets to it is the reading. Named sessions use a 15 min warm-up and a 10 min cool-down. If a week needs less, the coach writes a shorter session on purpose; the standard is never quietly made easier.

Saturday stays at each athlete’s own normal distance and conversational, except in week four, which finishes with 15–20 minutes at the Tuesday band and not faster.

THE 10K · SATURDAY OCTOBER 10

15–20 min very easy, 4 × 20s strides, the 10K, then 10–20 min very easy. Flat, measured, the same course for both if the schedule allows.

Open the first kilometre at your own Tuesday band. Do not open faster. Progress from there if the body allows. The last kilometre should be the fastest.

Record temperature, humidity and dew point. Miami in October will affect the result, and the result is worthless without the conditions attached.
