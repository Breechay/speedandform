01 / Your day

Know what comes next.

Start here. Keep breakfast after your morning training; these times organize your workday, not your workout schedule. Portions are a starting point, not a food ceiling.

BeforeYour run

One medium banana.

Eat it 20–30 minutes before your early run. On a non-running morning, eat it with breakfast. Keep the milk, whey and peanut butter for afterward.

Before a long or hard run, add 1 slice of bread with 1 tablespoon of jam. Test this in training, not for the first time at a race.

7:00 AMBreakfast

Bread, peanut butter, your shake.

2 slices of your mom's bread + 2 tablespoons of peanut butter. Aim for about 80 g of bread total. Check once so you know the slice size.

1 level scoop (32 g) of NOW Sports Whey Protein Isolate, Creamy Vanilla + 8 fl oz (240 mL) whole milk. Shake together. No blender needed. Eat the bread too.

For a run followed directly by lifting, have the shake after the run and the bread after lifting. Bring breakfast when you train away from home. Do not wait until lunch.

12:30 PMLunch

Keep your work lunch.

When you can choose, order chicken, rice and beans. Start with 4 oz cooked chicken, 1½ cups cooked rice and ½ cup cooked beans. Eat 1 medium apple with lunch.

When work orders a Cuban sandwich or gyro, eat that meal and your apple. Send photos of three normal lunches so we can check portions without making you calculate every meal.

3:30 PMAfternoon

Eat one pasta box.

Put it in the work fridge when you arrive. Heat it thoroughly at 3:30 and eat it. Keep this meal on weekends too.

On an afternoon-run day, move the pasta to afterward instead of eating a large meal just before running. Keep dinner. Tell me if the box is consistently too filling to finish both.

6:30 PMDinner

Eat the family dinner.

Start with 4 oz cooked chicken or beef, 1½ cups cooked rice, ½ cup cooked beans and 1 cup cooked carrots. Keep your mom's chicken fricassee and familiar mains. Use these portions as your guide.

Still hungry? Add 1 cup of cooked rice to lunch or dinner and tell me at check-in. Keep water with meals and take a refillable bottle to work; drink to thirst rather than forcing a fixed volume.

Bananas, apples and carrots are the choices for this version. Tell me when a chosen food does not work. We will replace it, not leave a gap.

02 / Your prep

Four pasta boxes.

1 lb
Dry penne, weighed before cooking
1 lb
93% lean ground beef, raw weight
24 oz
One jar of tomato pasta sauce
  1. Cook the pasta using the box instructions.
  2. Cook the beef to 160°F, checked with a food thermometer. Stir in the sauce.
  3. Mix with the drained pasta. Divide the whole batch into four equal portions. Each box gets one quarter of every ingredient.
  4. Label the cooking date. Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers. Freeze portions you will not use within 3–4 days.

Sunday: refrigerate three boxes for Monday–Wednesday. Freeze the fourth as a backup.
Wednesday: refrigerate three for Thursday–Saturday. Freeze Sunday's. Move it to the fridge Saturday night.

At work: carry the box cold with ice packs, then refrigerate. Heat in a microwave-safe container with a vented cover; stir, allow standing time, and check several spots reach 165°F. Keep the fridge at 40°F or below. Do not leave food out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. [4]

Pack tomorrow before bed.

Put the dry scoop in your clean shaker. Keep the milk refrigerated. Prepare your bread and peanut butter. Set aside the banana, apple and labeled pasta box.

Forgot the pasta? Eat 2 slices of bread with 2 tablespoons of peanut butter and 1 tablespoon of jam, then keep dinner. This is an emergency backup, not an equal nutrient swap.

Your first seven days.

Eat breakfast. Have your afternoon meal. Pack before bed. At check-in, send your appetite, digestion, sleep hours, soreness and whether your runs feel normal, flat or heavy.

Take your weight Monday, Wednesday and Friday mornings, after the bathroom and before food or training. Same scale, similar clothing. Use the weekly average, not one reading. Tell me if weighing feels stressful. No weight-gain deadline is set.

Food does not excuse a training dose you cannot recover from. Report persistent or worsening soreness. Severe pain, marked weakness or dark urine needs prompt medical assessment. [6]

NOW Sports Whey Protein Isolate · Creamy Vanilla · 1.8 lb tub

The label lists 32 g powder per level scoop, providing 25 g protein. Powder weight is not protein amount. Vanilla is the starting selection, not a recorded flavor preference.

Open the exact product · Check your batch with Informed Sport

The independent listing includes Creamy Vanilla batches. Verify the actual tub's batch before use; testing reduces risk, not eliminates it. This is not an NSF or USADA approval claim.

Contains milk, xylitol and stevia. Keep away from pets because of xylitol. Tell me about any allergy, dietary restriction or stomach symptoms before pushing through something that disagrees with you. No mass gainer, tongkat ali or second shake is part of this starting routine. [1,5]

Your long run needs fuel too

Your supplied week shows a planned 12-mile easy run, about 90–102 minutes at the listed pace. Keep your running coach's existing fueling instructions first.

Without an existing plan, a starting practice for a 90–105-minute run is one non-caffeinated gel containing 25 g carbohydrate at minutes 20, 50 and 80. That is 75 g during the run, about 43–50 g per hour. Check the package because gels differ; take water according to its instructions and carry enough water or arrange a refill.

Practice tolerance before a race. Do not extrapolate this to longer runs. Gels do not replace breakfast. Send the gel you already use so we can name it without replacing your running coach's routine. [2]
