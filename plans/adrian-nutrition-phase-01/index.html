01 / Your workday

One routine to repeat.

Built around your 8 a.m.–5 p.m. workday and morning training. These are starting portions, not a limit on how much you may eat. Follow your existing run and Forge assignments; this page adds no training.

Beforeyour run

One banana.

Eat 1 medium banana about 20–30 minutes before your morning run. Keep the milk, peanut butter and larger breakfast for afterward. On a morning without running, eat the banana with breakfast.

Aftertraining

Your shake at home. Your breakfast ready.

After your last planned morning session, mix 1 labeled serving of Thorne Whey Protein Isolate, Chocolate with 12 fl oz of whole milk (1½ cups / 355 mL). Drink it at home.

Eat 2 slices of bread, about 80 g combined, with 2 level tablespoons of peanut butter (32 g), and 1 medium navel orange. When the morning runs tight, pack the bread and orange and finish breakfast when you arrive at work. Do not wait until lunch.

On mornings with both a run and a lift, do only the sessions already assigned. If the gap between them leaves you hungry, eat a slice of your breakfast bread during that gap rather than delaying all food.

Before your first new powder serving, complete the label, lot and ingredient check. Keep breakfast even if the powder is not ready.

12:30 p.m.work lunch

Eat the lunch work provides.

Keep the chicken with rice and beans, Cuban sandwich or other supplied lunch. Eat a full meal, not only the meat.

3:30 p.m.prepared meal

Your beef-and-pasta box.

Eat one portion from the recipe you prepared. Take the chilled box to work in your frozen lunch bag. Put the box itself in the work fridge when you arrive. Reheat it before eating.

After your first three boxes, tell Brice whether dinner still feels normal. If the box repeatedly crowds out dinner, report it before changing the recipe. Do not quietly drop dinner or automatically cut the pasta and beef.

6:30 p.m.family dinner

Your mom’s dinner, with a full plate.

Keep the chicken fricassee, beef, elk or other family main. Start with 4–6 oz cooked meat, 1½ cups cooked rice and ½ cup beans. Add 1 cup cooked green beans. For a pasta dinner, the pasta replaces the rice and beans.

Keep your established long-run fuel.

You already have a gel routine. Keep the products, amount and timing you use with your running coach, including your usual pre-run food. Do not replace that routine with the banana alone or add a new gel schedule from this page.

Arrange water access and drink to thirst; do not force large amounts. Eat breakfast afterward. Include your actual gels and drinks in your Saturday food photos. Sources 2, 8

This week: have breakfast, protect your afternoon meal, and start moving bedtime earlier. Learn one recipe before trying to perfect everything.

02 / Prepare ahead

Cook once. Know where every box goes.

First Sunday: make 4 portions. Refrigerate 3 for Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday. Freeze 1 backup promptly.

First Wednesday evening: make 2 portions. Use the half-batch column in the recipe table. Refrigerate them for Thursday and Friday.

That is five workday meals and one backup—not eight meals you have to find room for. Weekend meals with your parents stay in place.

Next week, and starting midweek

Before your first prep: start the breakfast routine and keep lunch and dinner. Use the sandwich backup at 3:30. Begin cooking at the next Sunday or Wednesday slot; you do not need to wait for a Monday start.

Next Sunday, no backup left: repeat the 4-portion Sunday + 2-portion Wednesday schedule.

Next Sunday, one backup still frozen: move it to the fridge on Saturday night for Monday. Cook 4 new portions Sunday: refrigerate 2 for Tuesday/Wednesday and freeze 2 promptly for Thursday/Friday. Move each frozen box to the fridge the night before. Skip Wednesday cooking that week.

An unexpected extra box: label and freeze it promptly while still within the safe storage window. Send Brice your box count before buying the next batch so the next cook is reduced, not your food forced.

Use the oldest safely stored box first. Freezing does not rescue a box that sat warm too long. A thawed portion is for the next day; do not reset its label date.

Freeze the beef you will not cook yet.

On shopping day, refrigerate the package for Sunday only when you will cook it within 1–2 days. Otherwise freeze it too. Split the second 1 lb package into two 8 oz / 227 g portions and freeze both in labeled, leakproof bags.

Move one 8 oz portion to the fridge on Tuesday morning for Wednesday’s half batch. Leave the other frozen for later. Thaw on a tray in the fridge, never on the counter. Sources 4–5

Cool it promptly. Reheat it fully.

Divide food into shallow containers and refrigerate promptly—within 2 hours, or within 1 hour above 90°F. Do not wait for a large pot to cool on the counter. Keep the fridge at 40°F or below and freezer at 0°F or below. Refrigerated cooked portions last 3–4 days; freeze later meals promptly. Raw ground beef has the shorter 1–2-day fridge limit.

To reheat: add 1 tablespoon of water, cover with the lid vented according to its instructions, heat, stir, then continue until the center and several other spots reach 165°F. Check with a clean food thermometer; time and steam alone are not the test. Do not put a metal thermometer in a running microwave. Open the hot lid away from your face. Sources 4–5

04 / Learn the kitchen

Three meals worth repeating.

Start with 01. At your next cooking slot, try 02; try 03 after that. Then repeat your favorite. Make one recipe per batch, not all three at once.

These are simplified stovetop variations for your routine, inspired by the linked recipes. You do not need an Instant Pot. Photos show the publishers’ dishes for inspiration—not your exact portion, an identical adaptation, or evidence that you cooked them. Your first-batch feedback will refine these versions.

01 / Start here · Stovetop

Spaghetti with meat sauce.

Learn to soften onion, brown beef and build a proper tomato sauce.

Cook Recipe 01

Use: the shared base below, spaghetti, and ¼ cup / 20 g finely grated Parmesan per full batch (2 tablespoons / 10 g for a half batch).

  1. Make the sauce using the four shared steps below.
  2. Boil the spaghetti separately according to its package. Before draining, save a mug of the cooking water.
  3. Return pasta to its large pot. Add the meat sauce and Parmesan. Toss over low heat for 1 minute. If it will not coat the strands, stir in pasta water 1 tablespoon at a time.
  4. Divide everything equally into 4 portions, or 2 for a half batch. Label and chill promptly.

Your cooking cue: sauce should cling to the spaghetti, not leave a watery puddle underneath it.

02 / Your second variation · Stovetop

Creamy tomato penne.

The same beef base, a softer sauce. No pressure cooker needed.

Cook Recipe 02

Use: the shared base below, penne, and ½ cup / 120 mL heavy cream per full batch (¼ cup / 60 mL for a half batch). No Parmesan or cheddar is required.

  1. Make the shared sauce. Boil the penne separately according to its package and save a mug of pasta water before draining.
  2. Take the sauce off the heat. Stir in the measured cream. Return to low heat only until hot; do not boil vigorously.
  3. Combine sauce and pasta in the large pasta pot. Add pasta water 1 tablespoon at a time only if the sauce needs loosening.
  4. Divide into 4 equal portions, or 2 for a half batch. Label and chill promptly.

Your cooking cue: the sauce becomes a smooth pink-orange. This is a stovetop adaptation, not the source’s pressure-cooker instructions.

03 / Your third variation · Stovetop

Cheesy beef pasta.

Learn to finish a sauce with cheese instead of turning up the heat.

Cook Recipe 03

Use: the shared base below, penne, and 1 cup / 112 g shredded cheddar per full batch (½ cup / 56 g for a half batch). No cream or Parmesan is required.

  1. Make the shared sauce. Boil the penne separately and save a mug of pasta water before draining.
  2. Combine the pasta and sauce in the large pasta pot, then turn off the heat.
  3. Add cheddar in 3 handfuls, stirring until each melts. Add pasta water 1 tablespoon at a time if needed. If cheese will not melt, use low heat briefly while stirring; do not boil.
  4. Divide into 4 equal portions, or 2 for a half batch. Label and chill promptly.

Your cooking cue: glossy and coated, not oily or grainy. Try shells or rotini next time using the same dry weight and their package cooking time.

Four steps that make the sauce

Set up first: use a large pasta pot, a wide deep pan, colander, wooden spoon, measuring cup, knife and chopping board. Ask a parent to show you safe knife handling and the stove controls on your first cook. Chop onion and garlic before opening the beef.

  1. Soften. Warm the measured oil over medium heat. Add onion. Stir for about 5 minutes until softer and translucent, not blackened.
  2. Brown. Add beef and spread it across the pan. Leave it 2 minutes, then break into small crumbles and keep cooking. Check several thicker clumps reach 160°F; do not touch the pan with the probe. Color alone is not a safety test.
  3. Build flavor. Stir in garlic, seasoning and tomato paste. Cook 1 minute, stirring. The paste should darken slightly without burning.
  4. Simmer. Add tomatoes, measured water, salt and pepper. Bring to gentle bubbling, then lower the heat. Simmer 20 minutes with the lid partly open, stirring every few minutes. Scrape up brown bits, not burnt black patches.

Then follow your chosen recipe card. For its separate pasta step, use 4 quarts boiling water + 2 teaspoons fine table salt for a full batch, or 2 quarts + 1 teaspoon for a half. Follow the pasta package time and save a mug of cooking water before draining.

Taste and adjust: use a clean spoon. If the sauce is too thick, add water a tablespoon at a time. If watery, simmer longer before adding cream or cheese. Add extra salt only a small pinch at a time after tasting.

Wash hands and anything that touches raw beef; do not rinse the meat. Use a clean thermometer between checks. Divide the finished mixture evenly: the full batch makes four of your portions, not a family dinner plus four boxes. Set your portions aside before sharing, or arrange extra ingredients for the family. Food-safety sources 4–5

Your first cooking challenge

Make Recipe 01, photograph the finished food and your labeled boxes, and write one sentence: “Next time I would change…” It might be softer onion, finer beef crumbles, less salt or a looser sauce. Change one thing next time. That is how the recipe becomes yours.

Do not judge portions by the photos. Divide the whole batch evenly; the cooked weight changes with water absorption and simmering. If a portion does not fit with room to stir, split that same meal between two containers rather than throwing food away or cramming a lid shut.

05 / Saturday + Sunday

Keep your weekends. Keep eating.

You are off work on weekends. Breakfast and lunch with your parents belong in this plan. A restaurant meal replaces that home meal—it does not require a second breakfast or lunch.

Saturday · Your long-run day

Before and during: follow your established long-run food, gels and hydration routine. There is no new fuel product to buy here.

After: have breakfast. At home, use the bread, peanut butter, orange and shake above. Out with your parents, order 3 eggs, 2 pancakes and a glass of whole milk. This is your starting order, not an eating limit.

If breakfast out is more than an hour away, have your shake at home first. Count it as part of breakfast; do not add another shake later just because the schedule says breakfast.

Lunch: keep your usual burger or Cuban meal. If staying home with no lunch planned, use your thawed pasta backup. If there is no box, use the sandwich below rather than skipping lunch.

3:30: follow the afternoon rule below. Do not add a second pasta box simply to empty the freezer.

Sunday · Reset for the week

Morning: follow the running and Forge sessions already assigned. A day off running does not automatically mean a day off strength. Eat your banana with breakfast when you do not run.

Breakfast and lunch: use the same home-or-restaurant instructions as Saturday. Keep your family plans.

Before shopping: check your ingredients and whether the frozen backup remains. Follow the carryover rule.

3:30: eat an afternoon meal unless a late lunch has already filled that slot. Use a safe backup or the sandwich; the new Sunday batch is for the coming workweek.

After family dinner: prepare the Sunday batch, label the boxes, and pack Monday’s breakfast. Keep cooking early enough to protect bedtime.

Your weekend afternoon rule.

Lunch finished by 1:30: at about 3:30, eat a prepared box if available, otherwise the sandwich backup. Lunch finished after 1:30 and you are still comfortably full at 3:30: do not force another full meal. Keep dinner. If hunger returns before dinner, have the sandwich then.

This timing is a coaching starting rule, not a biological cutoff. If late lunch repeatedly leaves you without appetite for dinner, show Brice that day’s food rather than silently dropping meals.

Dinner at home: your air-fryer chicken nuggets can stay. Follow the package cooking instructions and pair your usual portion with 1½ cups cooked rice and 1 cup cooked green beans. Send one photo of the nugget label and your plated meal before we set a fixed nugget count; brands and sizes differ. Until then, use the family cooked-meat dinner above when you need an already-defined portion.

Send one photo of your usual restaurant breakfast or lunch as part of the Saturday photo-day. No need to order unfamiliar food or avoid a family meal to make the record look better.

When a meal or prep session falls through

Frozen box available: move it to the fridge the night before. If still frozen at mealtime, use your microwave’s defrost function in a suitable container, then immediately reheat, stir and check 165°F throughout. Do not thaw it on the counter.

No safe prepared box: eat 2 bread slices + 2 tablespoons peanut butter + 1 tablespoon jam + 8 fl oz whole milk. The sandwich replaces the missing afternoon meal or emergency lunch—not your breakfast shake. Do not add another scoop. This is a practical backup, not an exact nutritional equivalent to pasta.

No milk with you: eat the sandwich now and buy an 8 fl oz serving of whole milk from a fridge at mealtime. Do not skip the sandwich because the milk is unavailable.

Missed Wednesday prep: use the frozen backup for Thursday and the sandwich Friday. Do not use Sunday’s refrigerated food on Friday. Resume the next prep slot; missed prep creates no punishment meal.

An evening session: keep the day’s food but move the large pasta meal farther from your start time. Have Brice place it around the actual session before using that schedule. Do not force a full box immediately before running.

A rest day: keep regular meals. Use the weekend timing rule when a late family meal overlaps the afternoon meal; do not remove food automatically because you did not run.
