Field Notes · The shape of a week

Repeat the week. Notice what changes.

A familiar structure gives you something useful to compare, without making every week identical.

By Brice Ikouebe · Revised September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

A training week does not need to surprise you to be useful. A familiar route, a familiar session, and a familiar place for the long run can give you room to notice how you are running.

Instead of asking what new workout to try, you can ask a more specific question. Is the usual effort easier to settle into? Do you finish with more control? Does this arrangement still fit the rest of your life?

Repetition does not mean ignoring what has changed. A busy week may need fewer runs. A difficult session may need more space afterward. The structure is a starting point for those choices, not an instruction to force the same week through different circumstances.

Think of a week with one demanding session, easy running around it, and a longer run on the weekend. Repeat that shape when it fits. Change one part for a reason, then give yourself enough continuity to understand the difference.

You do not need a new system every time the work becomes familiar. You need a clear enough system to tell when something deserves to change.
