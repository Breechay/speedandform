Field Notes · The training decision

A good session does not need an encore.

Feeling good at the end is useful information. It is not automatically a reason to add more.

By Brice Ikouebe · Revised September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

You finish the last repetition and realize you could do another. The temptation is immediate: make the session a little bigger, turn a good day into a more impressive one.

Before adding anything, return to the job of the run. Was it to run a certain effort with control? To hold a pace for a little longer? To arrive at the next important session ready to work? Finishing with something left may be exactly what you were practicing.

For example, suppose you planned three controlled repetitions and finished all three without losing your rhythm. A fourth might be appropriate in a different session. It does not become necessary because you have the energy for it today.

The useful note is not “I should have done more.” It is “I completed this, and here is what it cost.” How did the effort change? Did the last repetition feel like the first? What did you have left?

That gives the next decision something to stand on. You can make the work harder deliberately rather than making every good day harder by instinct.

Take it into the week

Finish the work you chose. Let what you learned shape the next session.
