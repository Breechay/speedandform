Field Notes · Running together

The group should make the work clearer.

A shared session works best when people understand the purpose, not when everyone copies the fastest runner.

By Brice Ikouebe · Revised September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

A new runner should not have to guess what a training group expects. Explain where to meet, what the session is for, how recovery works, and which effort belongs to them.

The standard is not that everyone runs the same speed. It is that everyone understands the work. A runner choosing an appropriate pace is not doing a lesser version of the session.

There is room to enjoy being competitive. Give that energy a useful job: settle into the intended effort, keep the repetitions consistent, or finish with the control you were asked to practice. Another runner can be good company without becoming your pace prescription.

This also changes the welcome. People can ask a question, step into the right group, and understand why they are there. You do not need to lower the purpose of the session to make it clear.

Photographs, conversation, and the run afterward can all be part of the experience. The work and the people do not have to compete for attention.

Take it into the week

Run together. Keep the purpose clear. Let the pace belong to the runner.
