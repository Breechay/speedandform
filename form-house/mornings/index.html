01The experience5:50 AM–10 AM

Arrive before dawn. Choose an easy 10K or a longer 10–14 mile route. Return at different times to coffee, cold refreshments and breakfast, then stay somewhere comfortable until the morning naturally ends.

Working first scale: roughly 12–16 confirmed adults, adjusted to the host's seating, staffing and service capacity.

02What FORM bringsRunning + flow

Brice leads the running side: route choices, briefing, pace expectations, participant instructions and the arrival/return rhythm.

The goal is a morning that feels prepared without turning it into a race, a membership program or another generic run club. Photography or a short film can be scoped separately when useful.

03What the host confirmsAccess + service

Early access before 6 AM; bathrooms or changing access; somewhere secure for belongings; the food-service window; seating; guest limit; staffing; weather and cancellation terms.

Showers, towels, pool or recovery access are included only when specifically agreed. The first runners back should never be waiting on the last runners to finish before hospitality begins.

04How the partnership can workOne clear arrangement

A property can pay a defined hosting fee, guests can buy a ticket that covers the agreed costs, or the morning can be a clearly budgeted client or guest experience.

Those are alternatives, not simultaneous promises. Actual food, access, staffing and venue quotes come before setting a ticket price. Photography remains a separate scope.

05From partner brief to invitationWhen the facts are real

Once a host and date are confirmed, this concept can become the guest-facing invitation: exact arrival, route choices, what breakfast and access include, capacity, price if any, weather/cancellation terms and photo permissions.

Until then, the page stays a partner concept—not an announced event.
