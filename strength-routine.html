What strength exercises should a runner start with?

Start with a few movements you can control: bend the knees, hinge at the hips, raise the heels and keep the trunk steady. Use enough effort to learn, with room for your running.

Choose one from each job.

Choose familiar versions of the 4 movements. Begin with 1 set of each, resting until you can do the next movement well.

01

Squat or supported split squat

6–10 controlled repetitions

Set up: Use a chair squat, or stand in a split stance beside hand support.

Do: Bend the knees and hips through a comfortable range. Stand up under control. In a split squat, repeat on both sides.

Cue: Stand up under control.

Feel: Work in your thighs and hips, with your balance supported.

A version you can balance and repeat. Reduce range or use support before adding weight.

02

Hip hinge

6–10 controlled repetitions

Set up: Stand tall with soft knees. Start without weight.

Do: Push your hips back while keeping your trunk comfortable, then stand tall. A bridge is an alternative if the hinge is unfamiliar.

Cue: Push the hips back.

Feel: Work around your hips and the back of your thighs; no painful pull in your back.

Move at the hips. Choose a comfortable range without rounding or arching to reach farther.

03

Calf raise

8–12 controlled repetitions

Set up: Hold a stable support and stand on both feet.

Do: Raise your heels, then lower slowly. Move through a comfortable range.

Cue: Rise and lower smoothly.

Feel: Work in your calves without Achilles or joint pain.

Use both legs before trying one leg. Avoid bouncing or forcing a painful stretch.

04

Trunk control

A short hold or 6–8 repetitions per side

Set up: Choose a knee-supported side plank, a light carry or a dead bug.

Do: Use a version you can control while breathing. See the separate routine for setup.

Cue: Breathe while holding the position.

Feel: Abdominal or side-trunk work without a painful back or shoulder.

A small first session.

EquipmentChair or stable support. A light weight is optional.
First doseOne set of each chosen movement.
EffortFinish knowing you could do a few more controlled repetitions.

Move easily first, then rehearse each movement. Rest between exercises until you can do the next one well. If you already lift, your established program may need a more challenging dose.

Add work when the combination holds up.

Repeat the first dose. If the movements stay controlled and your following runs remain manageable, add a little load or a set. Change one thing.

Start with one session when lifting is new. Build toward two if the week and recovery support it. Soreness is not your score. [2]

Stop for sharp pain or joint pain. Get help with unfamiliar lifts or recurring symptoms. [3]

The checklist is temporary. It does not file a strength workout to your app.

When should you stop?

Stop the set if you lose control or must hold your breath. Reduce the amount or choose an easier version. Stop for sharp pain, joint pain or symptoms that change how you move; persistent or recurring symptoms need assessment.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Learn the movement before chasing the load.
  2. Leave a few controlled repetitions in reserve.
  3. Count the gym and the run in the same week.
You finish the circuit, but tomorrow’s run feels sore enough to change your stride. What do you change?

Reduce the strength dose or change its placement. Completing the circuit is less useful if the amount repeatedly spoils your running.
