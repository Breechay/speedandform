Field Guide
Open this when you are about to shoot

Everything you need before, during, and after a shoot. Open this on your phone at the track.

Observation → ritual → mythology

You don't find mythology first. You notice something. Then you ask what it might represent. Hideout and FORM are already full of rituals — you don't invent them, you witness them.

Level 1 — LiteralWhat am I looking at? Spikes on asphalt. Condensation on a bottle. A man arranging flowers.
Level 2 — FunctionalWhat is happening? Someone preparing. Athletes waiting before a rep. A room being readied.
Level 3 — MythologicalWhat does this represent? Preparation. Commitment. Care. Threshold. The frame earns weight here — not before.

Shortcut when Level 3 isn't obvious yet: What human thing has existed for thousands of years that this reminds me of? Coffee pour → ritual. Track workout → practice. Sunday DJ → fireside storytelling. Flowers → offering.

Before you shoot — mindset

The biggest shift: stop thinking about what the photo will look like and start thinking about what you're actually paying attention to. The image is downstream of the attention. Sharpen the attention first.

Before you arriveKnow your context (field / self / Hideout). Know what arc you're shooting (preparation / effort / recovery). Know your light window.
First 5 minutesDon't shoot. Walk the space. Find the geometry — lines, frames, light sources, interesting backgrounds. Shoot when you've found something.
The frame questionDon't hunt mythology first. Notice what's actually there — spikes, steam, silence, lane lines. Then ask: what ritual am I witnessing? Mythology follows. If you're forcing it, keep looking.
Weather firstIf there's rain, steam, fog, golden light — that is the shot. Everything else waits.
Position mattersMove your body before you touch zoom. Low angle changes everything. Behind the subject changes everything. Wide with context changes everything.

Self-shooting protocol

The goal is never a portrait of yourself. The goal is presence inside structure. You are the small human inside the large frame.

Setup sequence

Scout the location first — find the frame before you set up the tripod
Set tripod at the height and angle you want — low for temples, eye-level for corridors
Mark where you'll stand or walk with a visual reference (pebble, mark on ground)
10-second timer minimum. Burst mode. Take 20+ frames per setup.
Enter the frame and do the actual thing — walk, stretch, look away, keep moving
Review cold — next day. The honest frame is rarely obvious in the moment.

What works

Back to camera — anonymous presence, strongest default
Walking past or through the frame — not stopping inside it
Small figure in large environment — use architecture, sky, landscape
Silhouette against sky, water, or window
Reflection — puddle, glass, mirror, bay

What doesn't

—
Looking at camera and holding still
—
Posing inside the frame
—
Forced action that you wouldn't normally be doing

Athletes — at the track

Shoot the full arc — not just the rep

Before — spikes on track, watch setting, conversation, drills, the stillness before the first rep
During — rhythm and repetition over peak effort. The 8th rep not the 1st.
After — hands on knees, walking recovery, athletes talking, water, rest
Context — one figure, large environment, architecture of the venue

Position and logistics

Arrive earlyFind the geometry before athletes do. The empty track is a frame.
Low angleLane lines converge, athlete comes toward you. Temple logic.
BehindAnonymous, city or sky as background. FORM signature.
Wet trackWorth waiting for every time. Rain changes everything.
Light windowPre-dawn or golden hour. Midday is flat — use it for details only.
Clips10–30 seconds. One action, real time. Not highlight reels.

Hideout — the full day

The rituals to shoot

Opening — empty room, chairs, first light, preparation. Before people arrive.
Service — the espresso handoff. Steam. Pour. Cup placed on wood. Real time.
Village — conversation from across the room. Reading alone. Communal quiet.
Closing — sunlight gone. Wiped tables. Flowers still there.
Details — book spine, cup ring on wood, flower arrangement, hands

How to shoot it

LightWindow light only. Flash kills the atmosphere every time.
ObserverDon't direct. Don't ask people to hold still. Documentary mode.
ClipsOne action, real time. Pour. Place. Select. 15–30 seconds. No cuts needed.
Arrive earlyShoot the space empty first. Then shoot it full. Two different images.

Eye-sharpening exercises

These are things to practice regularly — not one-time exercises. The goal is to train your eye to notice what it already wants to notice, faster and more deliberately.

Exercise 01
The Geometry Walk
Weekly · 20 minutes · no shooting required
Walk a route you know — your block, the track, Edgewater waterfront. Don't photograph anything. Just look for frames: thresholds, corridors, symmetry, lines that converge, architectural repetition. Count how many you find. The goal is to train the eye to find geometry before it finds subject matter. When you can walk a familiar route and see 10 frames you've never noticed, the exercise is working.
Exercise 02
The Weather Obligation
Every time it rains · mandatory
When it rains in Miami, you go out and shoot. Not later. During. Wet streets, puddle reflections, bay surface, wet track if you're near it, steam rising from hot pavement. Even 15 minutes with your phone. The discipline is showing up to weather. Over time you build a Miami rain archive that nobody else has — because nobody else goes out when it's raining.
Exercise 03
The Frame Before the Subject
Every shoot · before you photograph anyone
Spend the first 5–10 minutes of every shoot photographing nothing but the environment — empty track, empty Hideout, empty room. Force yourself to find 3 strong frames before any subject enters them. This trains you to see the architecture before the person, which is the FORM visual hierarchy. The person makes the frame complete, not interesting. The frame has to already be interesting.
Exercise 04
The Ritual Question
Every shot · after you notice, before you press the shutter
Notice first — spikes, steam, silence, lane lines disappearing into dark. Then ask: what ritual am I witnessing? Preparation, ceremony, commitment, threshold. Mythology usually names itself once the ritual is clear. If you're hunting archetype before you've seen the ordinary thing, you'll force it. After a few weeks the sequence compresses: notice → ritual → shoot. Mythology is for the cold review, not the hunt.
Exercise 05
The Cold Review
Every shoot · next day only
Never cull a shoot the same day you shot it. Wait at least one full day, ideally two. When you return to the folder, you will not remember which shots you worked hardest for — only which ones actually have something. This is how you build the discipline of selecting for the image rather than for your effort. The shots you're most proud of in the moment are often not the ones that hold up cold.
Exercise 06
The Reference Audit
Monthly · 30 minutes
Once a month, open your camera roll saved folder and look at the last 10 images you saved from Instagram or anywhere else. For each one, ask: what world does this belong to (Precision Luxury / Atmospheric Machines / Ritual / Human Aliveness)? What is the mythology? What is the geometry? What is the atmosphere? Over time the pattern gets clearer and you stop needing the manual to know what your eye wants.

After the shoot — culling rules

WaitMinimum one day before reviewing. Two days is better. Never same day.
First passDelete anything that is technically broken — blurry, clipped highlights, wrong exposure. No mercy.
Second passDelete anything you kept because you worked hard for it. That is not a reason to keep an image.
Third passDelete anything that looks like other people's images. If it could be stock photography, delete it.
KeepImages where something is actually happening. Where you can name the ritual — and the mythology holds up cold. Where the atmosphere was already there.
The ratio50–200 shots in. 3–8 keepers is a good session. 1–2 is fine. 0 is information — what were you not seeing?
Move filesSame day as review. Stills → SELECTS. Clips for future reels → LIBRARY/. Nothing stays in Downloads.
Library testWould you pull this clip six months from now for a reel? If yes → LIBRARY/ by category. Not graded. Not a project yet.

The thesis stack

ThesisMythology inside reality. Every image elevates an ordinary thing into something archetypal. Not fantasy. Archetype.
MethodOrder + atmosphere. Highly organized systems wrapped in atmosphere. Neither works without the other.
SubjectSelf · Athletes · Hideout · Sundays · Architecture · Miami. 90% of opportunities are already in front of you.
TechniqueCapture → selection → edit → grade. The LUT is the last 5%. The capture is doing the work.

The six principles — compressed

I. MaterialityLight reveals texture. The grade protects it. Never manufacture it. Clarity near zero.
II. LuminosityShadows glow. Blacks breathe. Light feels like it's inside the scene, not striking it.
III. MovementThe athlete is not posing. The room has been lived in. Evidence of activity over arrangement.
IV. AttentionThe camera records what the eye learned to notice. Shoot what you actually see.
V. RestraintRestraint selects. Minimalism removes. Rich but organized. One thing speaks at a time.
VI. AtmosphereFORM does not manufacture atmosphere. FORM reveals it. The goal is not cinematic. Unusually alive.
If the grade becomes more interesting than the experience itself,
pull it back.

The visual hierarchy — in order

1 — First
Geometry
Architecture. Cards. Temples. Bridges. Track lanes. The eye finds structure first. Always.
2 — Second
Atmosphere
Fog. Rain. Steam. Dust. Light. Atmosphere softens geometry. Weather is production value.
3 — Third
Human Presence
Not portraits. Presence. Small humans inside large systems. Individual inside structure.
4 — Last
Narrative
Very subtle. Never obvious. Questions over answers. The image implies a world.

What to never do

—
Teal-orange split tone
—
Crushed blacks — destroys shadow texture
—
Heavy clarity or dehaze — texture must come from light, not sliders
—
Vignette abuse — signals low confidence in the frame
—
Orange skin — never
—
Anything that slows the feeling of being somewhere real

Cinema Doctrine
When It Matters — key extracts
+
The characters that stay are the ones who change the emotional reality around them. Not because they're central. Because when they're in the room — something shifts.

The cinema doctrine is a sibling to the perception manual, not a replacement. Perception studies the frame. Cinema studies people on screen — conduct, ensemble, inference, stillness, service ritual. They meet at director's notes.

The core question — designing characters

When pressure hitsWhat does this character do? Not what are their traits — what do they do? That's behavior. Behavior is character.
What they never doOften more useful than what they do. Charon never loses composure. McCauley never negotiates the code. The constraint defines the person.
Care indirectDoctore's care is in his severity. Gordon's in showing up. Characters that stay show care sideways — never performed directly.
Abundance or hungerRusty's ease comes from inhabitation. Ocean's from enjoyment. Same intensity reads completely differently depending on its source.
Protagonist or forceGordon matters because of Batman and Dent. Some of the most powerful characters don't carry the story — they change what the story means.

Character quick reference

Calm unused capacity
Gerri Kellman
Speaks only when leverage exists. Every word already placed. Institutional patience. The character you underestimate because she lets you.
The private machinery
The Killer (opening)
Slows everything down. Watches. Waits. Narrates internally. Recognition, not aspiration. Rusty is the public surface. This is the private operating mode.
Ease without effort
Rusty Ryan = Breechay
Stays completely himself. Ease sharpens. Nothing rattles the rhythm. Danny plans the operation. Rusty is already inside it.
Ironic precision
Zach Nichols
Destabilizes the room. Ironic detachment deployed as precision. Dangerous because you keep misjudging him. Grandmaster energy.

Ensemble thesis

The most consistent pattern: not singular characters, but systems of differentiated people. What happens when distinct ways of being face the same pressure together — that's where the real drama lives.

Ocean's crewDanny plans. Rusty inhabits. Each crew member stays fully themselves while coordinating into one operation.
Better Call SaulMike: internal law, no waste. Kim: composed precision. Jimmy: adaptive improvisation. Gus: total surface curation. Four architectures, one system.
Criminal IntentEmbodied intelligence. Inference over exposition. Goren noticed object placement, asked something unrelated — you reconstruct the chain. Modern TV explains the inference. That kills it.
SuccessionFamily as a power system. Gerri speaks only when leverage exists. Shiv confuses proximity to power with power. Logan: room reorganizes from fear.

Director's notes — visual choices

Author the environmentThe room communicates before anyone speaks. Grand Budapest, Phantom Thread, Collateral, Hannibal — all authored spaces.
Earn the stillnessIf stillness is the default, movement carries meaning. If silence is normal, speech carries weight. Establish the baseline first.
Service ritualCharon's desk. Doctore's drills. Gustave's rounds. Before dialogue, ritual communicates internal standard.
Vitality under containmentContainment without life reads inert. The character must be alive under the surface. M is just the surface. Craig Bond has vitality underneath.
Inference over expositionDon't explain what a smart character concludes. Let them ask something seemingly unrelated. The audience reconstructs the chain. Never write out the synthesis.

Directors to study

Michael MannHeat, Collateral — atmosphere as character, operator psychology, vitality under pressure.
David FincherSocial Network, The Killer — compression, controlled attention, intelligence at velocity.
Denis VilleneuveSicario, Dune — slow authority, sovereign atmosphere.
Steven SoderberghOcean's trilogy, Erin Brockovich — wide temperature range, ensemble movement.
Wes AndersonGrand Budapest — authored environments, warmth + rigor simultaneously.
Vince GilliganBetter Call Saul — long-arc character revelation, distinct people under shared pressure.

The rule — same day as review

On the day you cull, you move files. Shoot folders to FIELD/SELF/HIDEOUT. Stills to SELECTS. Clips with future value to LIBRARY. Exports to PROJECTS. Not tomorrow. Not "later."

After cullMove shoot folder from import location to correct FORM/FIELD/, FORM/SELF/, or FORM/HIDEOUT/.
Stills — keepersCopy best images to ARCHIVE/SELECTS/ with naming convention. One shoot's best stills.
Clips — libraryAny clip you might pull for a reel later → LIBRARY/ by category (rain, steam, preparation, etc.). Not graded. Not edited.
Reference imagesSaved inspiration → ARCHIVE/REFERENCE/ by world (PRECISION_LUXURY/, RITUAL/, etc.)
DownloadsEmpty after review. No exceptions. If it's still there, the review isn't done.

Media Library
Reusable clips · inventory for reels and edits
+
Not every clip belongs to the shoot it came from. Some clips are future raw material.

Your best reels won't always come from a dedicated shoot. They'll come from Matteo arranging flowers, Simon between reps, steam off coffee, rain on the patio, sunlight moving across a wall. At the time they're just clips. Six months later they're the opening shot.

You don't think "I need footage from June 2nd." You think "I need rain." "I need preparation." "I need an athlete walking away from camera." That's a library problem, not a date problem.

What goes here

LIBRARYReusable clips with signal — not graded, not edited, not tied to one project
SELECTSBest stills from a specific shoot — finished images
PROJECTSExports when you're building FORM reels, Sunday films, athlete profiles

Categories

What to watch for — regardless of film

EnvironmentDoes the room communicate before anyone speaks? How? What does the production design do that dialogue doesn't need to?
StillnessWhen do characters go still? What earns that moment? How much does the camera move or not move?
RitualWhat repeated actions reveal character? Service, preparation, cleanup, arrival. The ceremony inside ordinary behavior.
InferenceWhat is the film not explaining? Where does it trust you to reconstruct the chain without being told?
EnsembleWhat does each character reveal about the others by contrast? What does Batman become when placed beside Superman?
AtmosphereHow is atmosphere created? Lens, light, sound design, pacing — before grade. What is the capture doing?
