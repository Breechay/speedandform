# The Athletic Life: agent guide

October 28, 2026 · Research context for app audits and opportunity work

## Start here

The central question is: **What could FORM make possible in an athlete's life that probably would not have happened, or would have been significantly harder, without FORM?**

Use this collection to understand the ambition, test assumptions and recognize opportunities. It is not a feature specification, an approved backlog, a training prescription or proof of athlete demand.

## Authority and freshness

Read the current owning repository's AGENTS.md, current owner decisions and relevant implementation contracts before proposing or changing anything. Current explicit owner decisions and verified owning records control. This October 28 research collection provides context; it does not override them.

The reports share a brief and much of the same literature. Agreement among reports is not independent validation. No athlete interviews are established by this collection. Numerical examples and scenes are proposals unless linked to attributable observations.

The September 30 audience documents distinguish remote programs, grandfathered app access and paying personal-coaching clients outside the app. These are dated source statements, not a newly audited census. Verify the relationship and channel relevant to the present task.

Record counts do not establish active app users, paying customers, demand or retention. The 19 target/alternative race entries are not automatically a conversion failure. A filing count without timeframe and coverage cannot establish adoption.

## When auditing the app

Name the build, commit, environment, account role and date actually inspected. Separate source review, automated tests, signed-in runtime behavior, physical-device checks and production verification.

Trace a concrete athlete task through the owning records and visible experience. Ask whether the person can understand the work, see what they actually did, understand relevant progress and retain control of their choices. Correctness, identity, delivery, privacy and readable presentation are foundational.

Preserve these distinctions: prescribed work, performed work, athlete report, measured evidence, coach interpretation and coach decision. Missing evidence stays missing. Never infer movement mechanics from splits alone or treat engagement as readiness.

For each finding, provide the observed behavior, evidence, human consequence, current owner, severity and smallest useful correction. Separate defects from optional opportunities. Reconcile with existing work before creating another backlog or imposing a new research gate.

Do not turn a strategic proposal into automatic changes to training, access policy, notifications or private data sharing. The coach owns prescriptions. Introductions and publication need the appropriate consent. An audit does not authorize athlete contact or a production release.

## When considering opportunities

Start with a person and a wanted outcome. Consider coaching, practical coordination, introductions, hosting, film, photography and software as possible contributions. Remote athletes and independent members deserve complete experiences in their own right.

For a proposal, state:

- Who might benefit and what they actually want, distinguishing a known preference from an assumption.
- What could become possible, and why existing arrangements might be insufficient.
- The supporting evidence, source date, limitations and competing explanations.
- What FORM would contribute, who would do the work, and the likely time or cost.
- What requires permission, what could be unwelcome, and how someone can decline.
- What would be learned from the smallest useful attempt, including a result that would argue against continuing.

Capability, independence, enjoyment and a memory worth keeping can be valuable. More messages, registrations or screen time are not universal measures of success. Beauty and hospitality need not be justified solely by retention.

One New Door, Target Night, season artifacts and film sessions remain possibilities, not standing offers. No experiment or service is implied to have started merely because it appears here.

## Return useful work

Lead with the concrete finding or opportunity. Link its evidence. Label it as a verified fact, reported experience, interpretation, hypothesis or chosen value. State what changed, what remains unknown and which decision, if any, is needed.

Keep the founding brief untouched. New evidence belongs in a dated companion, source note or owning record. Preserve original reports and their disagreements; update the editorial assessment when stronger evidence warrants a change.
