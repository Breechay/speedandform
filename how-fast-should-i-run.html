How fast should I run?

Choose your effort from the job of today’s run. Easy and most long runs should let you chat; threshold work feels stronger, but stays controlled.

Your pace is a tool, not your grade.

Imagine a toolbox. A hammer and a screwdriver are both useful. You choose the tool for the job. Running works the same way: the fastest effort is not the best effort for every run.

This matters because you are training a whole week. If you race the easy days, you may arrive tired for the session you meant to do well. Easier running has value of its own. [1]

What should each run feel like?

Use these as coaching cues, not exact laboratory zones.
RunA simple checkThe job
EasyYou can chat in full sentences.Build endurance and repeat the practice.
Threshold / some tempo runsShort phrases. Strong running you can keep under control.Practice holding a demanding effort.
Long runUsually conversational, especially at the start.Keep a manageable effort going longer.
Faster repetitionsMore demanding breathing; recover as instructed.Practice a specific faster-running task.
StridesBrief, smooth accelerations with recovery.Practice quick running without a long hard effort.

Easy still feels like exercise. It does not mean you breathe as if you are sitting on a couch. Comfortable conversation is the useful check. [2]

One runner. Two different days.

On a cool, flat route, your easy run lets you chat at your usual pace. On a hot day, the same number leaves you grabbing for breath. You slow down.

You have kept the effort right. There is no lost pace to repay later.

Hills, wind and tired legs can change the same calculation. In heat, also choose a cooler time or shorter route when needed. [3]

How do I find my actual pace?

Start with the running you can do now. A recent race or familiar workout can help a coach estimate a training pace. A goal you hope to race later is not evidence that you can train there today.

Your watch gives you pace and heart rate. Your breathing, legs and control tell you how the work is going. Use them together. Watch zones depend on the settings and zone system.

A race pace chart tells you the pace needed for a finish time. It does not decide your easy or threshold pace.

Quick questions.

Will running slowly make me slow?

Easy running supports endurance. Faster work has a place when your training and recovery support it. A useful week has different jobs rather than one effort for every day.

Should I copy my friend’s pace?

No. The same pace can feel easy to one runner and hard to another. You can run together using a pace that lets both of you meet the day’s purpose.

Should the last mile always be fastest?

Only when that finish is part of the plan. If today is easy, finish easy. Feeling good is not an instruction to add a test.

Use it this week.

Before your next familiar 20–30-minute easy run, name its job. Check conversation after 5 minutes and once near the finish; slow down if speech stops being comfortable.

Three mistakes to catch.

  1. Choosing every pace from a future race goal.
  2. Making an easy day hard because a friend is faster.
  3. Holding the same pace through heat or hills despite rising effort.

Where does it fit?

Effort choice comes before workout choice. Get this right so easy days, stronger sessions and longer runs can each do their job.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Choose the job before the pace.
  2. Use breathing and control alongside the watch.
  3. Let the pace change when the day changes.
It is hot. Your usual easy pace makes you gasp. What do you change?

Slow down or use walking breaks so conversation is comfortable. The job is still an easy run. A familiar number does not make the effort easy today.

Try explaining the idea to a friend in your own words.
