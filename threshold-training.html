What is threshold running?

Threshold running is strong, sustained running you can keep under control. Its job is to help you hold a demanding effort for longer, with enough reserve to complete the planned work.

Think of a volume dial.

Your breathing is deeper than on an easy run. You can say a short phrase, but you would not choose to chat for the whole session. Your steps still feel organized.

You should be concentrating on the work, not fighting to survive every minute. Effort can rise as the session goes on. Repeated slowing, straining or needing much longer rests tells you to adjust.

Why should you care?

Being quick for one minute and holding a strong pace for a race are different skills. Threshold sessions give you practice with the second: keeping a demanding effort going.

In the whole week, easy runs build repeatable endurance work. Threshold adds controlled stronger work. A long run practices staying out longer. Each contributes something; threshold cannot replace the rest.

For a half marathon, stronger sustained running matters. You also need pacing, longer-run preparation, food and drink practice, and recovery. One good threshold workout is not a race guarantee.

What about lactate?

Lactate is a substance your body makes and uses during exercise. Other tissues can use it as fuel. It is not simply waste that makes your legs stop. [2]

Scientists use several tests and definitions for “threshold.” They do not all give the same speed. In this guide, the coaching target is strong, sustained running you can control. Breathing cues and a watch cannot locate an exact laboratory boundary. [1]

You do not need a blood test to understand the session’s job. You do need an effort and amount of work that fit you.

What does a threshold workout look like?

Keep the first piece restrained. Pick a recent training pace you already know, then adjust for today’s effort. Do not choose a pace from the race time you wish you could run.

The easy jogs give you a break so you can keep doing the intended work. Some runners use one continuous block instead. Broken and continuous work are options, not levels everyone must pass.

New to running or returning after a break? Establish comfortable easy running before copying this faster session.

How do you know when to add more?

You stay controlled and recover well.
Repeat it. Later, add a little useful time if it fits the week. You do not have to progress every Tuesday.
You race the first piece.
Slow the next one. Reduce the remaining work if needed.
You need far longer rests to keep the pace.
Change the pace or shorten the session. Longer rests do not turn an unsuitable pace into the right one.
Heat or hills push effort up.
Keep the job, adjust the numbers. You may need less speed or less time. [3]

Change one demand at a time. More minutes, less rest and more speed are three changes, not one. In my coaching, one threshold day can be a useful starting arrangement for an established runner. It is not a rule for every runner or every phase.

Tempo, threshold and race pace.

Is a tempo run the same as a threshold run?

Sometimes. “Tempo” can also mean moderately steady running. Ask what effort, duration and recovery the coach intends. The name alone is not enough.

Is threshold pace my half-marathon pace?

Not automatically. The relation depends on your ability and how long your race lasts. Two runners covering the same distance can race at different efforts relative to their thresholds.

What heart rate should I use?

There is no single threshold heart rate for all runners. Watch estimates and zone settings vary. Use your established guidance alongside breathing, pace control and conditions.

Should I sprint the last repetition?

Finish the work as written. A sprint changes the question. The useful result is sustained work you can recover from.

Use it this week.

Already ready for controlled faster work? The 3 × 6-minute example below shows the full session: 15 easy + 6 strong + 2 easy + 6 strong + 2 easy + 6 strong + 10 easy = 47 minutes. Use your current guidance; if this effort is new, learn easy running first.

Three mistakes to catch.

  1. Racing the first repetition, then fading.
  2. Using a dream race pace as today’s training pace.
  3. Adding speed, more work and shorter rests together.

Where does it fit?

Threshold is one way to practice sustained stronger running. Easy runs, long runs, fueling and recovery still have their own jobs.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Strong enough to work. Controlled enough to repeat.
  2. Train at today’s ability, not your dream race pace.
  3. Finish the planned work, then check your recovery.
The first six minutes are fast. The next two pieces get much slower. Was faster better?

No. Start more gently next time. The aim is sustained, controlled work. A fast first piece that spoils the rest misses the job.
