Where should I start with running mechanics?

Start with what costs you effort, comfort or control. Rhythm, balance and direction give you three ways to describe the question.

Three ways to notice movement.

01

Rhythm

Do the steps feel smooth and repeatable?

Understand cadence →
02

Control

Does the movement feel balanced without forcing it?

Explore trunk work →
03

Direction

Do you feel as if you are reaching, jarring or pushing upward?

Test shorter steps →

These questions overlap. They are not three diagnoses or a scorecard.

Use the smallest useful experiment.

  1. 01

    Describe

    Name when the difficulty appears and at what effort.

  2. 02

    Choose

    Try one small cue, or reduce the effort first.

  3. 03

    Check

    Compare at a similar speed and repeat another day.

A useful experiment may leave the movement alone. Persistent pain or a new limp needs clinical assessment. [1]

Use it this week.

Describe 1 movement question after a familiar run: when it appears, at what effort, and what it costs you. Choose 1 small experiment from the linked cue lesson.

Three mistakes to catch.

  1. Diagnosing a weak muscle from one video.
  2. Choosing a fix before describing the problem.
  3. Treating a prettier movement as proof of better running.

Where does it fit?

The map helps you find the right question. The linked lessons and routines help you test an answer.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Describe the problem before choosing a fix.
  2. Similar-looking movement can need different decisions.
  3. Check the effect in the runner, not only the picture.
A video shows movement at the hips. Can the map tell you which muscle is weak?

No. The map helps you name an observation. It cannot diagnose a muscle or the cause of pain.
