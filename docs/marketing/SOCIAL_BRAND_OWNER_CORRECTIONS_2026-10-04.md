# Speed & Form social brand · owner corrections

**Owner correction · October 4, 2026**

This note refines and overrides conflicting Instagram recommendations in `docs/SOCIAL_BRAND_SYSTEM_2026-10.md`. It does not replace the wider naming, mark, email, YouTube or partner-facing system.

## 1. Instagram's primary job is athlete attraction

The Instagram account is not primarily a hotel brochure or a partner deck. Its first job is to make strong athletes want to follow, reach out, train with FORM and identify with the practice.

The current account already supplies useful evidence. In the October 4 profile snapshot, the three pinned athlete/team posts show approximately 53.4K, 25.1K and 29K views. Other male-athlete, group-running, physique, movement, race and training clips also materially outperform many designed or explanatory posts. Brice reports that the account continues to attract strong athletes who reach out because they recognize themselves in that world.

Therefore:

- feed the formats and subjects that have already earned attention;
- do not rebalance the account primarily to make it easier for hotels or corporate partners to read;
- do not replace the existing athletic magnetism with generic premium-wellness imagery;
- use the website, FORM House page, email, partner briefs and decks for deeper partner explanation;
- let Instagram prove that FORM has a culture people want to enter.

The brand system should clarify and strengthen the proven content engine, not constrain or replace it.

## 2. The predominantly male athletic cohort is a core asset

The current FORM cohort is predominantly men. That is not a defect to correct and not a temporary embarrassment to edit around.

Keep showing the actual people and visual qualities that are already resonating:

- strong male athletes;
- shirtless running when it is natural to the weather and session;
- physiques developed through the work;
- group chemistry and friendship;
- Miami roads, track, waterfront and heat;
- running data, pace and distance overlays when they strengthen the story;
- raw training footage, race moments and post-run social life;
- individual athletes who look capable, confident and compelling.

Include women when they are genuinely present. Do not cast people to manufacture balance, broaden the grid artificially or imply that the current male-heavy roster is a branding problem.

Appearance is not an entry requirement, but the visual appeal of the actual athletes is part of why the account works. Do not hide that fact or write it explicitly as a promise.

## 3. Brice's quiet coaching does not need to become performance content

Brice coaches quietly from the sideline. Do not manufacture a louder, more visible or constantly hands-on coaching persona merely to make the account appear more authored.

His authority may remain understated:

- watching from outside the repetition;
- making one short intervention;
- speaking quietly after a rep;
- reviewing footage, notes or paces;
- writing the training and selecting what changes next;
- explaining a decision in a caption or record post when useful.

Brice does not need a fixed quota of coaching photographs. The athletes and their work can remain the visual center. Coaching authorship should surface occasionally through judgment, records, studies and precise language rather than staged demonstrations.

A useful expression of the method remains:

> Watch first. Change what matters. Let the athlete own the work.

## 4. Evidence-led Instagram content hierarchy

Use this as a directional mix, not a rigid quota:

- **65% athlete / action / group culture** — the proven attraction engine;
- **20% record / result / data / training evidence** — proof inside the same athletic world;
- **10% invitation / coaching / product** — Thursday, Run Development, plans and clear next steps;
- **5% return / hospitality / partner concept** — enough to expand the world without turning the feed into a venue pitch.

Prioritize the patterns already earning attention:

- athletic bodies in real movement;
- strong groups and recognizable personalities;
- camaraderie and belonging;
- speed, distance, race and training data;
- Miami light, heat and place;
- raw POV and phone footage when the moment is strong;
- high-quality reels that preserve the same energy rather than replacing it with a template.

The public black/bone identity should function as a light editorial wrapper: avatar, occasional record covers, restrained typography, consistent end cards and cleaner pinned/highlight treatment. It should not force every post into a designed template.

## 5. Preserve proven pins and social proof

Do not unpin high-performing athlete/team posts merely to manufacture a perfect brand architecture.

The current pinned posts are meaningful social proof. Keep them unless:

- a new post performs or communicates materially better;
- one pin becomes factually stale;
- a clear commercial or practice explanation earns the space without weakening attraction.

A practice explainer, Run Development post or record post may eventually replace one pin, but there is no requirement to replace all three at once.

Profile direction:

- Handle: **`@form.practice`**
- Display name: **Speed & Form**
- Bio: **A Coached Athletic Practice** may remain; add context only when it improves conversion without clutter.
- Link: `https://speedandform.com/`
- Avatar: use the real SF emblem in the cleanest legible treatment, but do not redesign the entire account around the avatar.

## 6. The next nine posts should feed the winner

Do not perform a decorative nine-post reset. Across the next nine posts, aim approximately for:

- six athlete/action/group posts using proven formats;
- one record/result post;
- one invitation, coaching or plan post;
- one return/hospitality/FORM Morning post when there is real material.

The exact order follows the strongest available footage and current practice. Performance, athlete response and actual moments outrank a checkerboard pattern.

## 7. Success measures

Judge the Instagram direction by:

- strong athletes reaching out;
- quality follower growth;
- shares, saves and profile visits;
- direct messages from runners who fit the practice;
- coaching inquiries;
- invitations and partnership interest that arise because the culture is visibly desirable;
- continued reach of athlete-led content.

Do not judge it primarily by whether every post looks like a partner presentation.

## 8. Desired interpretation

The account should communicate:

> Strong athletes train together here. The work is real. The people are compelling. FORM is a practice serious runners want to enter.

Partners should be attracted to the existing athletic culture, not because Instagram has been converted into hospitality advertising.

The website and partner materials explain the commercial structure. Instagram makes the world desirable.
