# Speed & Form social brand · owner corrections

**Owner correction · October 4, 2026**

This note refines `docs/SOCIAL_BRAND_SYSTEM_2026-10.md`. It does not replace the wider system.

## 1. The current FORM cohort is predominantly men

Do not manufacture gender balance, stage a more mixed roster than exists, or treat the current male-heavy team as a branding defect.

The public record should remain truthful:

- show the athletes who actually train;
- include women when they are genuinely present in the practice or event;
- never cast people merely to correct the appearance of the grid;
- keep invitation language open to the people the session can safely and honestly serve;
- do not imply that appearance, physique or gender is an entry requirement.

The risk is not that the team is mostly men. The risk is that a top layer made only of shirtless group poses can be misread as a body-focused social club rather than a coached athletic practice.

Preserve the male athletic energy while balancing the public edit with:

- complete bodies in real training;
- session preparation and structure;
- effort and concentration;
- quieter individual frames;
- team interaction;
- records and coaching decisions;
- the return after the work.

The account does not need artificial gender symmetry. It needs a more complete reading of the practice.

## 2. Brice coaches quietly from the sideline

Do not stage Brice as a loud, demonstrative or constantly hands-on coach. His authority should appear through attention, restraint and precise decisions.

The visual language of coaching should include:

- Brice standing slightly outside the action and watching;
- scanning the group rather than performing for the camera;
- one short cue before the next repetition;
- a quiet one-to-one exchange after a rep;
- reviewing footage, notes, paces or the next piece of work;
- walking beside an athlete;
- a small hand gesture or positional cue;
- athletes carrying the work without Brice occupying every frame.

The editorial language should make the judgment visible:

- what Brice noticed;
- why he did or did not intervene;
- the one change selected;
- what happened on the next repetition;
- what remains open.

A useful expression of the style:

> Watch first. Change what matters. Let the athlete own the work.

Brice's quiet presence is not an absence of coaching. It is part of the method.

## 3. Content implications

Replace any recommendation for performative coaching imagery with **observation / precise intervention / athlete ownership**.

For the first nine-post direction, item 3 should read:

> **Brice observing / quiet coaching / portrait** — leadership shown through attention and judgment, not volume.

For partner-facing proof, show that the practice is responsibly directed without pretending Brice is an emcee or motivational performer.

The desired interpretation is:

> A real, predominantly male athletic cohort. A quiet coach with a clear eye. Serious work, good people and a thoughtful return.

Do not make the practice broader or louder in the branding than it is in life.
