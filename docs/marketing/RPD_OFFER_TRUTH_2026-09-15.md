# Race Pace Durability — Offer Truth

**Date:** September 15, 2026  
**Status:** current product and distribution truth  
**Owner:** Speed & Form / FORM

This file exists so marketing, product, checkout, app work and future agents do not accidentally turn Race Pace Durability into a sub-1:30-only product.

## Core product

**Race Pace Durability is a 15-week half-marathon durability method for experienced runners.**

The method is shared. **The pace is athlete-relative.**

The race-pace number is not the product. The product is the progression:

**broken race-pace work → 5 continuous → 6 continuous → 8 continuous → late-race-pace work → 12 continuous late → race**

The canonical ownership asks are:

**5 → 6 → 8 → 12 → race**

A runner targeting ~1:15, ~1:30, or another half-marathon time can use the same architecture if the prescribed band and workload are appropriate to their current fitness.

## What is fixed

- 15 weeks.
- Six running days per week.
- Starts around 45 mpw and builds to ~60 mpw.
- Requires an existing long-run base; it is not a beginner/base-building plan.
- Tuesday develops race-pace ownership.
- Thursday keeps a ceiling above race pace.
- Saturday develops duration and later asks whether race-pace access survives prior mileage.
- The pace does not automatically get faster because a week went well.
- The athlete response can require a repeat, hold, or change.

## What is athlete-relative

- Race-pace band.
- Current threshold.
- Training target / margin above the stated race goal.
- Any coach-authored adjustment based on evidence.

Current study examples are evidence, not product constants:

- José: race-pace band 6:30–6:45/mi; threshold about 6:15/mi.
- Hope: race-pace band 6:45–7:00/mi; threshold about 6:20–6:25/mi.

Do not describe `6:30–6:45/mi` as Race Pace Durability itself.

## Important distinction: goal pace vs training band

The system already distinguishes three things:

1. **Stated race goal** — what the athlete wants to race for.
2. **Training target** — what the athlete is currently being trained to support.
3. **Prescribed race-pace band** — the actual band used in race-pace work.

For the current sub-1:30 study, the training target has intentionally been more ambitious than 1:29:59, and the prescribed band gives the athlete room above that target. That is a coaching decision, not a universal arithmetic rule.

Therefore, do **not** publish a generic formula such as “take goal pace minus 15 seconds” until Brice explicitly authors that rule for self-guided buyers.

## Acquisition positioning

The product is broader than sub-1:30, but an advertisement may still use a narrower wedge when that makes the problem more legible.

Examples:

- “You can hit the pace. Can you hold it?” — general product problem.
- A sub-1:30 creative may use 6:52/mi or Hope/José as a concrete case study **without implying that $79 only buys a sub-1:30 plan**.
- A future 1:15 case can use ~5:43/mi in the same method if the training band and workload are authored appropriately.

The landing page should always return to the general truth:

> **Your pace is individual. The progression is shared.**

## Product gap that must be solved before scale

The canonical coach-led system knows that the band is authored per athlete. A self-guided buyer currently needs a trustworthy way to choose or verify their band without hidden coaching.

This is the most important unresolved packaging question.

Before meaningful scale, choose one of these deliberately:

1. **Qualification + band-setting protocol:** buyer uses recent race/threshold evidence and a clearly authored rule.
2. **Pre-start diagnostic:** buyer submits a few inputs and receives a bounded band recommendation without ongoing coaching.
3. **Multiple authored pace examples / lookup table:** only if Brice is comfortable making the relationships explicit and generalizable.
4. **Keep band selection as a prerequisite:** sell only to runners who already know an appropriate race-pace band; state this plainly.

Do not invent a formula merely to remove friction.

## Copy law

- “the method is shared; the pace is athlete-relative”

- “the sub-1:30 plan”
- “6:30–6:45 is the plan”

- “everyone should run threshold 30 seconds faster”
