## 0. Assignment

We now have a real product and live checkout. The problem is distribution.

The job is not simply to “make a Meta ad.” The job is to design a measurable acquisition and execution system that can take the right runner from:

**attention → self-recognition → interest → trust → purchase → execution → useful result → referral / coaching interest**

The primary objective is **paid purchases of Race Pace Durability** from runners for whom the plan is genuinely appropriate.

The secondary objective is to learn, as quickly and cheaply as possible:
- who responds to the idea;
- which problem framing makes the right runner stop;
- which creative earns qualified traffic;
- which landing-page evidence changes intent;
- whether $79 is the right packaging / price;
- whether the app increases conversion or adherence;
- whether plan buyers later become coaching clients;
- where the funnel breaks when it does not convert.

This is a live experiment. Do not reward cleverness without measurable downstream behavior.

# 2. The commercial problem

Cheap and free half-marathon plans are abundant.

A current market scan shows a spectrum from approximately:
- ~$10 downloadable branded plans;
- ~$13 low-cost plans inside training platforms;
- ~$37 mid-priced TrainingPeaks-style plans;
- ~$60 bundles with app / resources;
- ~$120 higher-end app-delivered structured plans.

Therefore **$79 cannot feel like “15 weeks of rows in a table.”**

At $79, the product must earn its price through some combination of:
- a sharper training thesis;
- better fit / qualification;
- an execution experience;
- superior usability;
- credible evidence;
- app delivery;
- decision rules;
- clearer race-week behavior;
- transparent method;
- better adherence;
- access to a living body of evidence.

Do not lower the price merely because cheaper plans exist. First test whether the packaging communicates the value.

# 3. Who this is for

## Primary athlete hypothesis

An experienced, self-directed runner who:
- is already running consistently;
- is comfortable around 45+ miles per week or is genuinely close to that base;
- runs five to six days per week;
- can already complete a substantial long run;
- is targeting a serious half-marathon result, especially sub-1:30;
- has enough speed to believe the goal is plausible but does not yet trust the pace over the full distance;
- likes structure and wants to know why a session exists;
- does not need daily motivational theater;
- is willing to execute a demanding plan independently.

A likely emotional state:
> “I can run the pace in workouts. I am not sure I can still own it when the race gets long.”

That is more specific and valuable than “I want to get faster.”

## Candidate performance profile to test, not yet a hard public rule

Agents should challenge the exact boundaries, but a likely high-intent buyer may sit around:
- recent half: roughly 1:31–1:42;
- 5K / 10K fitness indicating that sub-1:30 is not absurd;
- already training 35–60+ mpw;
- race 12–20 weeks away.

Do not publish unvalidated cutoffs merely because they sound neat.

## Psychographic traits
- competitive but not necessarily elite;
- evidence-oriented;
- already knows basic running vocabulary;
- likely uses Strava / Garmin / COROS / Apple Watch;
- has downloaded or built plans before;
- dislikes generic advice;
- may have failed a race goal despite decent short-distance speed;
- values control, progress and clarity.

## Not for
- couch-to-5K beginners;
- runners currently unable to tolerate the starting volume;
- someone wanting one-to-one plan modification for $79;
- someone expecting an injury treatment service;
- someone who wants guaranteed sub-1:30 performance.

The creative and landing page should self-qualify aggressively. A smaller group of right buyers is better than a large group of wrong clicks.

# 4. The central problem / promise

The strongest current territory is:

## Problem
**Reaching a pace is not the same as keeping access to it.**

## Product mechanism
The resets disappear. Continuous exposure grows. Later, the pace is asked for after earlier miles.

## Desired transformation
The runner becomes able to carry more of an already-available pace deeper into the half marathon.

## Claim discipline
Do **not** say:
- “Guaranteed sub-1:30.”
- “This will make you durable.”
- “Science proves this plan works.”
- “Prevent injury.”
- “Run 1:29 in 15 weeks.”

Prefer:
- “Built around a durability demand.”
- “The plan progressively removes the resets.”
- “Designed to extend how long you can hold the assigned pace.”
- “A 15-week sub-1:30 block for experienced runners.”
- “Preview the first four weeks.”
- “The method is being tested publicly.”

# 5. Evidence basis: what can responsibly inform the funnel

## Endurance science
Recent durability / physiological-resilience literature treats the deterioration of endurance-performance determinants during prolonged exercise as a meaningful performance dimension.

The 2025 literature supports the **concept** that:
- performance determinants can deteriorate as prolonged exercise accumulates;
- athletes differ in how much they deteriorate;
- long-term consistent endurance training and prolonged sessions may contribute to resilience;
- some race-pace / higher-intensity work under accumulating fatigue is a plausible training strategy.

The literature does **not** validate this exact 15-week commercial plan as a proven intervention. The ad must maintain that distinction.

## Ad / attention evidence
Meta currently recommends Reels-native creative and Advantage+ placements. Meta’s own published tests report better performance for **9:16 vertical video with audio and key elements kept in the safe zone** than still-image assets in Reels placements.

Implication:
- build a native 9:16 master asset;
- make it legible without audio but better with audio;
- create a 4:5 feed derivative;
- keep key copy away from UI overlays;
- do not simply crop a desktop graphic into a Reel.

## Purchase psychology
Recent e-commerce reviews repeatedly identify:
- trust;
- perceived risk;
- perceived security;
- product information;
- seller information;
- e-WOM / social proof

as meaningful contributors to purchase behavior.

Implication:
The landing page should reduce uncertainty rather than merely intensify desire.

## Exercise adherence
Self-Determination Theory research consistently supports the relevance of:
- competence;
- autonomy;
- relatedness / support;
- autonomous motivation

to exercise participation and adherence.

Implementation-intention research supports making “when / where / how” concrete.

Implication:
The product should not stop at a calendar. It should help the buyer know:
- whether this plan is appropriate;
- when to start;
- what today asks;
- how to interpret the effort;
- what to do when life interrupts the week;
- how to see progress without needing constant coach intervention.

# 6. Funnel architecture

The real funnel is:

**1. Exposure**  
Did the right runner see it?

**2. Attention**  
Did the first frame / image create a pattern interruption?

**3. Self-recognition**  
Did they think “that is my problem”?

**4. Curiosity**  
Do they want to understand the method?

**5. Landing-page comprehension**  
Can they understand who it is for, how it works and what it costs within ~10 seconds?

**6. Trust / risk reduction**  
Do they believe the product is legitimate, appropriate and sufficiently specific?

**7. Purchase intent**  
Do they inspect the preview, price, progression or checkout?

**8. Checkout**  
Do they begin and complete Stripe checkout?

**9. Onboarding**  
Can they start correctly without emailing Brice to decode what they bought?

**10. Execution**  
Do they actually follow it?

**11. Outcome**  
Did the plan improve the problem it was purchased to solve?

**12. Compounding**  
Does the athlete refer another runner, buy coaching, or remain inside FORM?

Advertising is only stages 1–4. The business is the entire chain.

# 7. Recommended launch structure

## Phase 0 — instrumentation before meaningful spend

Required:
- Meta Pixel functioning on landing page.
- GA4 functioning.
- `ViewContent` or equivalent on the product landing page.
- `InitiateCheckout` on the Stripe CTA.
- Reliable `Purchase` signal.
- Campaign / creative UTMs.
- Stripe purchase record that can be reconciled with source.

### Purchase tracking
Current Stripe hosted confirmation alone is not the ideal endpoint for paid-media optimization.

Minimum viable:
- successful Stripe checkout redirects to a Speed & Form thank-you page;
- thank-you page fires GA4 purchase + Meta Purchase.

Better:
- Stripe webhook verifies payment server-side;
- server-side event / Conversions API records the purchase;
- buyer receives immediate access;
- browser event is deduplicated against server event.

Do not spend seriously on “Purchase optimization” while purchase measurement is ambiguous.

## Phase 1 — prospecting
Start with a small number of materially different creative hypotheses.

Recommended first test:
- 1 short 9:16 video;
- 1 4:5/static concept;
- same product;
- same landing page;
- same price;
- same audience strategy.

Do not test six tiny copy differences at once.

## Phase 2 — retargeting
Add only when the audience is large enough to be meaningful.

Retarget:
- landing-page visitors;
- 50%+ video viewers;
- Instagram engagers;
- Week-4 / paywall viewers;
- checkout starters who did not buy.

Exclude buyers.

Retargeting creative should answer objections rather than repeat the cold hook.

The ad should **not** dump cold traffic on the homepage.

The destination should be a purpose-built Race Pace Durability product page.

The existing plan / purchase surfaces should be treated as raw material, not sacred structure.

## Above the fold

Eyebrow:
**HALF MARATHON · 15 WEEKS**

Headline:
**You can reach the pace.  
Can you keep it?**

Subhead:
**Race Pace Durability is a sub-1:30 block for experienced runners. The resets disappear, the continuous miles grow, and later the pace arrives after earlier work.**

Facts:
**45→60 mpw · 6 days/week · $79 once**

Primary CTA:
**Get the full 15 weeks · $79**

Secondary text link:
**Preview Weeks 1–4**

Trust microcopy:
**One-time purchase · No subscription · Coaching not included**

The user should understand product, audience, mechanism and price without scrolling.

## Section 2 — the problem
Headline:
**Speed is only useful if it is still there.**

Explain:
- short reps can hide durability limitations;
- half-marathon performance is prolonged;
- the block progressively removes recovery and moves the same demand later.

Keep it short.

## Section 3 — the progression
A single strong visual:

**BROKEN**
↓  
**5 CONTINUOUS**
↓  
**6**
↓  
**8**
↓  
**12 AFTER EARLIER MILES**
↓  
**RACE**

This should replace paragraphs.

## Section 4 — what the week actually contains
Show one real representative week:
- race-pace / specificity session;
- threshold / ceiling support;
- long run;
- easy mileage.

The buyer should be able to imagine Tuesday morning.

## Section 5 — who should buy / who should not
Two-column or yes/no section.

**Good fit**
- experienced runner;
- current consistent mileage;
- substantial long run already normal;
- serious half goal;
- comfortable self-directing.

**Not yet**
- new runner;
- currently far below starting volume;
- wants daily individualized adjustment;
- returning from unresolved injury.

This reduces refunds and support burden.

Use the live study.

Copy:

Let the visitor inspect Weeks 1–4 in place.

Do not make “free” the hero promise.

Position:
**15-week paid plan. Preview the first four weeks before you buy.**

- exact key sessions;

# 15. Post-purchase experience

A purchase is not success. Execution is.

## Ideal immediate sequence
**0 minutes**
- payment succeeds;
- Purchase event fires;
- access appears immediately;
- buyer receives email receipt / access.

**First screen**
Headline:
**You own the full 15 weeks. Before Week 1, check the starting point.**

Ask:
- race date;
- recent weekly mileage;
- longest recent run;
- recent 5K / 10K / half performance;
- preferred long-run day.

Output:
- “Good fit to begin”;
- or “Build base first / contact us.”

This is qualification after purchase without pretending the plan is universally appropriate.

## Week 0
Give:
- how to choose / understand the working band;
- easy-run guidance;
- how to use the plan;
- missed-session rule;
- when to stop / seek professional medical guidance;
- fueling / weather reminders where relevant.

## During the block
Support adherence through:
- weekly view;
- one dominant key-session goal;
- session completion;
- visible progression;
- optional reminders;
- no guilt language for missed days.

## At milestone weeks
Ask one or two useful questions:
- Did you complete the prescribed continuous distance?
- Was the limiter aerobic / muscular / mechanical / other?
- What was RPE / reserve?

Do not turn a $79 product into hidden one-to-one coaching.

# 17. Economics

At $79, paid acquisition is not infinitely forgiving.

Ignoring taxes and support, a typical US card fee plus the selected Radar screening fee leaves roughly **$76** before ad cost.

Therefore:
- ~$76 CAC is near gross break-even before any labor / support.
- ~$38 CAC leaves roughly half the sale for contribution before other costs.
- CAC above product revenue is only rational if downstream buyer LTV clearly supports it.

This is why the plan must be treated as either:
1. a profitable standalone product; or
2. an intentional entry product with measurable downstream coaching / ecosystem value.

Do not let those two economic stories blur.

# 18. Initial media budget / test design

The first spend should buy information, not scale.

Working test:
- **$20–30/day**
- **7–14 days**
- 2 materially different creatives
- one landing page
- one price
- one product

Approximate first validation budget:
**$200–$400**, with explicit stop / review conditions.

Do not automatically spend the whole budget if the funnel is obviously broken.

## Early diagnostics
If:
- impressions but poor CTR → creative / audience problem;
- clicks but poor LPV → technical / accidental-click problem;
- LPV but no meaningful engagement → ad / landing mismatch;
- engagement but no checkout → offer / proof / price / risk problem;
- checkout starts but no purchases → checkout / trust / payment friction;
- purchases but no execution → onboarding / product problem;
- execution but poor outcomes → training / fit / adherence problem.

Optimize the broken stage, not the entire system at once.

1. **Verdict (0–10)** on the current proposed system.
2. **The single biggest reason it will fail**, if unchanged.
3. **The strongest market / customer hypothesis.**
4. **Recommended ad concept**, including exact first frame.
5. **Exact ad copy:** primary text, headline, description, CTA.
6. **Landing-page wireframe**, in order.
7. **Packaging recommendation:** web / app / support.
8. **Measurement plan and optimization event.**
9. **Budget / test plan and stop conditions.**
10. **What not to build yet.**
11. **Three changes that most increase purchase probability.**
12. **Three changes that most increase execution / result probability.**
13. **Any claims or evidence concerns.**
14. **Confidence level** for each major recommendation and what evidence would change their mind.

No reviewer should begin by rewriting copy. Reconstruct the business problem first.

# 23. Recommended reviewer roles

Run the same brief independently through:

### A. Direct-response performance marketer
Focus: creative → click → purchase.

### B. Consumer / behavioral psychologist
Focus: motivation, trust, risk, decision friction, adherence.

### C. Evidence-aware endurance coach / sports scientist
Focus: fit, claims, plan logic, athlete safety, outcome validity.

### D. Conversion UX / product designer
Focus: landing page, product usability, onboarding, app / web division.

### E. Meta media buyer / measurement specialist
Focus: campaign architecture, events, attribution, budget, learning.

### F. Growth economist / skeptical CFO
Focus: CAC, margin, LTV, pricing, scale conditions.

### G. Brand / editorial critic
Focus: whether the work still feels unmistakably FORM rather than generic DTC fitness.

The synthesis should prioritize recommendations that survive multiple roles.
