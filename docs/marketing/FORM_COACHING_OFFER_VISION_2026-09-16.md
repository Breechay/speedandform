## The realization

FORM has been treating the 8-week Run Development block as the primary public coaching door. Field evidence now shows a second, legitimate job that should not be forced into an 8-week relationship.

Some runners do not need FORM to own their training plan. They already have a triathlon plan, another coach, a run-club structure, or a training system they intend to keep. Their question is narrower:

> What is happening when I run, what matters, and what should I change?

That is not a discounted Run Development block. It is a different product.

The ecosystem therefore has two coaching jobs:

1. **FORM Analysis** — understand the running problem, identify the few priorities worth changing, practice them, and leave with something usable.
2. **Run Development** — develop the runner over time through assessment, individual planning, coaching, feedback and adjustment.

The two offers must be legible without competing with each other.

## What coaching is

> **Coaching is an external pair of eyes that knows what to look for and how to build a game plan. The quality comes down to how individualized that approach is.**

This is the simple public explanation. The deeper operating model has four parts.

### 1. External eyes

The athlete cannot fully observe themselves while they are inside the work. FORM brings perspective: movement, pacing, training load, execution and patterns across time. The coach's value is not merely seeing more data. It is knowing what deserves attention.

Do not turn this into a blanket claim that self-coached athletes fail or that FORM can predict injury. The useful distinction is perspective: athlete experience from inside the run plus an informed view from outside it.

### 2. The game plan

Observation without a decision is trivia. FORM converts what it sees into the next useful action: what to practice, what pace to hold, what volume to tolerate, when to progress, when to repeat and when to leave something alone.

A runner should know what they are doing and why it matters without having to become their own coach.

### 3. Individualized quality

Quality is not the number of workouts delivered. It is how well the prescription fits the person in front of FORM.

Individualization can include current fitness, movement, training history, available time, life constraints, response to recent work, athlete report, recovery and the actual goal. FORM does not change training merely to prove that it is customized. When the existing answer fits, keep it. When the athlete's response changes the problem, change the plan.

### 4. Data + feel

FORM uses numbers and athlete report together.

> **Data tells us what happened. How it felt tells us how you absorbed it.**

Pace, heart rate, distance, splits, volume and other measurements matter. So does the athlete's description of effort, restriction, control, confidence, soreness and what changed during the session. Neither source automatically overrules the other. Their relationship is often the useful signal.

`How did it feel?` is therefore not small talk. It is part of the coaching record.

## FORM is selective, not corrective by default

The name FORM does not mean forcing every runner toward a single visual ideal.

> **Not everything needs fixing. Some things just cost you more.**

`Cost` is broader than pace. Depending on the runner and context, something can cost unnecessary effort, tension, rhythm, control, repeatability, comfort or the ability to sustain the work. FORM does not declare a cost merely because software labels a segment non-ideal or movement differs from a textbook model. The athlete's report, observation, measurement and context establish what matters.

The operating sequence is:

**Observe → identify what matters → build the game plan → practice → retest → adjust.**

The goal is not perfect form. The goal is better running.

## Product architecture

### FORM Analysis

**Job:** `Understand your running.`

Best fit: a runner who has their own training structure or a specific concern and wants expert observation rather than a new plan.

Working service shape:
- running assessment in person when practical;
- video and movement review;
- coach observation and athlete report considered together;
- optional analysis software such as Ochy used as an instrument, never as the authority;
- identify the 1–3 priorities that matter now;
- specific cues and/or drills tied to those priorities;
- practice and retest when useful;
- concise take-home findings;
- optional bounded follow-up/retest if the product is ultimately designed as two contacts.

**Not the promise:** perfect form, a universal running model, a score of the athlete's worth, injury diagnosis, injury prevention, or a guarantee that every visible difference should be corrected.

Core doctrine:

> **Not everything needs fixing. Some things just cost you more.**

The desired felt result can be easier, smoother, more controlled or more capable running. Faster and farther may follow when relevant, but should not be guaranteed from a mechanics session.

### Run Development

**Job:** `Develop your running.`

Current public structure: **8 weeks · $1,200**.

Best fit: a runner who wants FORM to take responsibility for the development process rather than only inspect one problem.

The relationship can include:
- initial observation/assessment;
- individual running plan;
- mostly easy running used to practice cues and build consistency;
- appropriate threshold/speed work;
- endurance/long-run development;
- ongoing feedback and adjustment;
- race or capability goals where relevant.

The point is not to hand over workouts. The point is to make the athlete a better runner.

## Why both offers can coexist

They answer different questions.

| | FORM Analysis | Run Development |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Athlete question | What is happening when I run? | Can you develop my running? |
| Scope | Observe → prioritize → cue → retest | Assess → plan → coach → adjust → develop |
| Commitment | Bounded specialist encounter | 8-week coaching relationship |
| Plan ownership | Athlete may keep their existing plan/coach | FORM owns the running-development process |
| Output | Priorities and usable practice | Ongoing development and adjusted training |

Do not publish a ladder of one-, two-, three- and eight-session packages. That makes the offers compete on quantity of Brice's time instead of job-to-be-done.

## Pricing principle

**OPEN: exact FORM Analysis price and scope.**

Do not derive the price by dividing $1,200 by eight. The analysis is a concentrated specialist product containing observation, judgment, possible software/video review, recommendations and potentially follow-up work. Its per-contact price can therefore be higher than the implied per-week rate of the managed block.

Working research band for review only: **$250–$400**, with approximately **$325** a useful test anchor if the final product includes meaningful analysis and a take-home/retest component. This is not approved public pricing.

Before publishing a price, define:
1. session length;
2. what happens before and after the meeting;
3. whether one follow-up/retest is included;
4. what artifact the runner receives;
5. realistic coach time/cost to serve;
6. whether demand crowds out higher-value managed coaching.

## Analysis tools are instruments, not the product

Ochy or another analysis system may help Brice see timing, angles, movement segments and repeatable measurements. FORM must not outsource judgment to a software score.

Rules:
- never translate an app's aggregate score into `your form is X%`;
- do not assume every low-scored segment should be changed;
- preserve the athlete's felt report and training context;
- separate measured observation, software interpretation, coach interpretation and decision;
- use overlays selectively when they explain a decision better than prose;
- do not make medical claims from gait-analysis software;
- when pain is the presenting issue, FORM can observe running and training context but should not pretend to diagnose a medical cause.

The value is the coach deciding **what matters now**.

## Referral evidence and acquisition model

Repeated referrals from a House of Transformation trainer show a natural behavior: when someone says they need help with running, a trusted person sends them to FORM. Referred needs have included half-marathon preparation, leading a run club, improving running form and an Ironman 70.3 athlete who already has a broader training context but feels a specific asymmetry while running.

A second signal comes from Instagram: a non-client reported watching FORM athlete videos, trying movement changes himself, and feeling easier/more efficient at the same pace.

The acquisition system should amplify these existing behaviors rather than replace them with a generic advertisement.

### Three paths

**Direct recognition**  
content/ad → `that is me` → site → inquiry → appropriate door → assessment/client

**Referral recognition**  
content/ad → `I know who needs this` → send/share → runner → site → appropriate door

**Value first**  
content → learns something useful → tries/saves/follows → later inquiry or referral

The site must remain useful as something a trusted person can simply send.

## Three primary creative hypotheses

These are hypotheses to test, not cosmetic variants.

### Creative A — Recognition / Run Development

**Spine:**

> Run Development  
> Run better.

Speed, distance and endurance can appear through the footage, athlete need and later proof. Do not stack every possible benefit into the opening line.

Use real running and coaching footage. Controlled → sharper → sustained. Close on Run Development / individual running coaching / Miami / speedandform.com.

Primary job: make someone want ongoing development.

### Creative B — Coaching value / FORM Analysis

Open on a real athlete and a real observation. Show Brice observing; use one authentic cue or priority in context; show the runner practicing it. A brief analysis overlay/tool shot may appear if it clarifies the observation.

Primary job: give a tiny sample of FORM's judgment and make the narrower analysis product legible.

This should feel like useful FORM content that happens to be promoted, not a commercial pretending to be educational.

### Creative C — Referral native

Design around the transfer moment: the viewer recognizes a running problem in someone they know and wants to send the piece. Do not default to `share this with a runner` engagement bait or fake text-message theater.

Primary job: reproduce digitally the behavior already happening through trusted referrals.

The referral creative may route to a site surface where both doors are immediately legible:

> **FORM Analysis** — Understand your running.  
> **Run Development** — Develop your running.

## Website implication

Do not turn the homepage into a large service menu.

The site needs two clear coaching doors without weakening the flagship:

- FORM Analysis for the bounded question;
- Run Development for the development relationship.

The current Run Development offer remains the primary managed service. FORM Analysis should not be framed as `cheaper coaching` or a trial version of Run Development.

A visitor should be able to self-sort by need, not by counting sessions.

The complimentary first track assessment currently attached to Run Development must be reconciled carefully before FORM Analysis is published. A free assessment and a paid analysis cannot be allowed to look like the same service at different prices. Define the boundary explicitly before launch.

## Organic + paid + product loop

Paid creative creates recognition. Organic content demonstrates how FORM sees and coaches. The website explains which relationship fits. The encounter creates useful evidence. Coaching creates development and future referrals.

> **Content → recognition → encounter → judgment → development → evidence → referral → content**

The product should create the next acquisition evidence.

## Measurement

Track the full path, not only clicks:
- source / first known exposure;
- direct vs referred if known;
- creative family;
- site arrival;
- FORM Analysis inquiry;
- Run Development inquiry;
- qualified inquiry;
- assessment/analysis attended;
- paid conversion;
- cash collected;
- coach time/cost to serve;
- completion;
- downstream Run Development conversion where naturally appropriate;
- renewal/repeat analysis;
- referral outcome;
- saves/shares/sends where available.

Do not force an Analysis client into Run Development to improve an upsell metric. The smaller product is successful if it solves the bounded job well and remains economically worthwhile.

## Downstream rule

Any future agent changing the coaching funnel, homepage, Meta creative, intake, service pricing, gait/form analysis, coaching acquisition, coaching logic, athlete feedback or plan adjustment must read this document and the current creative brief first.

This realization is ecosystem-level: **FORM is an external pair of eyes plus an individualized game plan. It uses data and feel together, intervenes selectively, and offers different legitimate depths of help. The product boundary follows the athlete's job, not the number of sessions.**
