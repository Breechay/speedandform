# FORM Run Development · The Thread Across Distances

**Brice Ikouebe · November 6, 2026**  
**Status:** adopted coaching philosophy and cohort observation; race-family workout architecture is a working design, not an athlete assignment.  
**Audience:** FORM coaching authors, Method & Evidence, Labs, plan designers and editors.

## 1. One method, different race demands

> **Develop speed. Learn to sustain it. Carry it farther. Keep it available when the work gets difficult.**

This is the connecting thread of FORM Run Development. We develop more ability than the event immediately asks for, teach the athlete to inhabit a useful effort, then test whether that effort remains available after prior running, changing pace, and making decisions. The work is progressive: introduce an appropriate challenge, practice it, recover, and add the next layer when the athlete is ready.

**Train to win by following the plan.** The aim is an athlete with more available capacity, not a collection of exhausting sessions. Running form, the full muscular frame, speed, threshold, easy running, strength, recovery, and mental decision-making develop as one athlete. The same instruments appear across distances; their *weight, duration, placement, and consequence* change.

### The five questions, not five new training zones

1. **Available:** What faster movement or sustainable effort can the athlete actually produce today?
2. **Continuous:** How long can the athlete hold the useful effort without an escape?
3. **Repeatable:** Can the athlete return to it after reducing pace, and eventually with a float that does not provide complete recovery?
4. **Durable:** Can they still access the effort after more previous running or pressure?
5. **Expressed:** Does the athlete make good choices and use the ability in the event, then recover?

These are *coaching questions*, not a new state schema, score or calendar gate. They complement existing FORM athlete-state language and the race-season phases. An athlete may develop continuity before density or may need an easy step-back before another hard ask.

## 2. The governing progression levers

| Lever | What changes | What the coach watches |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed/effort** | Faster available movement, or the same output at less cost | Movement, pace source, effort and repeatability |
| **Continuity** | Longer uninterrupted useful work | Evenness, composure, fueling where relevant, recovery |
| **Recovery density** | Full easy reset → shorter reset → purposeful float | The float remains below work pace but does not provide full recovery |
| **Location** | Place specific work after more preceding running | Late pace availability without excessive deterioration |
| **Whole-week burden** | Rebalance easy running, threshold, speed, strength and long sessions | The next appropriate session is still absorbable |

**Progress one meaningful stressor purposefully.** Do not increase pace, volume, float demand and late placement together merely because a week passed. The athlete's execution and subsequent training, not soreness, establish readiness. Floats are work and must be authored as their own segments; they are not invisible recovery.

### The base is not the warm-up

A quality session that totals about 40 minutes including warm-up, work and cooldown does not replace independent easy-running duration. For a prepared half-marathon group, consider gradually building **standalone 50–60 → 60–75-minute continuous easy** runs, supported by a **75–90-minute absorbable easy long run**; the marathon-specific long-run platform remains two hours comfortably. Short recovery days can and should be shorter. The purpose is to make longer aerobic work feel familiar without making it fast. Specific intensity is an occasional withdrawal from that foundation.

### Two separate ways to develop pace durability

**Continuity:** short sustained work → longer uninterrupted race-pace work → purposeful late placement. **Density:** genuinely easy reset → shorter genuinely easy reset → a separately authored float that reduces effort without fully recovering. The two are related but not identical. Running for 60 minutes with 30 minutes at MP proves an alternating **30-minute cumulative dose**, not an hour continuously at MP.

Continuous `1 mi at race pace / 1 mi easy` alternation is **not a classic mile-repeat workout**. It is one uninterrupted aerobic run with explicit work and easy segments, no stopped recovery, and real total-run cost. Preserve the existing ban on stopped mile-repeat sets and 3 × 10-minute running intervals. The [Aerobic Base to Race-Pace Durability Bridge](FORM_AEROBIC_BASE_RACE_PACE_DURABILITY_BRIDGE_20261106.md) specifies the full half and marathon progression.

## 3. Brice's half-marathon threshold observation

**Adopted field observation, November 6:** Among Brice's trained, sub-1:30 half-marathon group, stable threshold work has often been approximately **20–30 seconds per mile faster than demonstrated half-marathon capability pace**. Earlier FORM observations described roughly a 30-second-per-mile difference in selected runners. This is firsthand coaching evidence, not an externally validated universal equation or a guaranteed race prediction.

**Example for reasoning, not automatic prescription:** If demonstrated HMP capability is 6:50/mi, the observed relationship suggests investigating a threshold working region of approximately **6:20–6:30/mi**. The athlete must earn the band through actual steady execution, breathing/effort, repeatability and recovery. The exact threshold construct remains a sustainable LT2/MLSS-type coaching relationship, not simply race pace minus a fixed number. The difference may change with fitness, heat, fatigue, race execution, and which runner is being measured.

Keep **stable training threshold**, **demonstrated half-marathon race capability**, **aspirational race goal** and **field observation** as different data. Do not calculate an athlete's threshold solely from an ambitious sub-1:30 goal. A runner can have a strong ceiling yet still lack race-specific continuity or late access. That gap is a coaching question, not proof the threshold test or the race was wrong.

## 4. The same instruments, differently weighted

| Objective | Principal development | Specific progression question | Long run / other work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5K** | Available speed and aerobic power, supported by threshold | Can fast movement be repeated with control and, later, less complete recovery? | Enough aerobic support to carry speed; no compulsory large late-race long-run test |
| **10K** | Threshold plus 10K-pace continuity | Can a strong pace stay even as continuous duration or float density increases? | Endurance and occasional later specific work, at lower cost than the half/full models |
| **Half marathon** | Stable threshold ceiling, HMP capability, continuity and durability | Can HMP be sustained, regained after less-than-complete recovery, and accessed after preceding duration? | Some meaningful specific long runs, with threshold reduced when Saturday is costly |
| **Marathon** | Two-hour comfortable foundation, MP continuity, fueling and late access | Can MP remain after more prior work and float transitions? | The long run becomes principal architecture; 60–70 continuous MP before advanced alternation; cumulative MP floor is 30 min |
| **HYROX** | Clean running capacity plus bounded station-exit skill | Can the athlete resume the planned running effort after known station cost? | Existing external strength/station work counts in the same stress budget |
| **Stay Fit** | Broad athletic capability, movement fluency and reliable practice | What meaningful improvement fits the athlete's life without a race countdown? | Enjoyable, recoverable variety with no invented race-pace gates |

All objectives include easy running, appropriate strength and glute development, mobility where useful, and recovery. More training days distribute support; they do not automatically authorize extra hard sessions. Pace is grounded in actual current ability, not simply race aspiration.

## 5. The session-authoring grammar

Each session must have exactly the job it claims. The coach selects and sequences from these families:

1. **Reveal speed:** short fast movement with adequate recoveries, technique and appropriate cost.
2. **Inhabit an effort:** controlled continuous threshold or race-specific work, at a meaningful duration.
3. **Return after a reset:** substantial repetitions with deliberate easy jogging; then shorter resets, then a float when justified.
4. **Move it deeper:** put the same meaningful work after more duration or steady pressure, without manufacturing a maximal race simulation.
5. **Protect the next ask:** easy, supportive, step-back and taper work, including selected speed maintenance.

The **purpose, dose and recovery cost** of every family are recorded. A session can be a *build*, *test*, *rehearsal* or *protection*. A test's informational value does not automatically make it good development work. A fast finish is a hard stimulus and must replace, not secretly supplement, another load. The athlete's own report is part of the evidence.

### Explicit exclusions

- No future **3 × 10-minute running intervals**; this ban does not remove allowed 2 × 10-minute work or recorded historical sessions.
- No future **classic mile-repeat workouts with full interval reset**. A single continuous mile, a mile split, or a **continuous pace/easy alternating mile** inside one non-stopped run remains legitimate; the alternating workout must use separate typed work and easy segments.
- No universal numerical conversion from HMP to threshold; use the cohort observation as a calibration question.
- No compulsory 12-mile continuous HMP ownership test. The existing reusable 5 → 6 → 8-mile HMP asks are context-specific, with later pace work a separate durability question.
- No marathon-pace-labeled session below **30 cumulative MP minutes**; this rule is for marathon-specific work, not HMP, 10K, 5K or non-MP support.
- No elite-only 3 × 10K MP assignment by template.
- No fixed float pace copied from another athlete. A float is below the work effort, above a complete reset, and individually authored.

## 6. A first race-family design sequence

**5K:** first reveal controlled fast movement, then extend repeatability, later compress recovery with a purposeful float if the athlete can still execute. Threshold and easy running support that work; avoid turning every speed session into a maximal race. See the [5K and 10K Session Canon](FORM_5K_10K_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md) for concrete session families.

**10K:** first establish controlled threshold/10K pace, then sustain longer work, then re-enter race-specific pace after a shorter reset or float, with selected later work that remains recoverable. The same [5K and 10K Session Canon](FORM_5K_10K_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md) gives separately graded threshold, pace and float work.

**Half:** establish repeatable threshold and a 75–90-minute aerobic long-run platform, with standalone easy runs growing beyond ordinary short workout duration; use 5/5 or one-mile/easy alternations when needed, build substantial continuous HMP, consider a high-value six-mile continuous check (eight miles selectively), then progress recovery density and place HMP after prior running. The [Half Marathon Session Canon](FORM_HALF_MARATHON_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md) defines working families. The threshold observation helps calibrate the ceiling; it is not a pace prescription.

**Full:** establish two hours comfortably, use substantive 5/5 MP/easy or one-mile/easy alternations as a bridge when needed, build continuous MP through 30–45 → 45–55 → 60–70 minutes (or use comparable recovered continuous race evidence), then select advanced 3 × 5K with shorter resets/floats or the separate Thirty-Thirty long; progressively preserve pace under more prior work. The [Marathon Session Canon](FORM_MARATHON_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md) remains the owning detailed source.

### Six-rep blended specific work and the final meaningful test

A newer FORM candidate family uses **six longer work repetitions** and five 1 km easy transitions, with controlled later progression from marathon toward half pace. The *working interpretation* is three alternative tiers—6 × 2 km, 6 × 3 km or 6 × 4 km—not one enormous compound workout. Six Fours with its easy transitions already totals 29 km before preparation/cooldown and is **exceptional/elite**. The half variant scales to shorter repetitions and selectively HMP → 10K → 5K effort. Workload, pace progression and float density are separate axes; final reps must be well controlled and the week recovered. [Six-Rep Ladder & Final Rehearsal](FORM_SIX_REP_DISTANCE_LADDER_AND_FINAL_REHEARSAL_20261106.md).

The proposed **90–100-minute continuous MP** workout is a potential **final major rehearsal**, not a universal pass/fail gate or the last literal workout. It must be reconciled with the frozen Donna **Closing-10K** experiment before scheduling. The related half-marathon check is usually a **40–60-minute continuous HMP** exposure or selected late HMP miles, not a near-complete race in training. [Donna First Official Plan Design Brief](FORM_DONNA_FIRST_OFFICIAL_MARATHON_PLAN_DESIGN_BRIEF_20261106.md) records the sequencing decision still to make.

## 7. Evidence and staff rules

For a new workout, capture: athlete and objective; phase; actual pace source; current limiter; session family and job; complete warm-up/work/recoveries/cooldown; exact pace/effort, total volume and weekly burden; fueling where relevant; athlete response; next-session cost; version and author. For progressive floats, store each recovery pace/duration and distinguish planned and filed values. Keep coach inference separate from athlete words and measurements.

The current published athlete occurrence and applicable medical restriction override this working canon. Old studies and completed sessions remain unchanged even where a historical example is now banned for future prescription. No agent may silently generate a harder family from a title, an old archived sample, or a race goal.

### Active language prohibition · adopted November 6

Brice has banned the word **“rhythm”** and its plural from future FORM programming language. Use **pace, effort, timing, continuity, control, coordination, cadence, or settle** when that is the accurate concept. Apply the prohibition to athlete-facing session titles, intentions, details, Week copy and to new coaching/philosophy copy. Do not mechanically change historical verbatim athlete quotes or completed workout evidence. A separate versioned editorial migration is required for published legacy copy; this document does not claim that migration is done.
