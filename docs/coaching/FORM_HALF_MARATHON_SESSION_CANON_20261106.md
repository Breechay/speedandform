# FORM Half Marathon · Session Canon · Working Edition 01

**Brice Ikouebe · November 6, 2026**  
**Status:** adopted principles and firsthand threshold observation; example session families are working designs for coach selection  
**Audience:** FORM coaches and Labs, not a publishable athlete plan.

## 1. The Half question

**Raise the ceiling. Inhabit the pace. Carry it farther.**

Half-marathon development uses much of the same equipment as a marathon build: easy running, meaningful threshold, faster-running reserve, specific-pace continuity, selected long runs, and deliberate recovery. The event changes how these tools are weighted. We do not prescribe a marathon's 60–70-minute MP gate to a half-marathon athlete, nor turn every half long run into a race simulation.

The progression begins with a credible threshold effort and a tolerable aerobic long run; it extends continuous half-marathon pace (HMP), then reduces recovery or positions HMP after prior work. The question is whether the athlete becomes able to **stay at pace and return to it without a complete reset**, then carry it into the later race. Each harder layer must be absorbed.

**Easy-running capacity is separate from the quality session.** A 40-minute workout that includes warm-up and cooldown is not evidence that the athlete owns 60–75 minutes of continuous easy running. As appropriate to history and readiness, develop ordinary 50–60 → 60–75-minute aerobic runs alongside the longer easy weekend platform. Shorter recovery runs remain allowed.

## 2. Brice's observed threshold relationship

For the trained **sub-1:30 half-marathon group**, Brice has repeatedly observed stable threshold training approximately **20–30 sec/mi faster than demonstrated half-marathon capability pace**. The earlier field description was often about 30 sec/mi in selected runners. Example: demonstrated HMP 6:50/mi might suggest investigating 6:20–6:30/mi as a controlled threshold training region.

**Evidence classification:** firsthand cohort observation adopted as a FORM calibration question, **not** a universal physiological formula, guaranteed race predictor or permission to derive a band from an aspirational goal. Compare actual sustainable threshold execution, breathing/effort, pace stability, repetition, conditions and recovery with actual half results. Preserve source dates, athlete distinctions and deviations from the observation. Threshold is LT2/MLSS-type sustainable high-end aerobic training; the race goal is a different construct.

## 3. Entry and selection

- Establish current threshold work that can be controlled for approximately 20–30 cumulative minutes, with individual adjustment.
- Establish recent, absorbable **75–90 minutes of easy long running**, appropriate to the athlete's history.
- Build enough **standalone 60–75-minute genuinely easy running** that the athlete's endurance does not depend on the next hard workout or a single exhausting long run. This is a developed capability, not a minimum for every easy day.
- Confirm a genuine HMP capability from running and racing evidence rather than converting an ambition into a pace band.
- Preserve the primary weekly question. When a specific Saturday long run carries the stress, Thursday may be easy, rest, or a smaller maintenance session. Glute/strength and other work spend the same recovery budget.
- No mandatory 12-mile continuous HMP pass. Existing reusable 5 → 6 → 8-mile continuous HMP asks may be used when individually justified; an athlete can later practice HMP after prior running rather than pushing uninterrupted distance indefinitely.
- A **6-mile continuous HMP check** can be a valuable race-pace durability question; **8 miles is selective and high-cost**, chosen when it would change a coaching decision. Neither requires recurring maximal simulation. A 5–6-mile pass plus specific later running can be a better balance for some athletes.
- The **30-minute MP minimum belongs to marathon** sessions; it does not automatically ban appropriately small HMP sharpening when its purpose is explicit. Likewise, a half athlete is not required to complete 60–70 minutes continuous HMP before any broken work.

## 4. Favorite session families

**Examples only.** Each requires an athlete-specific full warm-up, work, recovery, cooldown, actual effort/pace, total duration or distance, and compatible surrounding week before publication.

### H01 · The Threshold Hold

**Working sequence:** `20 min continuous threshold → 25–30 min continuous threshold`. A longer `35–40 min` is an advanced optional exposure, never a default progression. A permitted `2 × 10 min` can be a preparatory alternative for the athlete whose continuity is still developing; **3 × 10 min is excluded**.  
**Job:** Develop stable faster-than-HMP aerobic capacity without racing the workout.  
**Read:** Evenness, reported effort, session cost, 24–72h recovery and next useful work.  
**Pace:** The cohort's observed 20–30 sec/mi gap is a hypothesis to check, not an automatic target.

### H02 · Continuous Half Pace

**Working sequence:** `30–40 min continuous HMP → 40–50 min` when earned, within an appropriate session. Longer continuous HMP forms, including the current 5/6/8-mile reusable questions, remain individually selected and do not form a universal checklist.  
**Job:** Teach sustained half pace and independent pacing decisions before adding incomplete recovery or heavy preceding load.  
**Read:** Pace drift, effort, composure and recovery.

### H03 · Two Holds · Easy Reset to Float

**Base:** `2 × 20 min @ HMP`, initially with a genuine easy-jog recovery prescribed by the coach.  
**Density progression:** (A) full easy reset → (B) shorter easy reset, same HMP work → (C) a purposeful **float** between the 20-minute segments, slower than HMP but still aerobic work, without full recovery.  
**Job:** Re-enter and sustain HMP with progressively less relief.  
**Read:** Segment stability, exact float pace/duration, second-segment cost, next-session readiness.  
**Guardrail:** Floats are not a disguised increase in HMP speed; change recovery density independently and only after the previous form is absorbable.

### H04 · Twenty-Twenty · Alternating Half Long

**Base:** `20 min easy → 20 min HMP → 15 min easy/steady transition → 20 min HMP → 10 min easy` (85 minutes total; 40 minutes HMP). The 15-minute middle segment begins genuinely easy; any future **float** version must be separately named and authored as a more demanding session.  
**Job:** Find half pace again after leaving it, without turning the ending into a reckless finish.  
**Read:** Comparison of the two HMP segments, transition effort, later recovery.  
**Selection:** Advanced and only after the athlete demonstrates substantial continuous HMP and an appropriate aerobic base.

### H05 · Pressure, Then Half Pace

**Example:** `20 min easy + 20–30 min steady below threshold + 30 min continuous HMP + cooldown`.  
**Job:** Preserve HMP after controlled prior work. Steady pressure is not a threshold test before an exhausted HMP tail.  
**Read:** HMP after prior cost, relative strain and next-day recovery.  
**Selection:** One major quality session for that week, replacing ordinary threshold if necessary; long run remains easy support.

### H06 · Half Long · Move Pace Deeper

**Example:** `50–65 min easy + 30–35 min HMP`, with total duration chosen from actual long-run tolerance. An alternative uses `45–55 min easy + 20 min steady + 25–30 min HMP` when that specific question is justified.  
**Job:** Keep useful half pace accessible later, not simply cover the longest possible distance.  
**Read:** Late output, cost, movement observations only if observed, fueling as appropriate and subsequent training.  
**Selection:** A principal hard long run replacing a second quality stimulus. Not every week.

### H07 · Late Five-Kilometer Check

**Concept:** An individually authored late 5K near current HMP after an aerobic approach, used as a bounded read or sharpening session rather than the program's main development dose.  
**Read:** Pace judgment, ability to settle at the intended effort and recovery.  
**Status:** Coach-selectable, not a race-readiness guarantee. Do not turn a controlled read into a maximal time trial.

### H08 · Faster, Then Settle · Exploring

**Concept:** A short, appropriately faster effort followed by a longer sustainable HMP section, testing the ability to reduce intensity and establish a sustainable effort.  
**Status:** Exploring. Brice must explicitly author pace, duration, transition and whole-week cost before use. Do not invent it from a title.

### H09 · Five/Five · The Accessible Half-Pace Bridge

**Base:** `5 min evidenced HMP → 5 min genuinely easy × 6`, run continuously without stopping: **30 min at HMP + 30 min easy**. Add separately authored warm-up and cooldown when needed.  
**Job:** Introduce substantive HMP in a low-complexity pattern while preserving an easy descent between efforts. This is a bridge, **not evidence of 30 continuous minutes at HMP**.  
**Progress:** Lengthen a selected HMP section, reduce the easy transition after stable execution, or move toward H02/H03. Avoid advancing all levers together.

### H10 · One-Mile Pace / One-Mile Easy · Continuous Alternation

**Base:** Alternate `1 mi at evidenced HMP / 1 mi genuinely easy` for **4–5 work miles**, in **8–10 miles of continuous running** before any extra warm-up/cooldown; scale the complete dose to current capacity.  
**Job:** Teach repeatable pace access inside a meaningful aerobic run, with no stopped reset. It is **not a mile-repeat workout**: every mile is a separate consecutive **work** or **easy** segment; the run continues between them.  
**Progress:** Prefer longer HMP holds, for example `2 mi HMP / 1 mi easy × 2–3`, then `3 mi HMP / 0.5–1 mi easy / 3 mi HMP`, as appropriate. The quality question moves toward H02 and a selective 5–6-mile continuous pass rather than simply adding more alternating miles.

### H11 · Six-to-Eight-Mile Continuous Ownership · Selective

**Form:** `5 mi continuous HMP → 6 mi continuous HMP → up to 8 mi continuous HMP` **only as individually earned**. At approximately 6:50/mi, six miles is around 41 minutes; eight is around 55.  
**Job:** Confirm uninterrupted race-pace durability—not total HMP accumulated through intermittent work.  
**Selection:** One or two highly informative checks within a block, not weekly proof attempts and not a condition everyone must pass. Account for 48–72-hour recovery, reduce other quality, and sometimes prefer late-run HMP work instead of extending the uninterrupted check.

### H12 · Five/Five Float · Separate Higher-Density Variant

**Form:** `5 min evidenced HMP / 5 min coach-defined float × 6`, with deliberate easy cooldown. This retains **30 min HMP** and adds **30 min of slower but still working effort** instead of full easy transitions.  
**Prerequisite:** Stable, absorbed H09 easy alternations and an individualized float definition.  
**Job:** Practice pace changes without complete recovery. The float is *not* interchangeable with easy and is not required before the athlete progresses continuous HMP. A longer uninterrupted HMP section with easy recovery can be the wiser next step.

## 5. Which progression do we choose?

| Question | First exposure | Next layer | Later demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is threshold stable? | H01 at sufficient easy-to-recover dose | Longer continuity if appropriate | Maintain while HMP becomes principal |
| Can HMP be held? | H02 continuous | Longer HMP without pace chasing | H03 re-entry with shorter easy reset |
| Is the first specific HMP dose accessible? | H09 five/five with easy transitions | H10 one-mile pace/easy if aerobic base allows | Fewer, longer HMP holds |
| Can HMP be regained? | H03-A easy jog | H03-B reduced recovery | H03-C purposeful float |
| Is HMP continuous for a substantial distance? | H02 and 5-mile check | H11 selective six-mile check | Eight only if the additional evidence warrants its cost |
| Is HMP durable late? | Easy long-run platform | H05 pressure before pace | H06 specific long-run or H04 alternating |
| Is the capability retained? | Bounded late check | Absorb and sharpen | Race execution |

This is a **decision map, not a calendar**. H04, H05 and H06 are alternatives in a selected high-cost week, not three additional workouts to stack. Fewer running days means even more need to protect the principal ask. The coach may repeat or step back before extending.

It may eliminate the need to re-test 60–70 minutes continuously at that same pace, **after recovery and verification**; it cannot establish late-marathon availability, fueling after two hours, or the final 10K. ## 6. How Half differs from 5K, 10K and Full

- **5K:** More attention to available speed and repeatability; threshold supplies support.
- **10K:** Threshold and 10K-pace continuity share emphasis; floats can be valuable after controlled repeated efforts, but late-long burden is smaller.
- **Half:** Threshold ceiling and HMP durability share the work; medium-long specific sessions and selected late HMP long runs answer the decisive question.
- **Full:** The long run takes more of the weekly stress budget, fueling is mandatory in specific long runs, and the 60–70-minute continuous MP gateway precedes the advanced Three Fives / Thirty-Thirty choices.

## 7. Evidence, language and publication

Each filing should preserve actual work duration, pace and recoveries; planned vs completed; effort and athlete words; relevant conditions; optional HR when interpretable; fueling where relevant; and 24–72h recovery after major asks. Record competing explanations for a late fade. A runner doing faster threshold work does not, by that fact alone, own the corresponding half race outcome.

The FORM language prohibition applies here and in all new session titles, intentions, details and Week copy. Prefer clear terms such as **pace, timing, continuity, control, cadence, settle** and avoid the banned term recorded in the shared vocabulary rule. Historical completed sessions are evidence, not material to silently rewrite. Current published athlete versions remain authoritative until separately revised and verified.
