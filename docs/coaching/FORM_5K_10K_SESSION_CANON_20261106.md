# FORM · 5K and 10K Session Canon

**Brice Ikouebe · November 6, 2026 · Working design**  
**Authority:** Cross-Distance Development Thread and Method Bible. This file is a prospective coaching toolkit, not a live athlete plan or a fixed weekly calendar.

## The connection

Both distances develop a higher speed ceiling and sustainable aerobic control. They differ in what must remain available at race demand: the 5K benefits from faster controlled movement and repeatability; the 10K asks for greater continuous race-pace ownership supported by threshold. The same progression levers apply:

1. Establish a real current pace or effort from racing and completed running, not from a wishful goal.
2. Own the work with ordinary easy recovery and good execution.
3. Extend continuous time or substantial work duration before simply adding repetition count.
4. After reliable recovery, shorten the reset; advance to an individually prescribed float only where it serves a clear race question.
5. Later, place relevant work after preceding running or moderate steady pressure, while keeping the week recoverable.

**The workout's job must be visible in the actual work.** All durations and counts below are *candidate architectures* to personalize, not automatic standards. Favor useful sustained sessions over decorative complexity. A faster lap does not rescue a session the athlete cannot absorb.

## 5K family

### F01 · Speed reserve — 8–10 × 200 m relaxed fast

- **Question:** Can the athlete move clearly faster than 5K pace with relaxed, accurate mechanics?
- **Draft architecture:** comprehensive warm-up and drills; 8–10 × 200 m at an individually demonstrated fast-but-relaxed pace, with generous walking/easy-jog reset; cooldown.
- **Progress:** first improve control, then repeat good mechanics; do not rush to a float here. This is an economical speed-skill session, not a disguised maximal effort.

### F02 · The controlled threshold hold — 20 → 25 → 30 min continuous

- **Question:** Can a runner inhabit an aerobically demanding but sustainable pace for longer, with normal next-day recovery?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; 20 minutes continuous threshold at evidenced controlled effort; cooldown. Later 25 or 30 minutes *if absorbed*.
- **Progress:** extend uninterrupted work or lower perceived cost, not pace and duration at once. A bounded **2 × 10-minute** introduction remains legitimate if continuity is not ready; it is not the long-term endpoint. Do not use **3 × 10-minute** intervals.

### F03 · Five-kilometer pace access — 5 × 800 m

- **Question:** Can a runner repeatedly access their demonstrated 5K effort, then return to it cleanly?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **5 × 800 m at evidenced 5K pace** with 2–3 minutes truly easy jog, individualized; cooldown. Four kilometers specific work is substantial.
- **Progress:** stable paced reps with even movement → somewhat shorter jog → controlled float only in an advanced, separately named version. Do not raise target pace while shrinking recovery.

### F04 · Longer race-pace ownership — 2 × 2 km

- **Question:** Can the runner hold more specific pace per segment with fewer interruptions?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **2 × 2 km at evidenced 5K pace** with a generous reset; cooldown. This is an advanced specific workout, not an entry session or required benchmark.
- **Progress:** greater execution quality, more consistent pacing and lower perceived cost before reducing reset. The athlete's individual response determines whether F03 or F04 offers the better stimulus.

### F05 · Steady then fast — 20–30 min steady + 3 × 600 m

- **Question:** Does useful 5K speed remain after preceding aerobic pressure?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **20–30 minutes controlled steady** below threshold; then **3 × 600 m around evidenced 5K effort** with easy resets; cooldown.
- **Progress:** first perform both parts without drift, then vary steady duration or reset density. This spends a main quality slot; it is not extra speed added to an already hard week.

## 10K family

### T01 · Continuous threshold ownership — 25 → 30 → 35 min

- **Question:** Can threshold remain controlled for meaningful uninterrupted time?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **25–30 minutes continuous threshold** (35 only when supported), at an individually evidenced sustainable pace; cooldown.
- **Progress:** duration, perceived ease and next-day readiness before faster pace. Use as a threshold session, not as a 10K-pace trial mislabeled threshold.

### T02 · Two-kilometer pace blocks — 3 × 2 km

- **Question:** Can the athlete repeat substantial 10K-specific work without surging?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **3 × 2 km at evidenced 10K pace**, with 2–3 minutes easy jog between; cooldown. Six kilometers of specific work requires honest recovery.
- **Progress:** even execution → shorter reset → measured float, with all recovery segments explicitly authored. The float is slower than 10K work but remains a load.

### T03 · Sustained 10K ownership — 2 × 3 km

- **Question:** Does fewer, longer race-pace blocks strengthen continuity?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **2 × 3 km at evidenced 10K pace** with a genuine easy-jog interval; cooldown.
- **Progress:** first make both blocks controlled and repeatable. Only then consider reducing the interval or moving the second block later in the run. Not every athlete needs both T02 and T03 in one build.

### T04 · Threshold into 10K expression — 20 min threshold + 3 × 1 km

- **Question:** Can the athlete use 10K pace after meaningful prior aerobic work?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **20 minutes controlled threshold**, then a real easy transition; **3 × 1 km at evidenced 10K pace** with easy jog; cooldown.
- **Progress:** the quality and stability of the second part matters more than impressive first-part speed. This is a high-cost option, not a routine second workout after another hard threshold day.

### T05 · Float re-entry — 2 km / 800 m float / 2 km

- **Question:** Can the athlete change down in pace without fully recovering, then re-enter 10K pace?
- **Draft architecture:** warm-up; **2 km at evidenced 10K pace / 800 m individually prescribed float / 2 km at evidenced 10K pace**, then easy cooldown. That is **4 km at 10K pace with one 800 m float**; the float is a working bridge, not an extra tail after the final repeat.
- **Prerequisite:** strong T02 or T03 performance, stable continuous threshold and recovered subsequent sessions. This is not a default early-block progression.
- **Progress:** control float duration/intensity separately from work-pace volume. Never invent a universal float speed or treat it as full recovery.

### T06 · Late 10K pace availability — 45–60 min easy + 15–20 min race effort

- **Question:** Is race pace accessible after earlier running, without turning the whole long run into a maximal simulation?
- **Draft architecture:** **45–60 minutes absorbable easy**, then **15–20 minutes at evidenced 10K effort** only for an athlete with the capacity for that sustained late effort, then cooldown. This is an advanced, consequential workout.
- **Progress:** first protect mechanics, fuel/conditions as relevant, and the next week. If the final block degrades sharply, the result informs a smaller future dose instead of validating an automatic progression.

## Choosing within a training week

| Training purpose | 5K candidate | 10K candidate | Whole-week rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Develop faster available movement | F01 | Occasional F01 as support | Keep mechanics clear; do not add exhaustion to gain speed exposure |
| Build sustainable aerobic work | F02 | T01 | Respect the observed threshold effort and recoverability |
| Own event pace | F03 then possibly F04 | T02 then possibly T03 | One substantial specific session can be sufficient |
| Express pace after prior demand | F05 | T04 or T06 | Substitutes for another hard workout, not an addition |
| Reduce dependence on full recovery | advanced F03 variation | T05 after T02/T03 | Float is authored and counted as work |

## Controls and what this does not mean

- These families are not a race forecast or a fixed linear ladder. Athletes may repeat an earlier family, skip a family, or step back. A race, hard lift, HYROX or long run can own the week's main quality budget.
- No **mile repeats** and no **3 × 10-minute running intervals** in new FORM programming. Historical filings stay factual. Two × 10-minute threshold remains an available introductory tool.
- Brice's 20–30-seconds-per-mile threshold/HMP difference is a field observation about a trained sub-1:30 half-marathon group. **Do not transfer that arithmetic to 5K or 10K race pace.** Each athlete's current relevant evidence governs pace.
- No unmeasured float, hidden quality minutes, invented recovery ability, forced continuous race-pace test, or race-pace work stacked on an already excessive week.
- Before any session goes live, complete structured work and recovery, exact total dose, purpose, warm-up/cooldown, athlete-specific pace source, date/version, and coach review. Neither this canon nor the rule index publishes an athlete plan.
