# FORM · Aerobic Base to Race-Pace Durability

**Brice Ikouebe · November 6, 2026 · Method addendum and working session architecture**  
**Status:** owner-observed and coach-designed progression; no athlete prescription or published workout is changed by this document.

## The central connection

**Easy running supplies time and recoverable endurance. Threshold develops sustainable pace above race demand. Race-pace work extends continuity and repeatability. Long runs test whether pace is still available after accumulated running. Racing reveals which parts transfer.**

Five coaching questions stay distinct:

1. **Aerobic range:** Can the athlete run *comfortably* for longer than an ordinary workout session lasts?
2. **Pace availability:** Can they reach an evidenced race pace without forcing it?
3. **Pace ownership:** Can they stay there continuously for a substantial segment?
4. **Incomplete reset:** Can they return to it after a shorter easy segment and, later, an explicitly prescribed float?
5. **Durability:** Can they still do that after previous running, fuel it, recover, and use it late in the target event?

These are capabilities, not five zones or a mandatory five-week sequence. **The pace stays; the escapes gradually disappear.** The athlete should increasingly own the same useful pace, not chase a faster watch number every time.

## The overlooked base: independent easy-running duration

If a typical quality workout occupies **about 40 minutes total including warm-up and cooldown**, that elapsed time is not itself evidence of a substantial easy-running base. The athlete also needs standalone aerobic running that continues beyond the ordinary workout envelope.

| Running role | Illustrative duration for a prepared sub-1:30 half group | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short recovery / low-cost support | 25–40 min genuinely easy | Frequency, circulation, recovery; allowed to be shorter than workouts |
| Ordinary easy aerobic run | Build 40–50 → 50–60 → **60–75 min continuous easy** as earned | Comfortable uninterrupted base beyond many quality sessions |
| Half-marathon aerobic long run | Build toward **75–90+ min easy** when absorbed | Time-on-feet support before specific HMP durability work |
| Marathon preparation long run | Build toward **at least 2h comfortably easy** before marathon-specific long-run work | Fueling practice and the marathon long-run entry platform |

These are **coaching design bands**, not universal minimums for every athlete or every easy day. The athlete's previous weeks, injury/rebuild status, available days, conditions, pace and 24–72-hour response govern progression. Do not infer that a 50-minute easy run is safe because someone did a 50-minute interval workout. Easy running is not speed, threshold, or a hidden progression run; protect its low cost. The base earns the right to spend quality on a few decisive sessions, not permission to make every run harder.

## The two progression axes

**Axis A — uninterrupted pace ownership:** short continuous work → longer continuous work → meaningful sustained work after prior easy duration. This is the only axis that directly proves uninterrupted control at race pace.

**Axis B — recovery density:** full easy transition → shorter easy transition → coach-defined float that **reduces pace without fully recovering**. This tests pace re-entry under sustained demand. It can be developed before, during or after Axis A as appropriate, but does not replace evidence of continuous ownership.

Also progress **placement** (how late the pace occurs) and **whole-week absorption**. Increase only one or two meaningful stressors at a time; account for threshold, faster speed, strength, events and life load. Substantial race-pace long runs replace another hard session and earn a lighter surrounding week.

### A continuous alternation is not a banned mile-repeat workout

**Allowed:** a continuous run such as `1 mi at evidenced MP → 1 mi easy`, alternating without stopping or walking for full recovery. It is a *pace/easy running pattern* with independently filed work and easy segments, not classic repeated-mile intervals at a hard track effort. The mile is a useful distance unit here, not the training identity.

**Still prohibited:** classic mile repeats and 3 × 10-minute running intervals in future FORM authoring. Separate the two concepts in code, session title, and coach language. Do not disguise classic repetitions by changing a label. For the continuous pattern, explicitly record each mile as a separate consecutive segment with its correct role, distance, pace and recovery character; do not encode a single repeat-counted hard mile with full rests.

## Marathon: from access to one hour at pace

**Foundation:** the athlete already owns a comfortable, absorbable two-hour aerobic long run and substantive easy running during the week. MP must be supported by evidence; all workouts called marathon-pace-specific contain **at least 30 cumulative minutes actually assigned at MP**. Keep warm-up, cooldown and fueling in the full authored dose.

| Capability question | Working example | Why it belongs | Selection boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learn the change in gears | **5 min MP / 5 min genuinely easy × 6** = 30 min MP + 30 easy | 60-min continuous run with 30 min of specific work | The very first specific exposure, if easy base already supports it |
| Make pace/easy alternation tangible | **1 mi MP / 1 mi easy × 5** = 5 mi MP within 10 mi continuous, roughly 34 min MP at 6:50/mi | Repeated access with no stops | A substantial run; not the first workout for an underprepared athlete |
| Reduce the escapes | **2 mi MP / 1 mi easy × 3** = 6 mi MP + 3 mi easy | Longer uninterrupted MP segments | Own preceding exposure; account for total run |
| Lengthen the holds | **3 mi MP / 1 mi easy × 2** = 6 mi MP + 2 mi easy | Fewer resets, same major pace exposure | Not a required extra week; choose according to response |
| Prove continuity | **30–45 → 45–55 → 60–70 min continuous MP** | Direct uninterrupted ownership | Each layer repeated or absorbed as needed; do not advance by calendar |
| Add advanced late specificity | **3 × 5K MP**, or **2h30 Thirty-Thirty** with 60 total MP min | Work after a substantial prior cost or repeated access | After 60–70 continuous capability *or* equivalent strong, current race evidence, plus recovery/fueling/readiness review |

**The order is a map, not mandatory Saturday-by-Saturday scheduling.** Faster marathoners might need five 1-mile MP sections to meet 30 minutes; at slower MP the count can differ. Prescribe by actual MP **time** and full-session cost. Do not auto-assign the 10-mile alternating run solely because a pace label is available. A more economical 5/5 session can be first; continuity may then grow directly without every intermediate family.

### The five-minute float alternative

After `5 MP / 5 easy × 6` is stable and the athlete is absorbing it, a distinct session may use **`5 min MP / 5 min coach-defined float × 6`**. That is 30 min at MP plus 30 min sustained slower working effort, **not** 30 minutes of recovery. It is a meaningful intensity-density increase and may cost more than the same duration with easy segments. It is an **option**, not the default bridge to 60–70 continuous MP. Alternatives shorten the easy transitions or lengthen the MP sections while keeping the number of stress variables constrained. The float's pace/effort must be written, observed and filed; there is no FORM-wide fixed offset.

**Not the same session:** the advanced `30 easy / 30 MP / 30 easy / 30 MP / 30 easy` for 2h30 is the **Thirty-Thirty Long**, not a starter 5/5 or 1mi/1mi alternation. It remains gated behind substantial continuous MP or equivalent postrace evidence and a whole-week readiness review. An elite-only 3 × 10K MP is not a standard graduate workout.

## Half marathon: earn the six-to-eight-mile durability question

Half pace is individualized from demonstrated work, not the goal alone. The separate 20–30 seconds/mi faster threshold-work observation in Brice's sub-1:30 cohort is a **calibration hypothesis**, not a conversion rule or substitute for HMP capability.

| Capability question | Working example | Development read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish a first substantial HMP exposure | **5 HMP / 5 easy × 6** = 30 min HMP | Re-entry and even pace with no stoppage |
| Use an easy-mile bridge | **1 mi HMP / 1 mi easy × 4–5** | 4–5 substantial HMP miles separated by easy running; choose actual total dose |
| Extend time in each hold | **2 mi HMP / 1 mi easy × 2–3** | More uninterrupted ownership, fewer transitions |
| Reduce relief | **3 mi HMP / 0.5–1 mi easy / 3 mi HMP** | Six HMP miles with one reset, selected only if prior layers are absorbed |
| Remove the escape | **5 mi → 6 mi continuous HMP**, then **up to 8 mi** only if informative and recoverable | Longest uninterrupted qualifying segment; not cumulative interval miles |
| Carry pace into a long run | **45–65 easy + 25–40 min HMP**, or a selected later 3–5 HMP miles | Late availability after earlier time-on-feet, not merely early pace ownership |

**Six to eight continuous HMP miles are valuable but expensive.** For a runner at 6:50/mi, six miles is about 41 minutes and eight is about 55 minutes at HMP. A single well-placed 6-mile check, with an 8-mile check **only if the result will change a coaching decision**, can supply better information than repeatedly trying to prove the same capability. For some athletes, 5–6 continuous miles followed by later HMP in a long run provides enough evidence before race day. Do not impose an eight-mile gate on a recovery-limited athlete; protect the target event and the next 48–72 hours.

**Floats:** a separate `5 HMP / 5 float × 6` is possible after the easy alternation becomes repeatable. It is not equivalent cost and is not mandatory before continuous HMP. A longer HMP segment separated by genuine easy jogging may be a smarter next step than changing both duration and recovery intensity. Specific long-run Saturdays reduce or displace another hard threshold/speed day.

## The José / Hope half-to-marathon bridge

**Current sequence:** Orlando Half Marathon **December 5, 2026**, Donna Marathon **February 7, 2027**. The same proposed *clock pace* may serve as their Orlando HMP and candidate Donna MP, but **the later marathon target must still be checked against actual results, race effort, easy-running foundation and recovery**. It is not a fixed equivalence inferred from goal labels.

If either athlete sustains that proposed MP for roughly **90 minutes** in Orlando, it is strong **race-day proof of continuous pace access** at that speed. It would exceed the usual 60–70-minute continuous MP *time* requirement as evidence of pace control. **It does not by itself prove marathon durability, fueling competence over 26.2 miles, an absorbable training dose, or the late 10K.** A hard, near-maximal half-marathon at that pace answers a different metabolic and recovery question than a controlled marathon-pace training long run. Read the filed race effort, splits, symptoms and recovery separately.

That race result can **replace a redundant 60–70-minute pace-establishment test** when the pace is still appropriate and the athlete recovers. Then the post-Orlando question is no longer “Can this speed be held continuously?” but **“Can the athlete access it after more easy running, fuel it, and keep it through the closing 10K?”** The short nine-week bridge must allocate time to absorb Orlando, restore the long-run base and train late specificity rather than re-proving the same early capability.

The athletes cannot be cloned. José has historically demonstrated larger HMP stretches; Hope's recovery/PT interruption and current published workload require an independently scaled, potentially smaller progression. Do not assert that either has passed the upcoming race. No Donna assignment or changes to current plans are authorized by this doctrine alone.

## FORM evidence rules and failure modes

For every meaningful session capture: current evidentiary pace source; session total elapsed, total **easy** minutes, total race-pace minutes and **longest continuous race-pace segment** separately; full work/easy/float split sequence; RPE/athlete language; environmental conditions; fueling; late pace quality; and next 24–72 hours of training. Distinguish a continuous session from race-pace continuity. A 60-minute run with 30 minutes MP is **not** 60 continuous minutes MP.

Avoid these failures:

- Celebrating the 1mi/1mi run as a passed 60-minute **continuous MP** test; it is a pace **re-entry** test.
- Turning the new alternations into another set of forbidden mile-repeat intervals with full stopped recovery.
- Assigning 5/5 floats before the athlete can reliably absorb 5/5 genuinely easy transitions.
- Having every midweek easy run be just a short warm-up or cooldown while treating one exhausting Saturday as aerobic development.
- Stacking a threshold workout, eight continuous HMP miles, hard lower-body lifting and a race-specific long run without making choices.
- Treating an Orlando half result at candidate MP as a guarantee of the Donna last 10K.

### Scientific context, and its limits

- A systematic review of middle- and long-distance training-intensity distribution reported that substantial low-intensity training supports performance adaptations; it does **not** establish a universal 60–75-minute daily easy-run requirement. [PubMed 34749417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34749417/).
- An observational analysis of marathon runners found faster performance groups accumulating a larger proportion of low-intensity running; that association is **not** a prescription of FORM's exact durations. [PubMed 39616560](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39616560/).
- A study of marathoners tested endurance-performance markers before and after a 90-minute prior run, supporting the *concept* of measuring durability under accumulated running; it does **not** validate a particular 1mi/1mi or 5min/5min progression. [PubMed 41128270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41128270/).

**This architecture is Brice's coaching method.** Its numerical session choices are working hypotheses to test, refine and individualize—not methods proven by the cited papers.
