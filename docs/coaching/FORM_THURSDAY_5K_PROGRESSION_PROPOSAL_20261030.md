## Decision

The October 29 Fifth Circle (5 × 600m, 4 × 200m) showed available short speed. The coach reports most of the group at or ahead of the target and the 200s around 35 seconds. Brice's FIT verifies his five 600s at 2:04.189 / 2:01.537 / 2:03.378 / 2:01.324 / 1:59.216; the 200s were mistakenly programmed as 2-mile steps and manually lapped, so do **not** claim precise measured 35-second 200s or individual group splits from the recording. The next training question is duration at speed, not simply faster short reps.

The direction is a 16:30-range **goal 5K demand**, not a claim that the group can presently race 16:30: approximately 3:18/km, 5:19/mi, 79.2/400m, 1:58.8/600m, 2:38.4/800m, 3:18/1000m. Retain faster-than-5K mechanics in short closing pieces, develop 5K rhythm in longer work, and use a controlled sustained lane to support 5K/10K running and mixed-demand HYROX athletes. An actual mile benchmark and sustained 5K evidence remain unverified for most of the group.

## Session geometry and pace prescription

All speed-day warm-ups: 20 min easy + drills/mobility. All speed-day cooldowns: 15 min easy. Recoveries are **easy movement**, not an additional hard interval; exception only if Brice explicitly authors a standing recovery. Every block has one definite prescription. Targets are **rep times** to prevent watch pace confusion. Paces for timed sustained work are **/mile** with metric equivalents calculated for each athlete's preferred display, never from the rep's metres.

For timed holds, the displayed pace must be verified against the **actual current fitness and response** before first publication. The sequence is a coach-selected working standard, not lab-measured lactate threshold. Do not classify these as a validated physiological threshold just because the older calendar used that word. Athlete-facing **Long Holds** communicates what to execute, while private coach notes retain the physiological intent.

**Volume accounting:** Speed workouts move from 2.24–2.49mi of fast reps to 2.98–3.11mi late in the cycle. The 20-minute warm-up plus 15-minute cooldown add roughly 3.5–4.4mi at easy 8–10min/mi, *before* jog recoveries. Thus the next speed sessions are typically about 6–7mi total, and sustained days can be ~7–9mi. Do not label them 5–6mi total or reduce a proper warm-up silently. Brice called 5–6mi an estimate rather than an absolute cap. If the total-volume envelope needs to be strictly 5–6mi, the coach must deliberately choose a lower main-set dose and version the change.

**Training evolution:**
1. November: turn isolated short speed into 1000/600/400 changes and 800–1200m contact with 5K rhythm. Two shorter sustained exposures support that speed.
2. December: retain fast finishing mechanics, bring 800s near goal rhythm, then increase the repeatability of mixed 800/400 work. One continuous sustained day after the holiday reduces complexity.
3. January: hold the actual 3:18–3:22/km 5K demand through 5 × 1000m with recoveries, then transition to January 31 race-week freshness. This is a **specificity rehearsal**, not verification of a 16:30 continuous 5K.
4. Performance gates are **coach-private** after Nov 19, Dec 17 and Jan 14: rep-to-rep variation, reported effort, next-day leg/tibial response, actual Monday iRUN, Saturday work, and paired Forge lower-body exposure. No algorithm accelerates targets or overwrites a current session on the strength of one comfortable day.
