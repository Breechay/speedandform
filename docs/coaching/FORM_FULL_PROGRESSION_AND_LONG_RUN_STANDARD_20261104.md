# FORM · Full progression and long-run programming standard

**Owner:** Brice. **Decision:** November 4, 2026.

## 1. The plan is the decision

Author the **best complete, executable progression now** for each athlete, through the intended race or training-block end. Publish real sessions, exact work and recovery, pace/effort prescriptions, easy/support running, long runs, step-back weeks and taper. Use available evidence and individual constraints to choose the best path without inventing future results.

**No standing progression gates or pending coach reviews.** Do not leave a session provisional, withhold future training, or schedule a weekly approval checkpoint merely because the athlete may later send information. Athlete feedback arrives by text or through FORM; when new evidence actually warrants a change, revise the relevant future prescription through normal versioned authoring and verify the athlete-facing feed. Genuine safety restrictions are not optional, but should not be turned into invented administrative reviews.

This rule applies to every athlete, including Elijah and Sohel. Observation-led coaching means interpreting evidence when it arrives, not making a plan contingent on it.

## 2. Long runs have distinct jobs

**Some long runs must be actual workouts**, not an unchanging stack of easy miles. Design a deliberate long-run sequence with complementary roles:

- **Aerobic long run:** genuinely easy running, fueling and time on feet.
- **Progression or steady-finish long run:** mostly easy, with structured late miles at clearly prescribed steady or progressively faster speeds, as appropriate for the athlete's goal and current durability.
- **Race-specific long run:** selected, recoverable faster running inside a long run where its dose and timing serve the race. Race-pace finishes are *not* compulsory for everyone.
- **Step-back/taper long run:** easy and reduced where the week or race demands it.

A prescribed fast finish counts as a **quality session** in the weekly load. Do not simultaneously escalate Tuesday intervals, Thursday support, total mileage and Saturday intensity by default. Alternate harder long runs with easy ones when warranted by the block, and preserve meaningful recovery. The long-run work must be encoded as ordered typed components (distance, pace, recovery when applicable), not only in a narrative description.

## 3. Delivery

Keep each workout's exact total distance. Do not use the work-only segment as total mileage, add compensatory miles, copy cancelled prescriptions, or label a runner's workout as provisional. Protect completed history and athlete-specific overrides. Read back the published coach/athlete plan feed and confirm visible dates, title, pace units, components and week descriptions.

This standard adds to, and supersedes conflicting routine-review language in, `docs/FORM_OBSERVATION_LED_PROGRESSION.md`. Existing athlete reports still inform responsive revisions; no routine review must happen before already prescribed progressions proceed.
