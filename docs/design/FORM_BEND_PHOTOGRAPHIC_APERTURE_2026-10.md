# FORM · The Bend as Photographic Architecture
**Approved creative direction · October 19, 2026**

**Canonical reference:** the owner-approved October 19 `Inside FORM` composition: a real training photograph in a dramatic left-side aperture defined by the nine-lane bend, warm cream negative space on the right, an SF emblem integrated over the photographic field and unapologetically large type. This reference is **visual art direction**, not permission to publish the exact mock image or repeat its placeholder prose.

## Creative principle

**The bend is more than a logo background. It can become the edge of a world.**

The nine measured lanes lead the eye into authentic FORM photography. The SF mark can inhabit that photo field rather than floating in an isolated brand rectangle. Large editorial type occupies the counterweight, with room to breathe. The image, type, mark, linework and negative space together create *arrival*, scale, tension, depth and feeling.

The result should feel **grand, powerful, emotionally moving and distinctly athletic**, without making the athlete's training, photography or record into a generic motivational advertisement. **Don't remove theater to make things look expensive. Don't add ornament just to make a weak page look busy.** Composition is the effect.

## The photographic-aperture grammar

1. **One dominant bend gesture in one composition.** Treat the nine-lane, measured signature as a track or architecture around the image, not a decorative tiled wallpaper. Use `scripts/sf-bend.cjs` and the established kit/CSS or native path geometry. Do not redraw, mirror, distort, crop into unrecognizability or generate extra imitation lanes. The photo can fill the aperture inside the bend, with the lanes framing its edge. This is an explicit *scoped exception* to older generic guidance that the bend must always stay away from content.
2. **One authentic photographic subject.** Use rights-cleared, approved FORM runners, real group sessions, environmental photographs or film stills with verified publication/participant permissions. Edit for faces, skin luminance and action, not generic stock campaign beauty. The bend can hug a portrait, track scene, start line or team photograph. Don't imply the people are FORM athletes unless that is true.
3. **The SF emblem belongs in the scene when earned.** It can cross the photographic field as an intentional visual anchor, with strong contrast and correct official aspect ratio. Avoid covering faces, critical athletic action, meaningful data, evidence or captions. The emblem is not a repeated watermark on every photo; use it as a decisive signature in a select hero/editorial composition.
4. **Typography is monumental but factual.** Permit striking sans or serif display scale, unusual line breaks, strong weight contrasts and asymmetry. The words identify an actual person, practice, film, event or action. Don't narrate how impressive, emotional, cinematic, 'real', or 'not stock' the presentation is when the photograph already proves the subject. **Present the evidence. Don't narrate the presentation.**
5. **Deliberate tonal contrast.** Cream/bone against ink, graphite and real monochrome or richly graded natural photography. Black-and-white is one strong option, not a mandate. Keep the image's blacks, highlights, facial structure, especially darker skin, and light direction. Shading/gradients protect copy without bleaching or flattening atmosphere.
6. **Composition, not a template factory.** Explore left or right apertures when supported by the canonical non-mirrored geometry, upright/full-bleed crops, horizontal image strips, split editorial chapters, layered emblem/film stills, negative-space headlines, occasional cropped lane interruptions, and asymmetric mobile arrangements. **Do not paste the exact 'photo left / headline right' composition into every page, card or app screen.** One theatrical gesture may be followed by a quiet, highly functional room. Variation creates rhythm.
7. **Motion extends the architecture.** Where appropriate, a line may trace/draw, a photo may be revealed as the bend passes, or a room may transition into a film. Retain the established native cream ink-bend loading/sign-in animation unchanged unless separately approved. No elaborate scrolljacking, jitter, forced video audio or perpetual motion. Reduced Motion gets a beautiful finished still/crossfade with equal meaning.
8. **Scale gracefully.** Desktop may use a monumental split chapter. On phone, let the photo have its own generous frame and the text flow *below* or beside it when genuinely legible. Preserve important heads/bodies in the crop; avoid sideways overflow, cut-off emblem, text baked into photographs or an interaction that requires hover. Keep semantic headings, useful alt descriptions, contrast, touch targets and image size/budget appropriate.

## House rules and exceptions

- This is an **affirmative standard** that overrides prior *blanket* restrictions like 'all decoration is unnecessary,' 'the bend must always be clear of photographic content,' or 'all photographic composition must be a simple stripe of images' **within approved photographic editorial scenes**. It does not override the exact logo geometry, verified content rules, accessibility, published consent, private identity, or the no-dead/no-padded-sections contract.
- The screenshot's example headline `Every photograph is a FORM athlete` is not a universal factual claim or mandatory brand tagline; some approved media depict places, equipment or environments, and photos must use true captions. The layout's power comes from form and image, **not from narrating the art direction**.
- A beautiful page may need *less* visible copy, not more. Keep only a real title, date/context and a meaningful door. Avoid ungrounded 'discipline is a frequency'-style lines.
- Keep **People are the subject. FORM is the platform. The founder is not the narrator.** The emblem may be strong, but athlete photos are not canvases for gratuitous self-promotion.
- Source work and visual mock approval precede production. Do not interpret this document as permission to deploy, overwrite active hero films, re-export the brand kit, modify trained athlete data, publish the screenshot, or alter the native launch while Code is capturing a Release candidate.

## Creative review gate

1. Does the scene look unambiguously like **FORM** before the visitor reads?
2. Does the architecture **amplify real FORM photography** rather than cover or flatten it?
3. Does the mark feel *placed*, not pasted?
4. Does the type have **grandeur and readability** without telling people how to feel?
5. Is there a convincing mobile composition with real image crops, touch/accessibility and Reduced Motion?
6. Does the next screen provide a useful breath rather than repeating the same big flourish?
7. Are the people, evidence, rights, event status and calls to action **true**?

**The bend is the architecture. The photograph is the world. The emblem is the signature. The athlete is the subject.**
