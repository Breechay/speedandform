# FORM public copy law · October 19, 2026
**Active for public marketing surfaces.** Private coaching remains governed by [VOICE LAW](../VOICE_LAW.md); studies, privacy, terms and the Brand/Media Kit retain their evidence and reference duties.

## Present the work. Do not narrate the presentation.
A photographic composition is not a prompt for more copy. A different section's job can be a different photograph, a measurement, an action, or simply negative space. **Empty space is finished.**

Before introducing a sentence, ask:
1. Does deleting it lose a necessary fact or decision?
2. Is that fact already apparent in the photograph, title, nearby value, accessible control, or linked destination?
3. Is the sentence explaining what this page, picture, feature, link, or layout is?

If it loses no necessary fact, duplicates a fact, or narrates the interface, **delete it**. Do not backfill with a new slogan, kicker, helper paragraph, numbered process, or generated caption.

## Exactly four public page kinds

| Kind | Surfaces | Copy budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Picture** | Home, Photos, Athletes, Track and published albums | One line or none; real names, dates, photo/film/album titles, accurate outcomes, essential actions |
| **Offer** | Run Development, strength coaching, Runs, Thursday, plan storefront | Strong headline; price, duration, location, schedule or inclusion; at most one distinctive human line; one clear action |
| **Product** | Running and Strength app landing pages | Headline, one sentence, genuine app screen, store access and *required* compatibility/pricing terms. A program curriculum may list real phases. |
| **Reference** | Library **lessons**, Labs studies, Brand/media kit, Privacy, Terms, legal/support docs | Necessary explanation, methods, evidence caveats, citations, safety and rights information belong here; keep complete. The Library **lobby** is an index, not a lesson about lessons. |

## Prohibited on picture, offer and product entrances
- Mood or category eyebrows: “Inside FORM,” “From the practice,” “Shared standards,” “How I coach,” “The mornings,” “From the FORM team.”
- A subtitle that restates the headline, or a second sentence explaining the first.
- A paragraph reading a photograph or actual app screenshot aloud. Wide, face, effort, detail, after is **an editorial crop sequence**, never visible narration.
- Generic tours: “Explore the,” “Browse the,” “See the photographs,” “Open the archive,” “Learn more,” “A simple loop,” “Three ways in,” “Five kinds of running.”
- 01 / 02 / 03 process essays. **Numbers are allowed** for race/time/distance/price, actual training sets, program phases, published album counts and the Brand kit.
- Multiple promotional doors to the same URL **within main content**. Navigation, legal/footer links, citations, app-store attribution, keyboard skip links and functional session controls are exempt. Do not break information architecture in pursuit of a mechanical zero-duplicate count.
- A public homepage questionnaire, price tour, and “what happens next” guide. A single action goes to the existing actual inquiry page. Preserve that form's consent, spam, measurement and submission safeguards at its own address.
- Menu <small> subtitles, “Explore” headings or duplicate destinations. Labels are simply “Athletes,” “Runs,” “Photos,” “Run Development,” “Running app,” “Strength app,” “Plans,” “Thursday,” “Library,” and “Contact.”
- Narration of a visual change. Changing filters, typography, photography or arrangement grants **no extra words**.
- Decorative em dashes in newly rendered marketing copy. En dashes remain valid for ranges.
- Filling quiet space with an explanatory paragraph.

## Preserve actual voice, not the scaffolding
Established lines are protected **when they fit their specific page**, not required everywhere:
- **Home:** “Run farther. Feel smoother. Get stronger.”; “The work shows.”; “You can hit the pace. Can you hold it?”; “Tell me what you want to be capable of.”
- **Study evidence:** “Hope and José are working on that question.”; “6 miles. Continuous. No resets.” The detailed methods and caveats live in the study.
- **Run Development:** “Run better.”; “I look for ease.”
- **Runs:** “Run together.”; “All paces welcome.”
- **Running app:** “Train for your next race.”; “Know what to run today.”
- **Strength coaching:** “Get stronger. Build muscle. Get leaner.”
- **Strength app:** “Build muscle with a plan.”
- **Contact:** “Let’s talk.”

Do not delete a genuinely necessary fact, safety message, accurate result qualification, fee or subscription term, consent notice, publication/visibility safeguard, image alt text, accessible structural heading, data loading state, privacy text or attribution **just to satisfy the visual rule**. Accessibility names must name the destination, not a marketing story. The app screen takes the place of feature essays, never the place of real compatibility or purchase terms.

## Production contract
- **Write less by deleting**, never by rewriting an essay into a new essay.
- Source owner first: menu in `scripts/site-menu-panel.html`, generated pages in their original templates, cross-site build contracts in the relevant scripts.
- Check the relevant generated HTML after all generators run.
- CI's `tests/public-copy-law.cjs` enforces known recurring phrases, menu subtitles and destination uniqueness on selected authored pages. It cannot decide every sentence; editorial review remains necessary. Test source and rebuilt output.
- Visual QA at 1440, 1024, 390 and 320px, reduced motion, screen reader and focus checks before publishing.
- **One coherent release** by explicit approval, never one deploy per edit.

## Where not to apply this law blindly
Legal and study evidence is not a mood caption. Preserve the source's caveats. Lessons exist to explain. Plans need actual schedules. Native Training Week is not a public marketing landing page. Do not delete private coaching instructions, athlete training prescriptions, publication gates, forms on their destination pages, subscription conditions or consent disclosures.
