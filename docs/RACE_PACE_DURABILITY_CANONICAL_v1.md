# Race Pace Durability — the canonical prescription

**Revision 3. 11 September 2026.**

The method is shared. The pace is athlete-relative.

The race-pace number is not the method. The method is to keep a fast but submaximal pace below the athlete's ceiling, progressively remove the escapes, and extend how long that pace remains ordinary.

The public Plan therefore speaks in roles:

- **Race pace** = `your race-pace band`
- **Threshold** = `your current threshold`

An athlete assignment resolves those roles to actual numbers. Current study examples:

- José: race pace **6:30–6:45/mi**; threshold about **6:15/mi**.
- Hope: race pace **6:45–7:00/mi**; threshold about **6:20–6:25/mi**.

Those numbers are evidence from these athletes, not universal Plan constants.

The current paired evidence also sharpens the relationship between the instruments: threshold keeps a ceiling above race pace; race-pace work extends ownership underneath it; the long run asks whether that access survives duration.

---

## The progression

The race-pace ladder remains:

**5 → 6 → 8 → 12 → race**

The pace does not automatically get faster because an athlete succeeds. What grows is uninterrupted ownership.

The Thursday lane now has an explicit threshold spine rather than threshold appearing as unrelated workouts:

**W3 2 × 10 → W5 3 × 10 → W8 2 × 15 → W10 3 × 10 → W13 2 × 8**

The progression is not "run threshold faster every time." It first adds threshold time, then removes a reset, then re-establishes the ceiling later in the block, then tapers it.

W7 keeps one controlled higher-ceiling VO₂ session. The major race-pace asks stay protected by easy + strides on Thursday.

---

## Why 7 and 10 are not ownership rungs

### Do we want 7 continuous in W8 when 8 is coming in W9?

**No.**

7 would cost a full ownership-scale effort to answer a question the method does not need. W8 is a build week: high broken race-pace volume, threshold, and a long run carrying race pace late. W9's 8 is the next fresh continuous ask.

### Do we want 10 continuous in W11 immediately followed by 12 in W12?

**No.**

The 10 does not fit without crowding the culminating 12. W11 instead asks for 18 miles with the last 6 at race pace off tired legs. That is a durability question, not a new ownership rung.

The bridge is therefore:

**8 fresh → 6 late inside 18 → 12 inside 16**

## The three jobs

### Tuesday — own the pace

Touch the athlete's assigned race-pace band, repeat it, then remove recovery and extend uninterrupted distance.

### Thursday — keep a ceiling above it

Threshold work develops the sustainable ceiling so race pace remains submaximal. The threshold pace itself only moves when athlete evidence says the threshold moved.

### Saturday — make access survive duration

Easy long running builds the floor. Later long runs ask for race pace after prior mileage so fresh access and late access are not confused.

---

## Decision rule

The calendar does not automatically advance the athlete.

> **Continuous ask @ RP**
> Held inside the assigned band → the method may continue.
> Held but the band drifted late → repeat the distance; do not automatically advance.
> Could not hold → interpret why. Do not punish and do not automatically advance.

The Plan authors the method. The athlete response decides the next move.

---

## Public vs athlete-specific truth

The public Plan should never imply that `6:30–6:45` is Race Pace Durability itself. Public surfaces render **@ your race-pace band** and **@ your current threshold**.

The assignment owns the athlete's actual values. The living study may show José's and Hope's numbers because those numbers are evidence in the study, not the definition of the Plan.
