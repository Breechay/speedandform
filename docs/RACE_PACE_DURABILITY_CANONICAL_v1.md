# Race Pace Durability — the canonical prescription

**Proposal, revision 2. 5 September 2026. Nothing wired, nothing assigned.**
Authored from the method. José's and Hope's blocks were source material; neither
is cloned.

Brice's rulings are in: double 60 peak kept, 12 continuous kept at W12, race week
kept near 33, 45 as entry *capacity*, the 6:30–6:45 band and its buffer, and 7 as
a step rather than a published rung.

Two questions were put to me. Both have the same answer underneath.

---

## The two questions

### Do we want 7 continuous in W8 when 8 is coming in W9?

**No. It has been removed.**

7 was costing a full ownership-scale effort to answer a question nobody asked —
it is not a rung, and it sat seven days before the 8 that is. W8 becomes a build
week: the highest broken race-pace volume in the block, threshold, and a long run
carrying race pace late. **W9's 8 is now the first continuous ask in five weeks**,
and it lands on legs that have been prepared for it rather than legs that just
did 7.

### Do we want 10 continuous in W11 immediately followed by 12 in W12?

**No — and the schedule proves it cannot be fixed by moving things.**

12 continuous must sit at W12: three weeks out. W13 is two weeks out and too
close. That leaves **W9 to W12 — four weeks — for three ownership attempts with
absorption between each, which needs five.** It does not fit. Two of the three
would have to be consecutive, or a rung has to go.

**The 10 goes.** W11's Saturday becomes 18 miles with **the last 6 at race pace
off tired legs** — real durability work, but no ownership claim, because 6 is
already owned. Then 12 at W12 is the block's single culminating ask, which is
what you wanted it to feel like.

The bridge from 8 to 12 is not empty: 8 fresh on a Tuesday (W9), then 6 at the
end of an 18 (W11), then 12. **The fatigued 6 is arguably a harder question than
a fresh 10**, and it asks the one the race will ask.

**This changes the published progression to `2 → 5 → 6 → 8 → 12`.** Five rungs,
each with a real session behind it. Keeping 10 on the graphic would put us back
where the prototype was — a checkpoint with nothing prescribed under it.

## Race week

33 total, of which **13.1 is the race** — about 20 miles of training around it.
Not optimised to make the taper curve prettier.

Thursday is deliberately not carrying mileage: **4 easy plus 4 × 20 s strides**,
two days out. Tuesday's `2 × 1` at race pace is the reminder. Neither exists to
satisfy a weekly total.

## The thing this is missing, and it is the valuable one

The table says **W9 = 8 continuous**. It does not say **why W9 is allowed to
become 8**, or what happens when it is not held.

Your own rule — *we don't add another hard workout until you fail at holding the
race-pace miles* — means this is a progression with decision rules, not fifteen
rows. Each ask needs its verdict:

> **5 CONTINUOUS @ RP**
> Held inside the band → the method continues.
> Held but the band drifted late → repeat the distance, do not advance.
> Could not hold → interpret why. Do not punish, do not automatically advance.

That is what makes it a method someone can buy rather than a schedule they can
copy — and it is what the canonical plan needs before it is truly canonical.
**Not authored here.** It is the next coaching decision, not an implementation
detail, and I have not invented the verdicts.
