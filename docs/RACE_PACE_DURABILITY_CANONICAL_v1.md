# Race Pace Durability — the canonical prescription

**Proposal, 5 September 2026. Nothing wired, nothing written to any athlete.**
Authored from the method rather than transcribed from José or Hope. Their blocks
were the source material; neither is cloned.

---

## The argument

**You already have the speed. Now learn to carry it.**

The previous block built threshold capacity. This one keeps that ceiling in place
and spends the block extending how far **6:30–6:45/mi** can be carried before it
comes apart. Race pace does not get faster because the athlete succeeds — the
band is the prescribed stimulus, not a target to beat. **Under 6:30 is not a
better session. It is a different session.**

## Who it is for

Not a finish time. A capability:

- comfortable around **45 miles per week**, stable, not a peak
- enough speed above half-marathon pace that race pace is not the speed problem
- durable enough for **three meaningful days a week**
- already owns roughly **2 continuous miles at race pace**
- does not need more speed manufactured before specificity begins

## The two curves

**Volume** — 45 → 60 → taper. Peaks twice, at W9 and W11.

**Specificity** — broken race pace → longer race-pace volume → continuous race
pace → the race.

    owns 2  →  5  →  6  →  (7)  →  8  →  10  →  12  →  13.1
              W4    W6    W8     W9    W11   W12    W15

The 7 in W8 is a step, not a rung: a real session that does not claim a
checkpoint. The published progression stays **2 → 5 → 6 → 8 → 10 → 12**.

## The three days

**Tuesday — specificity.** Where the progression lives and where the smaller
checkpoints are established. Broken early, continuous from W4.

**Thursday — the ceiling.** Threshold, hills, VO₂ or strides, and easy when the
week needs to absorb. It preserves what is above race pace without letting
threshold become the protagonist again.

**Saturday — durability.** Long aerobic work that increasingly carries race pace
late, and the two large continuous tests — 10 and 12 — which are long-run scale
and belong here rather than on Tuesday.

Monday, Wednesday and Friday are easy. Sunday is rest, all fifteen weeks.

## The calls I made, and why

**Absorption weeks carry the checkpoints.** W4 (46) and W6 (56) drop or hold
volume in the week a new continuous distance is attempted. A checkpoint is a
question, and asking it on tired legs tests fatigue rather than durability. W7
(50) and W10 (54) are pure absorption with no new claim.

**Race-pace exposure peaks late, not early.** 14 and 15 miles in W11 and W12
against 10 in W8. The previous matrix peaked race pace in W8 and then declined
for seven weeks while the long run kept growing — the specific stimulus arriving
earliest and the general one latest, which is backwards for a durability block.

**The two big continuous efforts are Saturday sessions.** 10 miles inside 18, and
12 inside 16. At that scale they are long runs, and putting them on Tuesday would
either wreck the week or shrink them into something else. Everything up to 8 stays
on Tuesday, where the progression belongs.

**W12 is the block's closing statement, not W11.** 12 continuous inside a 16-mile
Saturday, three weeks out, in a reduced week (52). The last hard thing is the
most specific thing.

**Thursday is a real day.** Nine of the fifteen weeks carry threshold, hills or
VO₂; six are easy with strides, placed where the week needs to absorb. That is
what makes `3 KEY DAYS` true.

**Progression is not monotonic and is not meant to be.** Race-pace volume falls
in W4, W6, W10 and through the taper — every one of those either carries a
checkpoint or is a deliberate absorption week.

## Open — yours to rule on

1. **Two 60-mile weeks, W9 and W11, with W10 at 54 between them.** The alternative
   is a single peak at W11. Two lets W9 own the 8 and W11 own the 10.
2. **12 continuous at race pace, three weeks out.** It is the block's whole
   claim and also its largest single demand. W11 is the other candidate.
3. **W15 totals 33 including the race.** Tue `2 × 1`, Thu easy, Sat 13.1.
4. **Nothing here is Hope's or José's.** Both currently run ~46–58 with a lower
   easy budget. Assigning this to them mid-block is a separate decision.
