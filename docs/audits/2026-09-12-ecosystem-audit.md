# FORM — Ecosystem audit and proposal

Production reviewed September 12, 2026. Proposal only; no site changes or deployment.

**The judgment**

FORM already feels authored. Its strongest opportunity is to make the relationship between a prescription, an observed response, and the next decision easier to encounter. The core architecture exists. Repair contradictory promises, mobile navigation and sharing first; then improve a few transitions. A wholesale redesign would discard more than it would improve.

All seven requested routes were read from production, including rendered plan content, both Raise the Ceiling athlete selections and its final week. Desktop opening compositions were visually inspected. Public HTML, plan renderers, responsive CSS and preview assets were also inspected. Mobile findings below distinguish source-confirmed logic problems from layout recommendations: this audit did not include a rendered phone-width session. The Library index was read only to check whether proposed additions would duplicate existing material. Its linked articles were not audited. Research citations within the studies were not independently fact-checked.

**1. My read of FORM**

FORM makes the next training decision visible. Brice watches how someone runs, gives them work with a particular purpose, and treats their response as grounds to continue, repeat or change the prescription. The distinctive detail is what counts as progress: a pace repeated without a late fade, longer work without a reset, control still present at the end, or an athlete describing something the splits cannot show. Movement practice stays inside that repetition. Hope’s revised band is the clearest live example: her results change the prescription while the larger progression survives. The plans expose the work, the studies preserve the reasoning, and the coaching remains responsible for what happens next. [Homepage](https://speedandform.com/) · [Filed W3 observation](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/#note-w3).

**2. Language spine — Keep / Extend / Avoid**

| Keep | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|
| “I watch for ease.” | An individual coaching attention, immediately understood. |
| “You are an accumulation.” / “No session stands alone.” | Connects weeks without selling relentless escalation. |
| “I want the quiet weeks.” | Gives restraint a human voice. |
| “The app carries the work. The coaching decides what changes.” | Explains the service and the app in one relationship. |
| “See how the work builds.” | The right promise for Plans. |
| “Same method. Two paces.” | A demonstrated decision, not abstract personalization. |
| “The number is not the method.” | Helps a reader use an athlete-specific example appropriately. |
| “The response decides the next move.” | The bridge between execution and coaching. |

Keep homepage phrases on the homepage. Recurrence should come through consistent decisions and a few terms, not repeating its entire doctrine on every route. Sources: [homepage](https://speedandform.com/), [Plans](https://speedandform.com/plans/), [Speed That Endures](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/).

| Extend | Where and how |
|---|---|
| Repeat / continuous / late | Use as short descriptions beside actual progression changes. |
| Output / athlete report / coach read / next | Reuse inside evidence records; the visitor should understand who observed or inferred each claim. |
| Working band | Use for an assigned range, with athlete and date when relevant. |
| Reserve | Preserve the athlete’s wording and unit, when supplied. It is not a calculated readiness score. |
| Open question | Keep hypotheses open until observations support an interpretation. |
| Plan / study / season | Plan supplies work; study follows responses; season places the block within a person’s longer trajectory. |

**Current drift to correct:**

- **“Owned” is used too loosely.** The durability method says not to train a pace one does not own, then describes a pace one can reach but cannot yet sustain. Distinguish accessible pace from established continuous distance. Suggested heading: “Start with a pace you can already reach.” Do not make broken miles imply continuous capability. [Speed That Endures](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/)
- **A hypothesis becomes a promise.** Both Ceiling pages move from an open question to a claim that a higher top end makes everything underneath easier. Keep the question; replace the categorical teaching line with “Build speed above the working pace. Repeat the work and see what changes.” [Ceiling plan](https://speedandform.com/plans/raise-the-ceiling/) · [Ceiling study](https://speedandform.com/labs/raise-the-ceiling/)
- **Lab vocabulary becomes ornamental.** “Power / Economy / Mechanics / Chassis” on the Bridge suggests four established measurement domains without presenting corresponding evidence. Delete the tag cluster; the Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday explanation already does the job. [Labs](https://speedandform.com/labs/)
- **“~30 seconds per mile” is visually more authoritative than its qualification.** Simon’s stated threshold range of 6:12–6:20 and half pace of 6:34 imply 14–22 seconds, not roughly 30 throughout. Preserve the recorded numbers and describe the separation individually. Do not promote this to a formula or shared headline. [Ceiling study](https://speedandform.com/labs/raise-the-ceiling/)
- **“Cost” sometimes contains interpretation.** Hope’s W3 record labels execution as controlled enough to repeat while effort is not reported. Put the execution inference under coach read, preserving the absence of a direct effort report. Pace consistency alone does not establish lower internal cost. [W3 note](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/#note-w3)
- **Institutional language weakens the personal voice.** The Bridge’s language about collecting bodies and good evidence sounds more like recruitment into a research program than Brice coaching people. The inactive participation section can disappear.

Avoid universal stride prescriptions, composite readiness scores, causal biomechanics claims unsupported by the record, and claims that the 10K isolates what one training ingredient caused.

Second, much of the proposed architecture is already present. The task is to improve the location and specificity of connections, not add another global menu.

The resulting loop is homepage → plan or filed observation → matching study or plan → practical use, with a quiet route back to the coaching practice. Plans and Labs remain reciprocal. No connection requires a sign-up or an intermediate marketing page.

The FORM mark currently returns home on the plans but returns to Labs on both studies. Standardize it to home and provide an explicit Labs breadcrumb on studies. Keep one shared mark, navigation spacing, focus treatment and arrow convention; allow the contents of navigation to differ by page role.

**5. Make progression visible without adding another dashboard**

The durability study already contains disappearing breaks and a distance ladder. Reuse that thinking; do not add a second competing diagram there. On the plan, one compact summary above the table is enough:

**Broken work → 5 continuous → 6 → 8 → 12 late**

Label it as the planned progression. The 12-mile step needs its context: final 12 within 16 total. A simple ascending distance line otherwise hides that the condition changes. Keep the intermediate support weeks in the table; the ladder is not permission to skip them.

The most useful immediate comparison is already available:

| Published change | What changes | What remains | What has not yet been established |
|---|---|---|---|
| W3: 4 × 2 miles → W4: 5 continuous | Three floats disappear; uninterrupted demand increases | Each athlete’s assigned band | The ability to hold that band for five uninterrupted miles |
| W4 → later continuous asks | Continuous distance grows, with support weeks between | The band and broader training structure | Longer capability until the work is actually filed |
| Fresh continuous work → late segment | Prior running becomes part of the demand | Assigned pace | Late availability cannot be inferred from a fresh effort |

For Ceiling, retain two individual Tuesday sequences. Simon reaches the first continuous read in W2; Lisa in W3. Show the chosen athlete’s sequence, keeping the repeated 25-minute asks visible. The point of a repeat is comparison, not lack of progression. Thursday and Saturday need one short statement of purpose each, not additional rails. [Ceiling study](https://speedandform.com/labs/raise-the-ceiling/)

At a key transition, use the existing evidence note to answer “Why this next?” A coach-written decision can support progressing, repeating or revising. Do not invent an automatic threshold or traffic-light rule to decide this.

**6. Evidence — make the decision legible**

W3 is the strongest candidate for a concise public evidence composition:

| Athlete | Recorded output | Coaching decision already published | Next work |
|---|---|---|---|
| José | Four 2-mile reps: 6:42 / 6:43 / 6:43 / 6:43 | Keep 6:30–6:45 | Five continuous miles at that band |
| Hope | Four 2-mile reps: 6:50 / 6:50 / 6:53 / 6:50 | Change to 6:45–7:00 | Five continuous miles at her band |

This demonstrates response-led coaching without inventing a trend. The study also publishes their paired threshold results. Keep those as a separate session with its own date and prescription, not as extra race-pace reps. [W3 observation](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/#note-w3)

Preserve four distinctions within the existing notes:

- **Recorded:** rep pace, duration or distance, recovery and supplied conditions.
- **Reported:** athlete wording about effort, limiter and reserve.
- **Interpreted:** what Brice thinks the combined evidence suggests.
- **Decided:** the next prescription and its reason.

Do not connect numeric RPE, “high,” “hard” and missing values into a continuous cost line. Do not convert “chose not to push” into miles in reserve. More records can reveal a pattern, but the same athlete, work and relevant conditions must remain visible before claiming comparability.

For Ceiling, the new block starts September 15. Preserve the opening state as an opening state. Its useful future comparison is the same continuous ask returning, followed by the race. A 10K supplies another observation; it does not isolate the causal contribution of the Thursday standard.

The durability cost graphic is explicitly conceptual. Keep that qualifier next to the graphic. Do not substitute a fabricated athlete trace. Race result accordions are already collapsed in the rendered page; there is no need to add a new empty dashboard. In concise public summaries, omit unavailable fields. In detailed evidence, retain an explicit absence only when it prevents a misleading interpretation.

**7. Movement belongs inside the work**

The connection is already sufficient to begin: the homepage accepts video, explains cues at the track, and connects those cues to repetition through the week. Plans reinforces that relationship. The gap is the repeated, apparently separate one-time form-read offer, not a missing biomechanics product. [Homepage](https://speedandform.com/) · [Plans](https://speedandform.com/plans/)

Proposed optional note after the plan choices: “Want my eye on your running? Tell me what you’re working on. You can include a short video.” Link it to the existing intake and keep its coaching scope clear.

When an actual movement observation exists, add it to that session’s note: what Brice saw, the cue used, what the runner practiced, and what was observed afterward. Show only supplied content. One still or short clip can accompany that note. Do not generate a cue from a race photograph or populate cadence, foot-strike and posture fields for every athlete.

**8. Media — each piece needs a job**

| Placement | Useful media | Job and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Keep existing running and track photographs | Establish the person and setting. The homepage already has human presence. |
| A movement note in either study | A short real clip, perhaps 5–10 seconds, with one restrained annotation | Show exactly what the written observation refers to. Identify athlete, session and moment. |
| A repeat-session comparison | Early and late clips from a comparable angle, when actually captured | Let a reader inspect an observed change in control. Do not label it lower physiological cost on visual evidence alone. |
| A Thursday standard in Ceiling | A short real execution clip when it clarifies the session | Show rhythm or recovery practice that the set notation cannot convey; keep optional and adjacent to the relevant work. |

Keep original footage available behind edited excerpts when useful, avoid dramatic before/after claims, and use only material appropriate for public sharing. None of these proposed clips was verified as an existing available asset.

Direct-link behavior already worth preserving:

- Clicking a filed W3 note sets the URL to [the W3 observation](https://speedandform.com/labs/speed-that-endures/#note-w3); the source also handles opening a note from the hash.
- Selecting Lisa changes the plan URL to [Lisa’s assignment](https://speedandform.com/plans/raise-the-ceiling/?athlete=lisa) and changes the Tuesday work and displayed working band.
- Durability’s share action uses the page address, so it does not preserve the browsed week. Add week-specific links only if the intended sharing action is a particular week; distinguish selected week from actual current week.

Two genuinely useful share assets are sufficient: a legible progression card and the W3 decision, with date and direct link. Each should work as a phone screenshot without a surrounding button cluster. No automated quote-card library is needed.

**10. Useful additions — no new page is justified yet**

The existing [Library index](https://speedandform.com/library) already lists Running Terms, a pace reference, threshold training, a training map, field notes, movement material and a Split Calculator. A new glossary, pace calculator or form-analysis landing page would duplicate established categories before their existing content has been evaluated.

The actual gaps can be resolved inside present pages:

| Gap | Existing home for the solution |
|---|---|
| A visitor cannot quickly see what a study changed | One current observation near the study’s beginning |
| A plan’s order is hard to see before its table | One concise progression summary |
| Public plan and athlete assignment appear interchangeable | A short scope statement beside the plan/study connection |
| An athlete wants to share a particular piece of work | Existing note hash or athlete parameter; optional week link |
| A movement cue needs visual explanation | The relevant session note |

The Library index itself contains corrective/mechanical labels and schedule-specific entries that may deserve a later review. That is a separate scoped audit, not grounds to assert that the unseen articles are wrong or to redesign Library now.

**11. What not to build**

Do not build a separate video-audit product, a universal movement checklist, an athlete leaderboard, a synthetic cost score, another methodology manifesto, an inactive participation form, a new calculator, or a second article repeating each study’s existing explanation. Avoid making all seven pages share the same hero, card layout or typography scale. Do not add media simply to make Labs feel alive; dated decisions already do that.

Do not rewrite the homepage around capacity and durability. Do not turn its Instrument area into a content portal. Do not remove athlete-specific distinctions merely to make the tables look uniform.

**12. Priority**

**Do now**

1. **Repair Ceiling mobile paging.** In the served renderer, every `paint()` resets `first` to the live week when the width is below 600px, undoing the previous/next action. Separately, CSS shows the single-week mobile sheet through 720px, while JavaScript counts two visible weeks at 600–720px, allowing the pager to skip a week and disagree with its label. Initialize the week once, preserve user selection, and use the same breakpoint for layout and paging. Source-confirmed; verify the fix at phone and intermediate widths before deployment.
2. **Fix broken/stale sharing.** Repair the durability study’s missing image, replace the durability plan’s old screenshot, and add Ceiling study metadata. Keep the homepage photograph.
3. **Reconcile current truth.** The Bridge still groups Hope and José under the original band; the study correctly separates them. The public plan mixes an original fixed band with “your band” notation. The study still says the free plan is the exact version both use. Clarify the public version, individual assignments and dated changes without erasing history.
4. **Clean dates and claims.** Speed That Endures renders a September 12 update at the top, a September 11 footer and a “Today · Sep 08” season marker. Use separate, accurate meanings for page update and session date. Remove the oversized approximate 30-second claim and categorical ceiling promise. Preserve the actual data.
5. **Make the next decision easier to find.** Use the existing W3 note as the main evidence example, add one compact progression line on the durability plan, and place contextual links where the reader needs them.
6. **Remove the duplicated form offer and inactive recruitment section.** Keep optional movement input and existing coaching intake.

**Worth exploring**

- A shared typography and spacing pass on navigation, folios, links and footers. Keep the homepage/index serif and the plan/study sans-serif distinction. Reduce tiny operational text, particularly Ceiling’s track-set notation; preserve the hierarchy between prescription and annotation.
- Quieter lime usage: state, selected athlete or one action. Ceiling currently gives every Thursday rep lime, creating many equal accents.
- A single real movement note with footage, once a documented cue and useful clip exist.
- Legible progression and decision share cards, then week-specific plan links if runners actually use them.
- A scoped Library language audit before adding any new educational page.

**Leave alone**

The homepage hero and personal writing; the eight-week offer; its real photography; the practical plan sheets; individual athlete sequences and units; the study/plan distinction; the explicit separation of HYROX evidence from threshold; the provisional season after Orlando; existing note links; and the principle that the next block changes when the athlete gives different evidence.
