## Audit

1. Can the whole-day pattern plausibly support the actual running and strength load? Check carbohydrate availability, protein distribution, total-energy uncertainty and the risk of an afternoon meal replacing dinner. Use explicit assumptions for any nutrient estimate. Do not present unknown lunch/dinner portions or a calculated maintenance estimate as measured intake or need.
2. Check every quantity and unit: dry versus cooked pasta, raw versus cooked meat, one-quarter batch portions, powder serving versus protein grams, milk volume, shopping totals, storage days, container count, freezer use and reheating. Walk through Sunday–Sunday rather than only reading the recipe.
3. Test real days: early run, long run, late breakfast, normal workday, missed prep, restaurant weekend, evening session and rest day. Flag contradictory instructions, unnecessary duplicate meals, poor timing and ambiguous replacements. Do not silently take over the running coach's fueling plan.
4. Verify the exact Thorne chocolate product, label, lot-specific sport certification, allergens, cost and whether it is a reasonable convenience here. Use current manufacturer/certifier sources. Certification is not proof of zero risk or that the overall diet is adequate.
5. Identify what genuinely needs a sports dietitian/clinician, versus optional refinement. Keep essential food-safety and urgent-symptom information, without filling the daily routine with disclaimers or 'tell Brice' narration.
6. Audit the study: distinguish athlete reports, screenshots, result listings, official records, device estimates, planned work and completed outcomes. Same-name matches are not identity. Keep the rejected Miami result and demographic-conflict Courthouse result excluded. Keep 35:15 reported versus 35:21 listed unresolved. No causal effect or muscle gain is established.
7. Reduce reading and decisions. Keep 'Fuel Your Work' / 'Prepare the meals you tend to miss.' Use second person and short actions, such as 'Take photos of 2 normal work lunches.' No top navigation strip. Put preferences, evidence and deeper rationale in collapsed sections. Do not add choices simply to look comprehensive.
