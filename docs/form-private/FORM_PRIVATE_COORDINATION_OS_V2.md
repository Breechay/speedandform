# FORM PRIVATE — Coordination OS
## What removes friction.

---

## The governing principle

The curator reviews. The system operates.

FORM Private cannot depend on the founder's physical presence to function. The coordination infrastructure exists so that a gathering can be confirmed, filled, and executed while the curator is unavailable, recovering, or simply living their life. Human redundancy (a trusted co-operator plus backup) and operational redundancy (the system) work together. Neither alone is sufficient.

The Casa Neos event failed not because of headcount — but because the venue commitment was never confirmed before the social contract was made. Thirty people said yes to a beautiful Sunday afternoon. The venue required a table service commitment. Those two things were never reconciled before momentum made the situation unmanageable.

**The cardinal rule of the Coordination OS: venue terms are confirmed before the Drop goes out. Never after.**

---

## Event classes

Not all gatherings carry the same coordination weight. Two classes exist:

**Class A — High-commitment gatherings**
Venue minimum spends, ticketed dinners, beach clubs, boat days, anything with financial obligations or hard capacity limits. Full coordination sequence required. Deposits mandatory. Hard RSVP deadline.

**Class B — Low-friction gatherings**
Coffee walks, movement mornings, park socials, hosted home gatherings. Lighter coordination. Soft RSVP. No deposit required. Same sequence logic, reduced formality.

The class is determined at Step 1. Everything downstream adjusts accordingly.

---

## The sequence

Every gathering follows this order. No exceptions.

```
1. Curator identifies venue → confirms terms and class
2. Curator sets cost structure → per-person commitment clear
3. Drop goes out → with terms embedded
4. Members RSVP → knowing exactly what they're committing to
5. Confirmation deadline → hard count produced
6. Count delivered to venue → no surprises
7. Day-of execution → co-operator runs, curator attends
8. Post-event → attendance noted, signals logged
9. Learn → refine protocol for next time
```

This sequence is the fix. The Casa Neos problem was steps 1-2 happening after steps 3-4. That can never happen again.

---

## Step 1: Venue confirmation

Before any gathering is announced, the curator or co-operator confirms:

- **Access:** what does the venue actually offer for a group of this size?
- **Cost structure:** is there a minimum spend? Per-person? Table? Buyout? What triggers what?
- **Capacity:** what is the comfortable maximum for this space?
- **Contact:** who is the named person handling this group? Not a general manager — a specific human.
- **Terms in writing:** even a WhatsApp confirmation from the contact counts. Something that creates accountability on both sides.

If these cannot be confirmed, the venue is not confirmed. The Drop does not go out.

**If venue terms drift after confirmation:** the Drop pauses. Members are updated honestly. No heroic improvisation. Venues change terms — the protocol is transparency, not damage control.

**The informal hookup is not a venue relationship.** A friend who works there and "will try to get us a deal" is a starting point for a conversation — not a confirmed venue. The difference between those two things is $30,000.

---

## Step 2: Cost structure

Every gathering has a clear per-person cost before the Drop goes out. This may be:

- **Zero** — hosted gathering, private space, no spend requirement
- **Soft estimate** — "expect to spend around $X on food and drinks, ordered individually"
- **Fixed contribution** — "there is a $X per person flat contribution, collected in advance"

Where possible, individual ordering is preferable to even splits — it avoids the resentment that comes from asymmetric consumption being absorbed collectively. For events with venue minimums, a fixed contribution per confirmed head is cleaner than post-event reconciliation.

The cost is stated in the Drop. Not implied. Not left to assumption. People say yes knowing what yes means.

**Cancellation risk doctrine:** If confirmed headcount drops below the venue minimum after commitment is made, the delta is split among remaining confirmed members. This is stated at RSVP for Class A events — not as a threat, but as a fact. Deposits partially absorb this. Transparency eliminates the surprise.

---

## Step 3: The Drop

The Drop is the signal. It goes out through the coordination app to all active village members.

**What a Drop contains:**
- The occasion (not just the venue — the reason to show up)
- Date and time window
- Location (specific enough to navigate, not an address dump)
- What to expect (vibe, format, dress if relevant)
- Cost commitment (clear, specific, honest)
- RSVP deadline (hard date — not "let me know")
- Spots available when operationally relevant (not as a scarcity mechanic)

**What a Drop does not contain:**
- A guest list to browse
- Profiles of who's attending
- A chat thread
- Anything that turns the coordination tool into a social feed

The Drop is beautiful, minimal, and complete. One screen. One action required.

**Format example — Class A:**

> Sunday, June 15 · 4–8pm
> Casa Neos Beach House · Miami River
> Afternoon into evening. Music. Good company.
> $55/person fixed contribution
> 24 spots · RSVP by Thursday

**Format example — Class B:**

> Saturday, June 21 · 8am
> Bayfront movement walk + coffee
> No cost. Show up.
> RSVP so we know who's coming

That's it. Everything someone needs to decide. Nothing they don't.

---

## Step 4: RSVP mechanics

RSVP is a commitment, not an expression of interest.

**The RSVP options:**
- **I'm in** — committed. You will be there.
- **Not this time** — out. No explanation needed.

No maybes. Maybe is not a headcount. Maybe does not help a venue. Maybe is how Casa Neos happens again.

**Deposits — Class A only:**
RSVP triggers a deposit for Class A gatherings. The deposit amount scales with event cost — roughly 30-40% of the per-person contribution, minimum $20. This is not revenue. It is a commitment mechanism. People who pay $20 show up. People who don't pay cancel the morning of.

Deposit is collected at RSVP. Credited toward final cost. Non-refundable inside 48 hours — except at stewardship discretion for genuine emergencies. That discretion clause exists and is real. It is applied quietly, not advertised.

**Plus-ones:**
Guest must be named at RSVP — not mentioned day-of. One guest maximum per member per Class A gathering. Guest limit is event-dependent for Class B. The guest's name goes to the curator before confirmation is complete.

---

## Step 5: Confirmation deadline

The Drop has a hard RSVP deadline. When it passes:

- Final headcount produced
- Waitlist notified (if applicable)
- Venue contact receives confirmed number
- Co-operator notified of final count and any flags
- Curator reviews

**Waitlist logic:**
If a gathering fills, late RSVPs join a waitlist. If a confirmed member cancels before the 48-hour window, the first waitlist person is notified and given a time-bounded window to confirm. The window is long enough to be humane, short enough to keep the count stable. Exact duration is implementation detail — the principle is that promotion is automatic and time-bounded.

---

## Step 6: Venue handoff

The co-operator delivers to the venue contact at least 48 hours before the event:

- Confirmed headcount (hard number)
- Expected arrival window
- Any dietary or access needs noted at RSVP
- Reminder of agreed terms

The venue relationship is maintained between gatherings — not just activated at handoff. The goal is a standing relationship where the venue knows FORM Private as a group that delivers on its commitments. That trust compounds and produces better terms over time.

---

## Step 7: Day-of

**What the co-operator handles:**
- Physical presence and welcome
- Name confirmation at entry (Phase 1: name list; Phase 2: app-based)
- Real-time logistics with venue contact
- Any day-of issues requiring a human decision
- Stewardship observation

**What the curator handles:**
- Available by phone if genuinely needed
- Nothing that requires physical presence
- Social presence if attending — as a member, not an operator

The curator carries the standard, not the logistics. The co-operator carries the operations. These are not interchangeable.

**Day-of incident doctrine:**
If something goes wrong — a member behaves badly, an uninvited guest appears, a venue conflict emerges — the co-operator handles it in the moment and escalates to the curator by phone if necessary. The co-operator has authority to make real-time calls. The curator has final say on anything with stewardship consequences.

---

## Step 8: Post-event

Within 24 hours:

- Attendance logged (confirmed vs. showed vs. no-showed)
- No-show pattern noted — repeated no-shows without notice affect future RSVP priority
- Stewardship signals logged privately — both positive and concerning. Positive signals matter for future co-steward selection and vouch confidence. Concerning signals feed the stewardship OS.
- Venue relationship assessed — what worked, what to adjust, worth returning

**Step 9: Learn**
After every three gatherings, a brief review: what coordination friction appeared? What should the protocol adjust? The system improves through feedback loops, not through initial perfection.

---

## The co-operator role

**Selection criteria:**
The co-operator is not hired — they are recognized. The right person has attended enough gatherings to be fully calibrated to the doctrine, has demonstrated operational competence naturally, carries no conflicts of interest inside the village, and exercises discretion without being asked to. They understand the standard and can protect it without the curator present.

**Primary co-operator:** one person who carries the role consistently.
**Backup co-operator:** one person who can step in when the primary is unavailable. Named in advance. Not activated ad-hoc.

Single point of failure is not acceptable. The village must be able to execute a gathering with neither the curator nor the primary co-operator physically present.

**Co-operator responsibilities:**
- Venue relationship management
- Day-of physical presence and welcome
- RSVP monitoring and deadline enforcement
- Waitlist management
- Post-event logging
- Real-time incident response

**Co-operator is not:**
- A stewardship decision-maker (final authority remains with curator)
- A membership gatekeeper (remains with curator)
- A public face of FORM Private
- A permanent role — it is a trust relationship that can evolve

The co-operator is the operational engine. The curator is the standard.

## Technology: what the app actually does

**Architecture decision:**
Curator-facing dashboard: web app first. Faster to build, easier to iterate, sufficient for operational proof.
Member-facing surface: iOS first. The experience matters from day one for this demographic. The existing Swift prototype (Drop → Respond → Gate) is the correct skeleton and the design reference.

These are built in parallel, not sequentially.

**Curator dashboard (web):**
- Drop creation: date, time, venue, class, vibe, cost, capacity, deadline
- Live headcount: confirmed, waitlist, deposit status
- Guest log: named plus-ones per confirmed member
- Post-event logging: attendance, notes, stewardship signals

**Member app (iOS):**
- Drop receipt: event signal, one screen, one action
- RSVP: in / not this time, plus-one naming, deposit trigger
- Confirmation: access confirmation for day-of
- Nothing else

**What neither has:**
- Social feed
- Member profiles beyond name and avatar
- Chat or messaging
- Public guest list
- Anything that keeps people on the app longer than necessary

**Phase 2 — after operational proof:**
- Payment integration for deposits
- Venue partnership dashboard
- Stewardship notes layer (private, curator only)
- Member attendance history (private, curator only)
- Automated waitlist promotion

## The Coordination OS in one sentence

**Venue confirmed, terms clear, Drop sent, commitment collected, count delivered — and the curator is free to just show up.**

That's it. Everything else is in service of that sentence.
