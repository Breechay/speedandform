# FORM PRIVATE — Agent Prompt V5
## The operating system exists. Your job is to pressure-test it.

---

## Read this first

FORM Private is a private social village for socially intelligent adults in Miami. The full operating architecture has been designed across five constitutional documents. You are not being asked to design the system — you are being asked to pressure-test one specific part of it.

Read the context below. Then attack your assigned question with full conviction. No hedging. No option menus. Decisions only.

---

## The situation

A village of ~30 trusted adults already exists. One Telegram message produced immediate enthusiasm, 30 confirmed humans, organic vouching, and couples bringing partners. The hard part worked. Demand is real.

The first target event (Casa Neos beach club, April 12) collapsed because venue terms were never confirmed before 30 people said yes. The founder broke his tibia the same morning at a race and has been recovering since. The village has not gathered yet. The operating system was designed during recovery.

**The village is real. The infrastructure is on paper. The question is whether the paper survives contact with reality.**

---

## The five documents — what exists

### 1. Doctrine V3
The encoded standard. The soul layer.

Key principles:
- The product is the gathering itself — and the trust that it will be worth showing up for
- One beautiful gathering is a dinner party. The same people gathering twelve times is a village
- Not luxury. Correctness. Use all of it. Be seduced by none of it
- The goal is not safety through sterility. The goal is aliveness inside trust
- The village is not a stage. Presence matters more than presentation
- The founder set the standard. The standard now travels without him
- Structure carries the field. Not the curator's emotional labor
- One misaligned person can distort a room faster than one excellent person can improve it

### 2. Membership Architecture
Who enters the field.

Key decisions:
- Referral-first, not application-driven. No public waitlist
- Two vouching tiers: full-trust (auto-accept) and developing (curator review) — never made explicit to members
- Calibration through time: the village teaches the standard through immersion, not curriculum
- Three-word onboarding: this isn't a stage. RSVP means RSVP. Guests come through vouching
- Soft exit protocol: quiet removal, no announcement, "this isn't the right fit right now"
- Co-steward threshold: ~40-50 active members before shared stewardship is needed
- Dormancy acknowledged: ghost members create ambiguity, periodic quiet pruning required

### 3. Coordination OS V2
What removes friction.

Key decisions:
- Cardinal rule: venue terms confirmed before the Drop goes out. Never after
- Two event classes: Class A (high-commitment, deposits, hard RSVP) and Class B (low-friction, soft RSVP)
- 9-step sequence: venue confirm → cost structure → Drop → RSVP → deadline → venue handoff → day-of → post-event → learn
- Deposits are commitment signals, not revenue (~30-40% of per-person cost, minimum $20)
- No maybes. Maybe is not a headcount
- Primary co-operator + named backup. Single point of failure is not acceptable
- Technology: web admin for curator, iOS for members, built in parallel
- Cancellation risk doctrine: delta splits among remaining confirmed members, stated at RSVP

### 4. Stewardship OS V2
What protects trust.

Key decisions:
- Instinct is early signal, not final verdict. Triggers observation, not action
- Signal pathways: direct observation, co-operator escalation, member-to-curator, partner signal, indirect pathway
- Invisible trust layer: private notes after every gathering, positive and concerning signals both logged
- Narrative manipulation requires multiple independent corroborated signals before action — gossip must not become governance
- Five specific scenarios designed for: rejection aftermath, post-breakup both members, persistent pursuit, narrative manipulation, energetic drain
- Sixth scenario: performance drift — collective response, not individual exit
- Quiet exit protocol: rare, considered, "this isn't the right fit right now," door closed quietly
- Gender trust as health metric: thoughtful women self-selecting out is the earliest warning signal
- Co-steward conflict of interest: full recusal, no exceptions

### 5. Ritual Architecture
What keeps the field alive.

Key decisions:
- Flagship ritual: monthly dance night. Venue sourced through Lara (wedding organizer, trusted taste, venue relationships)
- Maintenance rituals: Sunday at Hideout, home dinner, movement morning, cultural evening, water day
- Build sequence: movement mornings first → Sunday at Hideout → home dinner → dance night. Village warms gradually
- The no-pressure principle: every ritual must have its own reason to exist beyond the people attending
- The curator creates conditions, then attends as a member. Not the entertainment. Not responsible for everyone's good time
- Core doctrine: "When we are together — that part doesn't matter. Perfect is connection, smile, laughter, memories."

## Fixed constraints — all agents

- Miami only. One city, one village, done right before anything else
- The founder is recovering from injury. Every recommendation must be operable with minimal physical overhead
- The human architecture works. Don't fix it. Fix the operations
- Warmth is load-bearing. Elegance without warmth dies
- No dating app logic. No social media logic. No startup scaling logic
- The goal: a recurring village that does not exhaust the curator
- Phase 1 is manual and concierge. Software is coordination layer, not the product

## Your assignment

---

### Assignment 1: Contradiction hunt
Read all five systems as a whole. Find the contradictions — places where one system's decisions conflict with another's. Where does the membership doctrine create coordination problems? Where does the stewardship protocol conflict with the warmth doctrine? Where does the ritual architecture assume infrastructure the coordination OS hasn't built yet?

Deliverable: A ranked list of contradictions, most dangerous first. For each: what breaks, when it breaks, and how to resolve it.

---

### Assignment 2: Cold start pressure test
The village has 30 people and no gathered yet. The tibia heals. The curator wants to run the first real gathering under the new operating system.

Walk through the exact first 90 days. What is the first gathering, the second, the third? What goes wrong at each stage? What does the coordination OS miss in its first real deployment? What does the membership architecture fail to anticipate when new people try to join on the momentum of the first event?

Deliverable: A 90-day operational reality check. Specific failures anticipated. Specific mitigations proposed.

---

### Assignment 3: Technology specification
The operating system requires two technology surfaces: a web admin curator dashboard and an iOS member app. The iOS prototype (Drop → Respond → Gate) already exists in Swift.

Specify exactly what needs to be built for Phase 1 to function. Screen by screen. Data model. What the curator does, what the member does, what happens automatically. What gets deferred to Phase 2.

Deliverable: A complete Phase 1 technology specification ready to hand to a developer.

---

### Assignment 4: Venue partnership model
The coordination OS requires pre-negotiated venue relationships. The ritual architecture requires a monthly dance night (sourced through Lara) and recurring maintenance rituals at Hideout and other Miami venues.

Design the venue partnership model. What does FORM Private offer a venue? What does the venue commit to? How does the financial structure work? What venues in Miami are the right first targets for each ritual type? How does Lara's role function operationally?

Deliverable: A complete venue partnership model with specific Miami venue targets and partnership terms.

---

### Assignment 5: Monetization architecture
FORM Private currently has no revenue model. The doctrine says membership makes more sense than tickets. But when does money enter the picture, and how?

Design the monetization architecture that aligns incentives correctly — doesn't create transactional event hunters, doesn't undermine the warmth doctrine, doesn't make the curator feel like they're running a business instead of a village.

Deliverable: A complete monetization model with specific pricing, timing, and sequencing. Include what never gets monetized and why.

---

### Assignment 6: Brand and public language
FORM Private needs language for the world outside the village — venue partners, potential members who don't know the curator personally, any future public presence.

Design the brand language. What do you say to a venue partner? What does the waitlist page say if one ever exists? What is the one-sentence description that makes someone immediately understand what this is and want to be part of it — without making it sound like a dating app, a networking club, or a startup?

Deliverable: Complete brand language system. Specific copy for specific contexts. Words to use and words to never use.

---

### Assignment 7: Full system stress test — Miami summer
It is July in Miami. Heat is brutal. The founder's social energy is lower. Half the village travels in summer. The dance venue Lara recommended is unavailable for two months.

How does the village maintain rhythm and cohesion through a difficult operating period? What rituals survive summer? What does the curator do when their own energy is compressed? What does the coordination OS do when attendance is low and the venue pipeline is thin?

Deliverable: A summer operating protocol. Specific decisions for a compressed operating environment.

---

## How to use this prompt

Copy the full prompt. Delete the six assignments you are not running. Keep one. Send to any agent.

Run multiple assignments in parallel across different agents or sessions. The contradiction hunt (Assignment 1) should be run first — it may reveal issues that change the other assignments.

The documents are the source of truth. If your recommendations conflict with the documents, say so explicitly and explain why the document is wrong. Don't work around it silently.

---

## The test

**Does a recurring village exist six months from now that does not exhaust the person who built it?**

Every recommendation gets evaluated against that test. Nothing else matters as much.
