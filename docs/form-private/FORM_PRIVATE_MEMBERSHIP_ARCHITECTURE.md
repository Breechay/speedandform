# FORM PRIVATE — Membership Architecture
## Who enters the field.

---

## The governing principle

The village admits people, not profiles.

No application form captures what the gut knows instinctively. No checklist produces the feeling of a correctly assembled room. Membership is a judgment call — and the system is designed to extend that judgment through a trusted network rather than centralize it in one person forever.

The founder seeded the standard. The village now carries it.

---

## Village size

Science informs but doesn't govern. Dunbar's research identifies a functional band layer at ~50 people — the size at which everyone knows everyone, trust is high, and social cohesion holds without institutional scaffolding. Above 80, rooms stop feeling like a village and start feeling like an event.

The outer membrane — rotating attendees, occasional guests, people moving toward membership — can stretch to 100 without destroying core cohesion, provided the inner village stays intact.

In practice: the village doesn't manage to a number. It manages to a feeling. The room will signal when it's too large before any spreadsheet does. Someone will feel like a stranger. The ease will thin. The curator notices first.

The number is a lagging indicator. The feeling is the metric.

---

## The standard for belonging

**Does this person make rooms better?**

Not: are they impressive?
Not: are they beautiful?
Not: are they socially polished?

Does their presence improve the room they're in? Would you genuinely enjoy four hours with them? Would you vouch for them with your name attached?

Awkward is welcome. Nervous is welcome. Quiet is welcome.

What isn't welcome: chaotic, selfish, attention-seeking, socially exhausting, or extractive. The person who performs connection rather than has it. The person whose presence costs the room more than it gives.

The gut knows this before the mind can articulate it. The system trusts the gut.

**One misaligned person can distort a room faster than one excellent person can improve it.**

This is not a minor operational note. It is the central reason curation exists.

## How membership works

### Entry: vouching only

There is no public waitlist. No application form. No way to request membership directly.

Every new member enters through a current member who vouches for them personally. The voucher's name is attached to the person they bring. That accountability is implicit and permanent — not punitive, but real.

This is how trust transfers. Not through screening. Through people.

### The vouching tiers

Not all vouches carry equal weight. This is never made explicit to members — it operates quietly in the background.

**Tier 1 — Full trust vouchers**
Members whose social judgment is calibrated to what FORM Private actually is. People enjoy their company. They understand instinctively what the room is protecting and why. Their vouch is accepted without a curator check-in.

Characteristics: they've internalized the doctrine without being told it. They bring people who feel immediately correct. Their additions don't require adjustment.

**Tier 2 — Developing vouchers**
Good people. Well-meaning. Benign. But either newer to the village, younger and still developing judgment, or haven't yet fully calibrated to what makes this specific environment different from a regular social group.

Their vouch goes through curator awareness before confirmation. Not approval exactly — just a quiet check. "Who is this? Tell me about them." One conversation. No committee.

### Calibration over time

Tier 2 is not permanent. Time in the village calibrates judgment.

Repeated attendance teaches the standard more effectively than any onboarding document. People learn what FORM Private is by being inside it — by feeling the ease, observing what gets protected, understanding what makes a gathering feel correct. After enough gatherings, a Tier 2 member naturally becomes Tier 1. The curator notices and adjusts accordingly. No ceremony. No announcement.

The village is also a cultural transmission system. Every gathering is teaching the standard to everyone inside it.

## Guest policy

Members may bring guests to appropriate events — not all events.

**Guest rules:**
- One guest per member per event maximum
- Guest must be pre-notified to the curator before the event (name, brief context)
- The vouching member is responsible for their guest's experience and behavior
- A guest who attends three or more events becomes a membership conversation — they either join properly or they don't continue attending

Guests are how new members are discovered. They are also the highest-risk entry point for misalignment. The pre-notification requirement is not bureaucracy — it's the curator maintaining awareness of who is in the room.

---

## Couples and mixed composition

Couples belong. This is structural, not incidental.

Couples stabilize the field. They reduce romantic evaluation pressure. They normalize the environment. They bring friends, siblings, visiting connections — organic village expansion through the most trusted possible referral network.

The village should always feel socially mixed: singles, couples, people in various life configurations. A room of only singles evaluating each other collapses into a dating market. A socially mixed room becomes civilization.

**Composition is a curatorial responsibility.** The curator watches the ratio. If it drifts — too many singles, too many of one gender, too many people from one social cluster — the correction happens through invitation strategy before it becomes a felt problem.

There is no formal ratio target. The curator knows the feeling of a correctly composed room. That instinct is the instrument.

---

## Onboarding

There is no formal onboarding.

New members receive a welcome from the curator — personal, brief, warm. Something that communicates: you were brought here by someone whose judgment we trust. You belong here. Show up like yourself.

The rest is transmitted through the room. The first gathering does more onboarding work than any document could. New members feel the standard operating — the ease, the authenticity, the absence of performance pressure — and calibrate to it instinctively.

The one explicit communication: how the coordination works. Here's the app. Here's how a Drop works. Here's what RSVP means and why it matters. That's operational, not cultural.

---

## Soft exits

People leave the village in two ways: by choice, or by curator decision.

**By choice:** Someone's life changes — they move, their circumstances shift, they stop attending. This is natural. The door is never closed permanently. Life in motion means composition changes.

**By curator decision:** A member's behavior, energy, or social pattern is no longer correct for the field. This will happen rarely. When it does, the protocol is:

1. Curator makes the decision privately. No committee. No deliberation.
2. The member is quietly removed from coordination channels.
3. No public announcement. No explanation to the group.
4. If the member asks directly, a brief honest conversation: "This isn't the right fit right now."
5. The vouching member who brought them is informed privately — not blamed, but aware.

The exit should feel like a door closing quietly, not a verdict being delivered. Dignity is preserved. The room is protected. These are not in conflict.

---

## What membership is not

Not a status signal. Being in the village is not something to post about, name-drop, or perform. The moment membership becomes a credential, performance drift begins.

Not permanent by default. Membership is renewed through participation and through continued alignment with the standard. Someone who stops attending, stops contributing to the village's energy, or drifts from the standard simply fades from the active community. No formal process required.

Not exclusive for exclusivity's sake. The membrane is high-trust, not hermetic. Good people should be able to enter. The curation exists to protect the feeling — not to maintain an illusion of scarcity.

---

## The membership system in one sentence

**The founder set the standard. Trusted members carry it forward. Time in the village calibrates judgment. The room tells you when something is wrong.**

That's it. Everything else is downstream.
