# Race Pace Durability — the plan on the page, reviewed as a prescription

5 September 2026. **Audit only. Nothing changed, nothing wired, nothing
reconciled to José or Hope.** The 15 weeks below are exactly what the approved
public page currently prescribes, read as a training plan on its own terms.

Correction accepted first: my previous audit treated a historical W1 total as
evidence that `45 MI STARTING VOLUME` was wrong. That conflated *what was
scheduled once* with *what volume platform this plan is designed to begin from*.
The 45 is intentional. What follows tests the matrix against that intention
rather than against anyone's database rows.

---

## The four findings that matter

### 1 · The matrix is a 25 → 54 plan wearing a 45 → 60 headline

| | headline | matrix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting volume | **45 mi** | **25 mi** stated at W1 (28 if you sum the cells) |
| Peak volume | **~60 mi** | **54 mi**, at W10 |

It is not close, and it is not a rounding difference. Across the block the
matrix runs roughly **15–20 miles per week below the platform the page claims**.
A runner who arrives able to hold 45 would find weeks 1 and 2 a substantial
*cut*, and would never see 60.

Either the headline describes the intended athlete and the matrix has not been
written for them yet, or the matrix is right and the headline belongs to a
different edition. They cannot both stand.

### 2 · Nothing in the matrix is continuous race pace

Every Tuesday is broken work — `3 × 1 MI`, `5 × 2 MI`, `3 × 3 MI`. Every Saturday
is a bare distance with no pace attached. **There is not one continuous
race-pace session anywhere in the fifteen weeks.**

So the two things the page argues hardest for have no cell behind them:

- `12 MI · LONGEST CONTINUOUS AT RACE PACE` — never prescribed
- `2 → 5 → 6 → 8 → 10 → 12` — no week contains 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 continuous

And the sentence beneath the matrix — *"Race-pace miles progress from broken into
continuous"* — describes a transition the schedule never makes. The block is
entirely broken work from W1 to W15.

This is the single largest gap between what the page says and what it asks for.

### 3 · Race-pace exposure peaks in W8 and declines for seven weeks

Race-pace volume by week: **3 · 6 · 4 · 8 · 5 · 5 · 8 · 10 · 9 · 8 · 8 · 6 · 4 · 3 · 2**

It peaks at W8 and falls away from there. The long run peaks three weeks later, at
W11 (16 mi). For a block whose claim is *extending how far race pace can be
carried*, the specific stimulus arrives earliest and the general one latest —
which is backwards. Specificity should tighten toward the race.

It is also a sawtooth rather than a progression: 6 → 4, 8 → 5, 10 → 9 → 8. Three
times the plan advances and then retreats without a down-week pattern explaining
it. **W5 and W6 are the same session twice (`5 × 1 MI`)**, so a week passes with
no advance at all.

### 4 · Thursday is claimed as a key day and prescribed as an easy one

The hero says `3 KEY DAYS · TUE · THU · SAT`. In the matrix, Thursday is a bare
number — 4, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 6, 8, 8, 8, 6, 6, 6, 5, 4 — indistinguishable in kind
from Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Two key days are visible, not three.

If Thursday carries the threshold work the methodology section refers to
(*"threshold work remains in the program to maintain the ceiling"*), the matrix
never says so, and that sentence has nothing behind it either.

### Smaller observations

- **The TOTAL row is not the sum of its column.** Summing the days gives
  28 · 35 · 36 · 41 · 43 · 44 · 48 · 54 · 51 · 52 · 51 · 41 · 37 · 30 · 30, against
  a stated 25 · 33 · 36 · 39 · 43 · 46 · 50 · 53 · 52 · 54 · 51 · 42 · 38 · 30 · 28.
  Ten of fifteen disagree, by −2 to +3. The gap changes sign, so it is not
  warm-up and cool-down being counted — those would push one way only.
- **The easy distribution is the strongest part of the plan.** M/W/F rises
  13 → 22 smoothly and tapers cleanly, with no week jumping more than 3 miles. It
  does what the volume dimension claims, at a lower altitude than the headline.
- **W9's shape is contradictory**: the longest reps of the block (`3 × 3`) land in
  the same week the long run is cut from 15 to 12. That reads like a down week and
  a breakthrough week at once.
- **The taper is sound** — 51 → 42 → 38 → 30 → 28, an 18% first cut, with race-pace
  contact kept every week.
- **No week is unnecessarily aggressive.** Nothing here is dangerous. The problem
  is the opposite: for a 45-mile athlete, most of it is under-dosed.

## What I would want the plan to answer before it is canonical

Not changes — questions the prescription has to be able to answer, and today
cannot:

1. **Where do the continuous efforts live?** If 5, 6, 8, 10 and 12 continuous
   miles are the checkpoints, they need days. Saturday is the only place with the
   room, which would make the long run the establishing session and Tuesday the
   volume session.
2. **What is Thursday?** Either it becomes the threshold day the methodology
   claims, or the hero says two key days.
3. **Does the matrix move up to the 45–60 platform, or does the headline come
   down to 25–54?**
4. **Is the sawtooth intentional?** Three retreats and one repeat may be
   deliberate down weeks — but they are not on a cycle, and nothing on the page
   says so.
