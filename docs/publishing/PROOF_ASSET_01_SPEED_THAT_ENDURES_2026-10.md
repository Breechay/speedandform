# Proof Asset 01 · Speed That Endures
## Internal creative brief · October 2026

**Status:** candidate proof asset, not approved for publication.

This is the October Experiment A candidate because the underlying work already exists as a living study and the creative asset can serve two jobs at once:
1. make coaching judgment easier to understand;
2. test whether Brice's photography / short-film work is commercially credible.

Do not create a separate portfolio project.

## The question

**What changes when the pace stays the same but the athlete has to carry it farther?**

The film should make one coaching idea legible:
race pace is not only a speed problem; the cost of holding it changes as uninterrupted duration accumulates.

## Subjects

José and Hope are the current study subjects.

Before using any new athlete footage publicly:
- confirm the exact clips / photographs;
- confirm athlete permission for the intended public usage;
- preserve each athlete's distinct story;
- do not imply race outcomes that have not occurred or been verified.

If permission or useful source material is insufficient, do not force this asset. Select another real story.

## Evidence spine

Use the canonical study and current athlete records for facts.

The story should preserve this sequence:

**1. Problem**
They can run the pace. The question is how long the pace remains available before its cost changes.

**2. Decision**
Hold the assigned pace relatively stable and extend uninterrupted duration over the block.

**3. Work**
Show only sessions actually completed and verified. Upcoming work stays future tense.

**4. Athlete response**
Use a short approved first-person line when available. Label subjective reports as athlete reports.

**5. Coach read**
One concise interpretation: what Brice noticed and why the next decision followed.

**6. What remains unknown**
Do not turn a training milestone into a race prediction. Final race claims wait for verified results.

## Film structure

Target: **30–60 seconds.**

### Five visual moments
1. **Arrival** — the person before the work.
2. **Preparation** — shoes, watch, quiet instruction, route/track detail.
3. **Effort** — the session, not generic running beauty shots.
4. **Human moment** — exchange, recovery, concentration, humor, uncertainty or coach observation.
5. **Return** — the work is over; what changed / what happens next.

The story can be photographed, filmed or both. Use the strongest real medium available rather than forcing motion footage.

## Copy / voice

Keep language plain.

Possible opening:
> They could already run the pace.  
> The question was how long they could keep it.

Possible close:
> Same pace. Longer ask.  
> Then we decide what changes next.

These are working lines, not approved public copy.

## What the asset must prove

Not that FORM caused a race result.

It should demonstrate that Speed & Form:
- notices a specific training problem;
- designs a progression around it;
- observes the athlete response;
- makes the next decision from that response.

If a viewer only thinks "nice running video," revise it.

## Outside critique

Before public use, show the near-final version to one person with useful visual/editorial judgment.

Ask only:
1. What did you think the story was about?
2. Where did your attention drop?
3. What felt unclear or unnecessary?
4. Which single moment felt most true?

Make at least one revision from useful critique or record why no revision was warranted.

## Publish gate

Public use requires:
- [ ] source facts current and verified;
- [ ] exact athlete permissions;
- [ ] no unverified result claim;
- [ ] no private athlete detail exposed;
- [ ] outside critique completed;
- [ ] one-sentence statement of what the asset demonstrates;
- [ ] final placement chosen deliberately (site, social, partner material or archive).

Prepared is not published. Published is not proof of commercial demand.

## Commercial test

After the asset exists, do not build packages.

A creative-service opportunity advances only if a named buyer can identify:
- the intended use;
- the approver;
- the desired deliverable;
- a timing need; and
- a budget range or willingness to receive a scoped paid proposal.

If none appears, keep the asset as Speed & Form proof and move on.
