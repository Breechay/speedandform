# Speed & Form Homepage — Positioning V7

## Primary promise
**The thinking is done. You just train.**

The promise is not that users receive more training information. It is that a real goal becomes a clear next action without requiring the athlete to become their own coach.

## Buyers

### FORM
Race preparation, especially first or faster half marathons. The buyer may have a demanding life and a real race date, but little desire to assemble a week from generic plans, AI advice, and disconnected pacing rules. FORM should show that race preparation is built around the goal, current running, available days, and actual progress.

### FORGE
Physique development for men who want a broader, better-balanced frame and more direction than an exercise library or blank tracker provides. The program allocates emphasis deliberately rather than giving every area the same volume.

## Voice
- Warm, direct, capable.
- Plain enough for a beginner; informed enough for an experienced athlete.
- Challenge-oriented without fake aggression.
- No cryptic doctrine that requires interpretation.
- No needy cross-selling between products.
- No founder mythology; one credible coaching statement is enough.

## Ecosystem
The athlete starts with the goal in front of them. The other product is not an upsell inserted into the current chapter. It is a future challenge available when it genuinely belongs.

## Brand meaning
Speed & Form began as the Miami training group. **Form keeps the work precise. Speed keeps it alive.** Composure and intensity are not opposites; each belongs at the right time.

## Visual ruling
- Cormorant remains the homepage hero signature and appears inside app screenshots.
- The explanatory website voice is Jost.
- JetBrains Mono carries navigation and structural labels.
- FORM uses plum as the chapter conversion accent while retaining its multi-instrument palette inside product imagery.
- FORGE remains black, bone, and gold.
- The SF mark stays large enough to own the navigation; the wordmark remains on desktop and disappears on narrow mobile screens.
