# Ask Brice these before writing more 02 arguments

Do not invent a methodology. The first thesis is locked. Everything after it has to be how he actually works — in his words, then compressed to site voice.

Ask one at a time. Write down the answer in his language first. Only then compress.

## The work

1. What do you look at first in a running video?
2. When do you change the week, and when do you hold it?
3. What do you refuse to program, even if someone asks?
4. What does a good week look like when nothing exciting happened?
5. What happens in week 1 that is different from week 8?

## Form and speed

6. When you say form, what are you actually looking at — and what are you not?
7. How does speed show up if you are not chasing it?
8. What is the difference between FORM the house and form in a stride?
9. Where does lifting belong next to the running, when it belongs at all?

## The relationship

10. When is someone not a fit?
11. What does “I answer when a decision is required” look like in a real week?
12. What do you never say to an athlete?
13. What do people think they are buying, and what are they actually entering?

## How to use the answers

Each extra 02 argument = **one claim** (short, serif, like “I develop runners.”) + **one proof** (two or three sentences, quieter).

Ceiling: four arguments including the one that already exists.

If an answer is generic (any decent coach could say it), throw it out. That was the reason 03 died.

Form → speed may become one argument. Only if he says it in a way that is his. Do not pre-write “once you have great form, speed will emerge” unless that is how he talks.
