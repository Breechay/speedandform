# FORM House Research
## What people may actually need from a running home / residency / program
### Research synthesis · October 2026

This document is the evidence layer behind FORM House. It should inform the program, space, hotel/residential/café partnerships and public report page. It is not proof that any specific FORM House format will work. The correct next step is to turn the mechanisms below into testable operating hypotheses and observe real participants.

## Working thesis

The opportunity is larger than “runners need a clubhouse.”

A useful running home may solve five different human jobs:

1. **Make showing up easier.**
2. **Give effort a social meaning.**
3. **Let people belong without requiring identical ability or programming.**
4. **Give the activity a repeatable ritual and place.**
5. **Extend the useful experience before and after the run.**

The physical building is one delivery format. The same system could be a hotel residency, apartment amenity program, café partnership, gym annex or recurring hosted morning.

---

# 1. Social connection is not a decorative benefit

The WHO Commission on Social Connection reported in 2025 that roughly 1 in 6 people worldwide experience loneliness and argues that social health should be treated alongside physical and mental health. This does not mean a run group is a treatment for loneliness. It means connection itself is a legitimate human-health domain, not merely marketing language.

Source:
- World Health Organization, *From loneliness to social connection: charting a path to healthier societies* (2025).

### Design implication
Do not design FORM House only around equipment.

Design for:
- recognizable faces;
- repeated encounters;
- enough dwell time to speak before/after;
- easy arrival for someone who does not know everyone;
- a host who notices people;
- continuity between mornings.

The coffee table may be as important to the social mechanism as the foam roller.

---

# 2. “People around me” is not the same as belonging

A 2026 systematic review of 21 adult group-fitness studies found the evidence for generic social support/cohesion was mixed, while more specific constructs such as social attraction, reliable alliance and group identification were more consistently favorable. Self-efficacy was the most consistently supported predictor of attendance adherence.

A separate mixed-methods study of fitness-club members found social identification with the group, rather than attendance frequency alone, was associated with well-being.

Sources:
- Gao et al., *Perceived Capability, Social Belonging, and Behavioral Adherence in Adult Group Fitness Classes* (2026).
- *What explains the well-being benefits of physical activity?* Social Science & Medicine.

### Design implication
Do not optimize for a crowded room.

A person needs to feel:
- **I can do this.**
- **I know what happens here.**
- **Someone will notice me without judging me.**
- **I can participate at my level.**
- **These are becoming my people / this is becoming one of my places.**

This supports FORM's two-lane long-run model. Shared place does not require shared prescription.

---

# 3. Group identity can strengthen running identity

Research on runners has found that group contexts and membership are associated with stronger running identity and, in observational work, greater running behavior. Research on exercise clubs has also linked stronger group social identity with attendance.

Sources:
- *When a sense of “we” shapes the sense of “me”: exploring how groups impact running identity and behavior* (Self and Identity).
- Bruner et al., *A Sense of Connection: Examining Social Identity and Adherence to a School-Based Exercise Club*.

### Design implication
The product is partly identity scaffolding.

FORM House should make it easy for a person to think:
**I am someone who trains in the morning.**
not:
**I occasionally attend an event.**

Useful cues:
- a recurring day/time;
- familiar host;
- familiar meeting ritual;
- named sessions / lanes;
- visible Archive/history;
- people remembering previous attempts;
- a place to return to.

Avoid cultish or exclusionary identity. Belonging should not require speed.

---

# 4. Repetition + context can make the behavior easier to repeat

A 2026 meta-analysis of 14 studies / 6,069 participants found context consistency was associated with physical-activity behavior and habit strength. Temporal and routine consistency were the cue dimensions most clearly associated with behavior; social, location and other consistent cues were associated with habit strength.

Source:
- Zhu et al., *Not all cues are equal: A systematic review and three-level meta-analysis of context consistency, physical activity and habit strength* (2026).

### Design implication
The “same door at 5:50 AM” is behavior design.

A useful residency should reduce decisions:
- same arrival point;
- same bag procedure;
- same coffee/water expectation;
- same way to find today's route;
- same way to join a lane;
- same return ritual.

FORM software can carry changing training content while the physical ritual stays stable.

---

# 5. Competence matters: people return when they feel capable

The 2026 group-fitness systematic review found self-efficacy was the most consistently supported psychosocial domain for adherence. A prospective study of beginner running groups also reported improvements in self-efficacy and running identity, with self-efficacy and adherence predicting program success.

Sources:
- Gao et al. (2026).
- *Prospective study of beginner running groups: psychological predictors and outcomes of participation* (2022).

### Design implication
A successful group should not make the least-fast person feel like a failed version of the fastest person.

Build:
- multiple valid lanes;
- clear “run your own effort” language;
- turnarounds / distance flexibility;
- authored targets appropriate to the runner;
- coach support;
- visible personal progress.

This also supports Vault target scoring as personal precision/history rather than a global leaderboard.

---

# 6. Autonomy matters: belonging should not become pressure

Systematic reviews grounded in Self-Determination Theory find support for autonomy-supportive coaching and more self-determined motivation in exercise persistence. Competence and intrinsic/autonomous motivation are repeatedly relevant.

Sources:
- Teixeira et al., *Exercise, physical activity, and self-determination theory: a systematic review*.
- Rodrigues et al., *Can Interpersonal Behavior Influence the Persistence and Adherence to Physical Exercise Practice in Adults?*

### Design implication
FORM House should offer structure without coercion.

Examples:
- “You can leave after the first rep.”
- choose the lane that fits today's training;
- shorter distance is a valid use of the morning;
- no attendance streak;
- no public shame for absence;
- no forcing a social personality;
- no requirement to buy coaching to belong.

This is especially important because not everyone experiences groups the same way.

---

# 7. The third-place mechanism matters

Research on “third places” — settings outside home and work where people can repeatedly encounter others — suggests accessible, comfortable places can support social interaction and networks. Public-health scholarship argues that the loss of these places may remove important social infrastructure.

Sources:
- Finlay et al., *Third Places for Older Adults' Social Engagement: A Scoping Review and Research Agenda*.
- Oldenburg-inspired public-health literature including *Closure of ‘Third Places’? Exploring Potential Consequences for Collective Health and Wellbeing*.

### Design implication
FORM House should behave like a third place for active people, not merely a service appointment.

The design question becomes:
**Can someone remain for 20 minutes without needing another transaction?**

Needed:
- comfortable seating;
- social edges, not one giant communal table only;
- coffee/water;
- bathroom;
- bag safety;
- shade / climate relief;
- a reason to linger;
- enough openness for weak ties to form.

A hotel lobby, café corner or apartment lounge may already have most of this infrastructure.

---

# 8. Recurring events can produce a social network, not just attendance

A 2026 prospective social-network study of two parkrun sites found participants became more interconnected over time. A persistent socially connected core emerged, and being in that core was associated with higher participation rather than faster finish times or better fitness.

Source:
- Arias-Calluari et al., *Social networks in parkrun: a comparative analysis of an urban and a rural event* (2026).

### Design implication
This is especially relevant to FORM.

Do not make speed the organizing principle of belonging.

Fast runners can have a workout lane. Easy runners can have an easy lane. The social core should emerge from **showing up and participating**, not performance rank.

The host should make the periphery permeable:
- introduce newcomers;
- explain the morning;
- avoid insider-only language;
- make arrival obvious;
- keep post-run space open.

---

# 9. The after-run period may be a core product surface

The evidence above does not prove that coffee after a run causes adherence. But the mechanisms suggest informal interaction matters, and third-place research emphasizes spaces comfortable enough to stay in.

### Hypothesis to test
The run creates the shared experience.
The return creates the relationship.

Measure:
- percentage who return to the hub;
- dwell time;
- conversations / new introductions (qualitatively, not surveillance);
- repeat attendance;
- whether people say the return makes the morning easier or more enjoyable;
- food/coffee/recovery use.

Do not assume every participant wants to linger. Support quick exit too.

---

# 10. Travel: people increasingly want activity + experience, not only a room

Hilton's 2025 trends research reported that roughly 7 in 10 global travelers enjoy being active when traveling and 76% look for accommodations offering a variety of experiences during the stay. The Global Wellness Institute reported that wellness trips represented 8.3% of tourism trips but 17.6% of tourism spending in 2024. Expedia's 2025 hotel research also shows travelers actively compare amenities and proven guest experiences.

Sources:
- Hilton 2025 Trends Report.
- Global Wellness Institute, 2025 Global Wellness Economy Monitor.
- Expedia Group, 2025 Traveler Value Index.

### Design implication for hotels
Do not pitch “a run club for locals inside your hotel.”

Pitch a **local running layer**:
- guest can wake up and know where/how to run;
- optional hosted morning;
- safe route / distance choices;
- bag/water/coffee/recovery;
- local runners make the property feel connected to the city;
- guest can participate once without pretending to become a member.

Possible hotel product:
**FORM Morning**
- 5:50 meet;
- 6:00 run;
- 2–3 route/distance options;
- return;
- breakfast / coffee;
- optional mobility/recovery;
- guest receives route and session in advance.

This can exist without a permanent FORM House build.

---

# 11. Apartments: the opportunity is activation, not another amenity

A physical fitness room can exist without creating social connection. Emerging housing research supports the broader idea that community engagement and satisfaction can matter for residents' perceived health, though this should not be oversold as evidence for a FORM program specifically.

### Design implication for residential partners
The pitch is not:
**Your building needs more fitness equipment.**

It is:
**Your building already has space. We give residents a reason to use the city and return to one another.**

Possible residential product:
- one weekly coached run;
- one monthly/selected long run;
- route library;
- bag / water / coffee;
- resident assessment days;
- guest-friendly participation;
- optional training-plan/coaching path separate from the amenity.

---

# 12. A program may be more scalable than a building

The research mechanisms do not require FORM to own real estate.

The irreducible system may be:

**PLACE + TIME + HOST + TRAINING SHAPE + PEOPLE + RETURN RITUAL + CONTINUITY**

Everything else is optional infrastructure.

This creates four product forms:

### FORM Morning
A hosted recurring run + return ritual inside someone else's place.

### FORM Residency
A deeper recurring partnership with a hotel, apartment, café or gym.

### FORM Annex
A dedicated physical zone inside a partner property.

### FORM House
A standalone expression when demand/capital/site justify it.

The program should be designed first. The building should amplify the program.

# 13. What FORM should test in the field

Do not ask only “Did you like it?”

Ask what job the gathering did.

### Before
- Would you have run at this time anyway?
- Did knowing people were waiting make it easier to show up?
- Did the fixed meeting place reduce friction?
- Did bag/bathroom/coffee access matter?

### During
- Did the lane make the session fit your actual training?
- Did you feel pressure to run faster/farther than planned?
- Did you have someone compatible to run with?
- Did you feel capable / included?

### After
- Did you return to the hub?
- Did you stay?
- What made you stay or leave?
- Did you meet/talk to someone you would not otherwise?
- Did recovery/food/coffee improve the morning?
- Would you choose a hotel/apartment/gym partly because this existed?

### Over time
- repeat attendance;
- number of recognizable recurring participants;
- newcomers who return;
- self-reported belonging;
- whether participation supports or disrupts individual training;
- conversion to plans/coaching only as a secondary business outcome.

---

# 14. The deeper story

FORM House is not primarily about runners needing somewhere to put a bag.

Modern running can be solitary and still deeply social. People may arrive for fitness, performance, structure, identity, companionship, a reason to get out of bed, a way into a city, or simply because somebody expects them at 6 AM.

The research suggests the strongest program will not manufacture “community” through branding. It will repeatedly create the conditions in which connection can form:

**same time**
**recognizable place**
**useful work**
**appropriate challenge**
**autonomy**
**familiar people**
**low-friction arrival**
**a reason to remain afterward**

FORM already owns the useful work.

FORM House / FORM Morning should own the conditions around it.

---

# 15. Research cautions

- Most evidence is observational or context-specific; do not claim causality where it is not established.
- parkrun evidence is useful but is not a direct evaluation of FORM or performance-running clubs.
- third-place evidence spans populations/settings different from competitive runners.
- industry surveys from Strava, Hilton, Expedia and wellness organizations are market signals, not clinical research.
- social connection is beneficial broadly, but groups can also create exclusion, pressure and comparison. Design autonomy and multiple valid ways to participate.
- measure actual FORM participant experience before scaling the concept.

# 16. Operating protocol · research translated into mornings

The concept is ready enough that the next evidence should come from operating the ritual, not adding more conceptual copy.

For the next four eligible Thursday / Saturday shared mornings, behave as though the physical program already exists even if the venue is temporary.

## Stable ritual

**Same door + same time + host notices people + training fits the person + return does not require another purchase.**

Keep stable where practical:
- meeting point;
- bag procedure;
- where route/session is found;
- explanation of the morning;
- lane language;
- return point;
- water / seating / coffee expectation when available.

Change the training as needed. Do not unnecessarily change the ritual.

## Host behavior

Before:
- make arrival obvious;
- greet / notice people;
- introduce newcomers when useful;
- explain lanes/session once;
- reinforce that easy/shorter is a complete use of the morning.

During:
- preserve individual prescription;
- no pressure to follow the faster lane;
- allow clean early exit / turnaround.

After:
- make the return obvious;
- leave room to sit / talk when the venue allows;
- support people who want to leave immediately;
- do not turn the return into a sales pitch.

## Five-line host note

After each morning, record only:
1. **Arrived** — rough headcount / meaningful newcomer note.
2. **Returned** — roughly how many came back to the hub/return point.
3. **Dwell** — rough pattern: left immediately / ~10 min / ~20+ min.
4. **Friction** — one thing that made arrival, training or return harder.
5. **Worked** — one thing that noticeably improved the morning.

Optional sixth line:
**Witness** — one sentence somebody said that reveals what the morning did for them.

This is field observation, not surveillance. Do not create social scores or individual dwell histories.

## Visual evidence

Capture, with appropriate permission:
- one arrival frame: bags / shoes / route / people gathering;
- one return frame: sitting / coffee / recovery / conversation.

These two images are higher priority for the FORM House page than additional architectural fantasy renders because they prove the ritual already exists.

## Success question

Do not ask whether the morning converted someone to coaching.

Ask:
**Was the training morning noticeably better because arrival, belonging and return had a shape?**
