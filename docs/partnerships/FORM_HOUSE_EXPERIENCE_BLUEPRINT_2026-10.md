# FORM House / FORM Morning · Experience blueprint

October 4, 2026 · Working proposal for a host conversation and delivery planning.
No host, date, route, supplier, guest price or recurring program is confirmed.

## The recommendation

Build a morning people would want to return to even after the run is over.
The defining experience is a personal welcome, a run that respects your training,
a prepared return and a generous table. Towel off is normal. Showers are optional.
The House is the wider ambition; one hosted Morning is its first testable expression.

Plan around **12 runners**, compare 10 and 15, and count friends joining for
breakfast and working staff separately. Start with a host-backed arrangement
because the property helps create the experience and may want to fund it for
its guests. This is a commercial hypothesis, not a proven profitable model.
The existing athlete long-run practice remains free. A possible future public
boardwalk 10K experience is a separate product to explore after a successful
team home and hosted-morning model. No Eventbrite listing, ticket or fixed 10K
route is part of this first team morning.
A first property conversation can happen before a rehearsal. A rehearsal at the
interested property comes before the event invitation and delivery.

## Guest journey and service blueprint

| Moment | What the guest experiences | Team action | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invitation | Understand the run choices, food, finish and what is included. | Confirm actual format first; collect lane, breakfast choice, dietary needs, breakfast-only places and private contact details. | Guest can describe where to arrive, which run fits and what they will receive. |
| Arrival | Someone greets them, handles their bag and shows the bathroom. | Property host stays at welcome; running lead briefs route and return. Newcomers get introductions. | Nobody looks for an unlocked door or wonders who is hosting. |
| Departure | Keep their own distance and effort with clear support. | Named lead for each group; planned turnarounds and regroup points; counts at departure and return; contact plan available. | Everyone knows the route, their shorter option and how to ask for help. |
| First return | Towel, drink, easy food and a seat, immediately. | Onsite host opens the return service before any expected finish, including an early turnaround. | First runner receives the same welcome as the main group. Record actual service readiness and first return. |
| Breakfast | Choose a substantial meal; sit with friends. | Kitchen batches food around returns, checks substitutions and maintains reserve portions. | No wait for the last runner or photograph; record longest wait, leftovers and any unmet order. |
| Stay | Time to talk, read, work or rest without buying again. | Seating remains welcoming; host explains agreed access, handles belongings and unobtrusively clears dishes. | Breakfast-only guests join naturally; no pressure to order or leave early. |
| Goodbye | Leave when they need to, with a clear end to the hosted time. | Return bags, thank guests; separately permission any pictures. | Actual headcount reconciled and property restored. |
| Closeout | Have a simple way to say what worked. | Record host and guest feedback, actual costs and hours; decide repeat, revise or park. | A concrete commercial next step, if any. Enjoyment alone is not recurring demand. |

## Space and service design

Use a simple physical sequence: **welcome and bags → route out → towels and
refreshments → breakfast and seats**. Keep the bathroom visible from arrival.
Keep the return station near the finish without blocking the welcome or kitchen.
A bag label and simple claim procedure are enough; confirm responsibility with
the host. Keep dry towels separate from used ones. Put water where an early
returner can find it without asking. Use a readable menu and ingredient labels.

Select a real shaded or climate-relieved area with enough seats for all guest
places, including breakfast friends. Let people sit together without a pace
hierarchy. Normal café conversation is enough; no required icebreaker, group
reflection or recovery ceremony. No mandatory stretch or shower interval.
Pool, spa and other amenities enter the invitation only when access is agreed.

Visual quality comes from attention: the welcome, the prepared table, clean
service surfaces, considered vessels and towels, and an environment worth
remaining in. Avoid a pile of branded props. A small internal return sheet tells
the kitchen what to expect; it never ranks runners. Real Arrival / Return images
are a future evidence need, not a reason to generate a fictional property.

## Food specification and production

The [return-table brief](FORM_MORNING_RETURN_TABLE_2026-10.md) owns recipes and
recovery rationale. This blueprint owns coordination, quantities and service.
Offer water and the labeled electrolyte option, bananas/cut fruit, then a choice
of bowl, shake or savory breakfast. Orange juice and coffee are optional table
choices. Include reasonable extra food in the scope. Do not serve three full
breakfasts to every guest or sell refills as a hidden second transaction.

Food order = runner choices + breakfast guest choices + staff meals + a stated
service buffer. Buffer size follows supplier lead time, substitutions and the
host's ability to replenish; it is not an unexplained safety percentage. Count
the same person once. Plan water/electrolyte stock from expected service demand;
it is not an instruction for everyone to drink the same quantity.

| Kitchen line to complete | Required record |
|---|---|
| Bowl / shake / savory | Recipe, exact products, yield, portions, carbohydrate/protein totals, allergens, substitution, unit cost and lead time. |
| Drinks and fruit | Products, serving plan, ingredient labels, batch/replenishment plan, cost and storage. |
| All dietary substitutions | Guest order count, complete meal equivalence, cross-contact process and service identification. |
| Breakfast reserve | Quantity and reason, where held, who can release/replenish it. |
| Service | Owner, earliest readiness, last supported return, temperature procedures and cleanup. |

Walk through a very early finish, a late runner, a dairy-free order and a guest
who initially wants only a small drink. Use the host kitchen's food procedures.
Final recipes and recovery claims need kitchen and sports dietitian review;
hospitality feedback does not prove physiological recovery.

## Economics: one scope before three scenarios

Use actual quotes and distinguish full delivery cost from incremental cash
outlay. Existing host payroll may be allocated to the morning without being
new cash spending. Keep that allocation visible. Separate first-time development
from repeat delivery. FORM's fee can include its labor: count either the fee or
its component hours in the same delivery model, not both.

| Planning case | Runners | Breakfast friends, example | Guest places | Staff eating, example | Meal covers before buffer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small | 10 | 2 | 12 | 4 | 16 |
| Working | 12 | 2 | 14 | 4 | 18 |
| Expansion comparison | 15 | 2 | 17 | 4 | 21 |

These columns are editable assumptions. Fifteen runners is not automatically
supported because it appears in the model. Breakfast-only guests and staff are
not automatically paying customers.

For every expense record supplier/duty, quantity or hours, unit rate, total,
who pays, cash vs allocated cost, quote status, source and date. Lines: FORM
fee or labor, additional run support, onsite hosting, kitchen/service labor,
food/drinks for every cover, access, towels/cleaning, transport, optional
photography and other agreed costs. Check taxes and transaction fees with the
actual vendor/payment arrangement. No benchmark proposal supplies verified rates.

- Full delivery cost = all scoped operating costs, including allocated labor.
- Net receipts = host cash funding + ticket receipts − actual transaction fees.
- Event balance = net receipts − full delivery cost.
- Required net ticket receipts = max(0, full delivery cost − host cash funding).
- Required net receipts per paid place = required net ticket receipts / paid
  places. This is a cost allocation, not profit or a quoted price.
- Ticket contribution = net ticket receipts − costs caused by those tickets.
  Fixed costs and nonpaying covers remain part of the event balance.

Compare host-funded (guest tickets zero), hybrid (host and guest funding) and
ticket-only (host funding zero). Same scope, same covers, same cost classification.
Show both first-time and repeat cases. Recalculate if guests cancel or staffing
changes. Do not set a ticket from another agent's unsupported $75 estimate.

## Rehearsal, go/no-go and repeat

One interested host → scope and quotes → route/property walkthrough → service
test → resolve failures → invitation → event → closeout. A standalone rehearsal
before a property conversation is optional and must have permitted access and
a budget; no former café is assumed available.

The [pilot checklist](FORM_HOUSE_PILOT_CHECKLIST_2026-10.md) is the single current
readiness gate. Go requires a named buyer, a supported route/format, independent
onsite cover, first-return service, adequate seats/food, written commercial
scope, a credible funding case and guest/weather/help terms. Revise when the
need exists but the scope does not fit. Park when access, people or economics
cannot be made credible.

Expansion follows observed delivery and real host demand: waits, food readiness,
route support, cost/hours and actual repeat intent. Do not adopt an arbitrary
runner-to-lead ratio, event count or satisfaction score as permission to expand.
