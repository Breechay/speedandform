# FORM Morning · The return table

October 4, 2026 · Proposed food and service design. No kitchen, menu, supplier or
dietitian has been contracted. Owner direction: be specific about water, fresh
orange juice, açaí, protein shakes, fruit and electrolytes, and design recovery
after the long run deliberately.

## The experience

Finish the run. Find shade, a seat, cold drinks and something easy to eat.
Towel off. Sit down to a proper breakfast. Showers are optional when available;
most guests may simply towel off. Food is available from the first return; the
group photograph and last runner never hold it up.

The same table welcomes the shorter-run group and breakfast-only guests.
Appetite and training needs differ. Offer a choice of breakfast and extra food;
do not require every guest to consume a bowl, shake and juice together.

## Proposed menu

| Service | Food / drink | Kitchen specification to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| At the finish | Chilled water, a sodium-containing electrolyte drink, bananas and cut seasonal fruit | Clearly label drink ingredients, sodium and carbohydrate per serving; mix to the manufacturer's instructions. Water remains available. |
| Bowl | Açaí, banana, berries, granola and Greek yogurt; a protein-rich soy alternative | Use actual product labels and weighed portions to provide a substantial carbohydrate breakfast and approximately 20–30 g protein. A small fruit-only bowl does not meet this brief. Granola/toppings on the side. |
| Shake | Banana, oats, milk or soy milk, plus whey or soy protein | A breakfast shake with carbohydrate as well as protein. Approximately 20–30 g protein in the complete recipe, not an extra 30 g on top of the milk. Offer a smaller first pour if appetite is low, then breakfast or a refill. |
| Savory option | Eggs or tofu on toast, with fruit | Cost and calculate a comparable carbohydrate/protein meal. Extra bread available; eggs or tofu alone are not the carbohydrate portion. |
| At the table | Fresh orange juice, seasonal fruit, coffee, extra granola and toast | Juice is an optional carbohydrate-containing drink; it is not the specified sodium replacement. Coffee is optional, never a prerequisite or replacement for food. |

Prototype quantities for the kitchen tasting, not published nutrient claims:

- Bowl: 100 g açaí purée, one banana, 100 g berries, 50–60 g granola and 200 g
  Greek yogurt. Adjust yogurt/type/portion to the protein brief. For a dairy-free
  bowl, use a soy yogurt with verified protein or agree another protein source;
  coconut yogurt is not an automatic nutritional equivalent.
- Shake: 250–300 mL milk or soy milk, one banana, 40–50 g oats and enough whey
  or soy protein to reach the complete-recipe protein brief. Blend and taste for
  texture; keep an oat-free alternative if needed.
- Savory plate: toast, eggs or tofu, fruit; exact portions follow the chosen
  products. Granola, toast, banana or juice can increase carbohydrate without
  another full meal.

Actual carbohydrate/protein totals, allergens, yield, cost and substitutions
must be calculated from the final recipes. These are menu prototypes, not
individual prescriptions. The chef and a sports dietitian should review the
long-run offering and substitutions before claiming recovery performance.

## Recovery basis

Sports Dietitians Australia recommends matching recovery to the athlete,
exercise and time to the next session, combining carbohydrate, protein and
fluids/electrolytes. Its guidance gives 15–30 g protein depending on body size.
Our 20–30 g kitchen brief is a practical starting point within that range;
portion choice and the rest of the day's eating still matter.

Water with food can support rehydration. AIS notes that sodium replacement
helps restore fluid balance, and that electrolyte drinks can be useful when
food is delayed or losses are substantial. Choose a product with known sodium
content. Orange juice and fruit are welcome foods; neither is a complete
substitute for the sodium-containing option.

There is no universally optimal bowl, fixed fluid volume or recovery promise.
Do not prescribe the same drinking target to all guests or encourage forced
drinking. An individual plan for substantial losses belongs with a sports
dietitian. This breakfast supports recovery; it does not replace fuel/fluid
planning before and during the long run, later meals or sleep.

## Production count and timing

Use the [experience blueprint](FORM_HOUSE_EXPERIENCE_BLUEPRINT_2026-10.md) for the
return-based run sheet. Plan quantities from runner choices, breakfast-only
choices and staff meals, then a stated service buffer. Twelve runners plus two
breakfast guests and four staff meals is 18 covers before that buffer, not 12.
Do not count the same person twice. Buffer and replenishment depend on the host.

Breakfast follows actual returns. A shorter run departing around 7 can finish
before 8; do not delay service to the earlier proposed 8–9 breakfast clock.
The onsite host is ready even for an unexpected early turnaround. Confirm
complete-recipe yields, order counts, unit costs and storage with the kitchen.
Keep this document as the food specification rather than making a second menu.

## Service standard for the first morning

- One named host remains at the finish, separate from the running leads.
- Drinks and fruit are in place before the earliest plausible return. Keep them
  stocked through the last arrival; breakfast service follows actual returns.
- Collect bowl/shake/savory preference and dietary needs with reservations;
  guests may change their mind. Include breakfast-only guests in the kitchen count.
- Show ingredients and allergens. Confirm milk, soy, gluten/oats and any nuts in
  granola/protein products; agree substitutions and cross-contact procedures.
- Keep perishable food cold and use the host kitchen's food-service procedures.
  Batch to the return spread so açaí stays cold and shakes are pleasant to drink.
- Test the full service with the host: early return, delayed return, towel supply,
  dairy-free order and a runner who wants only a small drink initially.
- Cost one complete breakfast choice, drinks and reasonable extra food into
  each place. Staying and eating enough must not require a second transaction.

First-morning evidence: Was food ready for the first return? Could runners eat
without waiting for the full group? Were portions enough, substitutions credible
and food still available for late returns? Record appetite, taste, waiting time,
leftovers and costs. Do not infer physiological recovery from hospitality feedback.
