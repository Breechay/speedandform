# FORM: let the athlete shape the work

Owner: Brice. Decision: October 6, 2026.
Status: shared coaching method; not a universal workout prescription.

## The standard

**Success is more useful running, not more suffering.**

We judge the work against the athlete's goal: the pace or effort they can sustain, how they enter and hold it, what it costs them, and whether they recover well enough to continue. Hard work belongs in training. Exhaustion is not the score.

The coach's comparison is the athlete against their earlier work and the current challenge. Four one-mile repetitions becoming four two-mile repetitions doubles work distance. It does not establish doubled fitness or equivalent internal strain. A harder new task can feel hard while the athlete is improving.

A familiar short session can remain the right support late in a block. More elaborate or longer is not automatically more advanced. Change the structure when it serves the next capability, not because basic work has become unfashionable.

## The observation loop

1. Name the goal and the purpose of this session.
2. Record the prescription and what actually happened separately.
3. Preserve the athlete's account: rhythm, pacing decisions, restarting, legs, breathing, confidence and reserve.
4. Consider competing explanations, including preparation, conditions, total load and session format.
5. Choose the smallest useful adjustment; write what would make it useful and what would make us change direction.
6. Review execution and subsequent recovery. Then repeat, extend, shorten, change format or absorb.

This is FORM's practice with its athletes. It does not prove that the same format works for everybody. A useful field decision does not require a proven mechanism, but the mechanism must not be invented to justify the decision.

Broken work and continuous work answer different questions. Recoveries can provide physiological relief while repeated restarting still feels difficult to a particular athlete. Do not presume which format an athlete must experience as harder.

## How the week fits together

Tuesday develops the principal race-relevant capability. Thursday supplies complementary work only while it supports the whole week. Saturday develops longer-running tolerance; any fast finish is additional quality, not invisible easy mileage. Recovery, ordinary easy running and familiar fueling are part of the program.

A short-to-long progression can change total work, longest uninterrupted bout, number or duration of recoveries, pace, preceding fatigue or frequency. Identify which is changing. Do not increase them all merely because the calendar advances.

For the half-marathon studies, eight controlled continuous miles can be meaningful progress. Nine or ten may become later options, not automatic requirements. The goal is a well-executed race, not collecting every possible training distance. An eight-mile result does not guarantee thirteen-point-one; a ten- or twelve-mile near-race effort is not a required price of admission.

Longer running below race effort can support endurance and rhythm. Use **slower than race pace** or **below race effort** when that is intended; do not use the ambiguous phrase 'sub race pace'. Faster-than-race work has a separate purpose and dose.

### Current use

For an athlete whose sustained effort and recovery justify it, the library includes a modest continuous block, a long first piece with a short second piece such as 20+8 or 20+12, and eventually a longer uninterrupted effort. These are selectable formats, not a mandatory ladder.

A possible later sequence is **20 continuous → 20+8 → 20+12 → 25–30 continuous**, with repeat or absorption weeks as needed. Total minutes fall when the uninterrupted block grows; that is an explicit tradeoff, not a contradiction. Longer 35–45-minute controlled work belongs only where intensity, training history and the rest of the week support it. It must not become 45 minutes at an assumed one-hour race pace simply because an old label says threshold.

## Lactate and claims

Training can improve lactate transport, oxidation and sustainable exercise performance. Lactate is also useful fuel, not simply waste to be removed. We do not infer improved clearance from a watch trace, a float pace, or completion of a named workout. Even a blood concentration is a balance of appearance and removal, not a direct measurement of clearance rate.

The observable coaching outcome is controlled useful running. A claim about running economy, VO2max, critical speed or lactate kinetics needs the corresponding evidence. Longer threshold work is a plausible tool, not a uniquely proven answer to José's difficult session.
