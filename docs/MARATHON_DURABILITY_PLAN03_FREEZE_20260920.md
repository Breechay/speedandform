## Governing sequel

**Act I · Race Pace Durability** increases how long an athlete can hold an already reachable capability.

**Act II · Marathon Durability** increases how much running can happen before the athlete still has access to that capability.

The progression is primarily the **context before the capability**, not an endless increase in specific-volume.

## Weekly doctrine

> **Tuesday remembers the capability.**  
> **Thursday protects the ceiling.**  
> **Saturday asks whether it remains.**

- Tuesday = specific contact, not a second durability experiment.
- Thursday = threshold / aerobic power / FORM speed only when it does not steal from the governing Saturday question.
- Hard Saturday → quiet Thursday.
- Easy Saturday → more room for ceiling development.
- Saturday owns the major marathon-durability claim.

## Three jobs

### Ceiling
Faster performance above marathon demand: threshold, aerobic power, economy, speed.

### Specific reserve
The faster capability supported by current evidence and carried forward from Act I.

### Marathon rhythm
The pace/rhythm that can actually be distributed across 26.2.

**Specific reserve is not automatically marathon race pace.**

## Reusable Plan 03 arc

**Access → Embed → Re-access → Closing 10K → Rhythm → Confirm → Race**

Principal Saturday logic:
- W4 · late access
- W5 · leave and re-access
- W8 · same reserve after more prior running
- W9 · re-access deeper
- W11 · principal Closing-10K reserve experiment
- W12 · separate marathon-rhythm + fueling rehearsal
- W13 · smaller absorption/cost check

The principal rehearsal deliberately stops short of recreating the full race-day 20-mile prelude. Training needs enough prior running to make the late-access signal meaningful without becoming a full-marathon simulation. Race day is the full test.

## Fueling law

From the first marathon-specific long run, fueling is part of the prescription.

Record:
- product
- amount
- timing
- tolerance
- material deviations

A late fade under improvised or failed fueling does not cleanly answer the durability question.

## Evidence grammar

For major asks:

**OBSERVED → READ → NEXT**

Minimum useful record:
- prescribed vs filed work
- segment pace / stability
- athlete RPE/report
- fueling adherence/tolerance
- meaningful conditions
- HR when interpretable
- mechanics only when actually observed
- 24–72 h recovery after major asks
- coach read
- next decision

No durability score.  
No readiness score.  
No weighted composite.

## Study protocol

**The method is frozen. The answer is not.**

The Last 10K is a prospective two-athlete coaching case study, not a controlled trial. It can document what happened to Hope and José under this method and challenge the coaching model. It cannot establish population-level causality.

### Hypotheses
- H01 · Late access
- H02 · Field-performance ceiling preservation
- H03 · Restraint
- H04 · Cost / absorption

### Orlando decision framework
Each athlete is read independently using:
- late output
- cost
- race distribution
- context / failure mode
- recovery
- athlete report

Possible decisions:
- keep assignment
- redefine reserve/rhythm
- protect or delay the next Ask
- branch the athletes

No single field metric automatically decides the verdict.

### Recovery protection
Materially abnormal recovery triggers review before the next major Ask. The response may reduce, replace, delay, or proceed only after normal easy-running function returns.

### Amendments
Post-Orlando changes preserve:
- original claim/prescription
- date
- triggering evidence
- revised read

Do not silently rewrite the pre-outcome record.
