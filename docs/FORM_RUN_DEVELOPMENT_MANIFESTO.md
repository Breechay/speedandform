# FORM Run Development Manifesto

**Decision date:** September 17, 2026 · terminology refined September 27, 2026  
**Owner:** Brice / Speed & Form  
**Status:** canonical source doctrine for Run Development, FORM coaching language, homepage philosophy and downstream product interpretation.

## Why this document exists

This document preserves how Brice actually sees and coaches running so future site, app, plan, ad and agent work does not drift back into generic coaching language.

For any public or athlete-facing description of Run Development, read this before writing copy. Where an older marketing document describes the same idea more generically, this document controls the philosophy and voice.

The public surface may stay very short. The depth belongs here.

---

# The north star

> **Reveal what wants to be set free.**

Run Development is not mainly the delivery of workouts. It is the development of the runner.

Brice looks for ease, fluidity and connection. When running looks or feels effortful in a way that does not belong to the task, something is fighting the movement. The job is to notice it, decide what matters most, change one thing at a time, then repeat the better pattern until it belongs to the athlete.

Running can be meditative when the runner is not constantly fighting it. Everything starts to click.

A hard workout can still be hard. Ease does not mean every run is easy. It means the runner can meet the work with confidence instead of unnecessary resistance.

Brice's image for the desired feeling:

> **Like a kite upon the wind.**

# I develop runners

A concise technical descriptor for the practice is:

> **RUN DEVELOPMENT · GAIT RETRAINING · PROGRAMMING**

`Run Development` remains the umbrella. `Gait retraining` names the deliberate movement-change work. `Programming` names the authored training structure. The descriptor supports the philosophy; it does not replace `Run Development` as the public promise.

## 1. Look for ease

The first read is not a checklist of textbook faults.

Look for:
- fluidity;
- coordination;
- what feels out of tune;
- where movement looks restricted or unnecessarily effortful;
- whether the athlete looks like they are fighting the run;
- whether the work appears connected or fragmented.

Some differences are harmless. Some cost the athlete more. The useful question is not `does this look ideal?` It is `what is getting in the way?`

## 2. Change what matters most

Everyone is different.

Two athletes can show a similar movement pattern and receive different coaching. One athlete may need arm-swing work because the sling relationship is limiting the whole movement. Another athlete with similarly limited arm motion may already move fluidly enough that changing it is not the highest-value intervention.

Do not change five things at once.

Find the change that is most likely to free the most.

This is judgment, not template matching.

## 3. Layer the work

There is usually more than one cue available. That does not mean the athlete should receive all of them at once.

FORM layers changes so the runner can feel, practice and own each one. The sequence matters.

A cue is useful only when it changes the runner in front of you.

## 4. Repetition teaches the body

Frequency matters.

Near-daily running may be only ten minutes. That still counts. Repeated exposure gives the runner more chances to reinforce better movement, better choices and better habits.

Progress can look like:
- ten minutes becoming thirty, then forty-five;
- 400s becoming 800s, then a mile;
- a cue becoming automatic;
- a movement becoming stable enough to survive more speed or distance.

Distance and pace are not always the first priority. Correct exposure, repeated often enough to stick, can matter more.

### Coach the runner who exists

Repetition matters, but a training plan must be built for the athlete's actual life, habits, willingness and demonstrated follow-through. Do not assume someone will suddenly become a person who never misses a run because the plan asks for it. Make high-value sessions clear and attainable, then learn from what the athlete actually does. Missed support work is not a moral failure or a debt to repay.

This is not a ban on developing consistency. If an athlete wants more frequency, or shows readiness for an achievable change, the coach can nudge one manageable exposure every few weeks, review how it fits, and progress from there. Do not force a behavioral transformation the athlete does not want or cannot currently sustain. Improve the runner within their real circumstances, while leaving room for those circumstances and habits to change voluntarily.

## 5. Form is not decoration

FORM treats mechanics as part of performance, not as aesthetics.

Small changes are multiplied by thousands of steps.

Examples Brice watches and coaches include:
- shoulders held high or tight;
- restricted arm action when it meaningfully limits the runner;
- cadence and landing timing;
- hip and single-leg stability;
- trunk organization;
- anything that creates visible or reported unnecessary effort.

Strength can be part of form development. Bulgarian split squats, single-leg RDLs and other structural work may be used to build the stability the runner needs to express better movement.

### Gait retraining

`Gait retraining` is the technical name for the movement-change work already inside Run Development. In FORM, it means deliberately changing a runner's posture, timing, mechanics or movement strategy through a useful cue, practice, feedback and repeated exposure until the new pattern can hold under running load.

Keep the terms distinct:
- **running form** is the runner's movement expression;
- **gait analysis** is observing or measuring that movement;
- **gait retraining** is the deliberate process of changing a useful part of the pattern;
- **running economy** is the energetic cost of running at a given speed and is an outcome or measurement, not another name for gait retraining.

Gait retraining does not mean forcing every athlete toward one ideal visual model. The selective-intervention rule still governs: change what has a meaningful cost in context, then retest.

### Coaching belief versus public claim

Brice's operating belief is that form is often a major limiter and that athletes commonly get faster when their form improves.

Preserve that belief internally.

Do **not** turn it into a universal public causal claim such as `fix your form and you will get faster` unless the statement is tied to measured evidence for a specific athlete. Public language should distinguish observation, coach interpretation and proven result.

## 6. The athlete's internal state is part of the work

Coaching is not only pace, splits and movement.

Brice pays attention to:
- the choices an athlete makes;
- the thoughts they bring into the run;
- their mental approach;
- their outlook;
- their attitude toward the work;
- confidence under effort.

The runner's internal state changes how the work is executed and learned.

### Signature cue: stop negotiating with yourself

One recurring Brice cue is:

> **Stop negotiating with yourself.**

The original field wording is preserved as: `its not much you just got to stop negotiating with yourself`.

This is not a command to ignore pain, injury signals or a bad training decision. It is a mindset cue for the moment when the athlete has already established that the work is appropriate and the remaining problem is bargaining with effort. Brice coaches the conversation underneath the running: notice the negotiation, make the next useful decision, and reengage.

José Santos De-Soto supplied a clear example on October 6, 2026. During 4 × 2 miles with two-minute floats, he reported nearly stopping after the second repetition, later remembered this cue, and described continuing through his own internal conversation. He also said he still thinks about the original text often. The carryover matters: a coaching phrase has done its job when the athlete can use it without the coach standing beside them.

## 7. Running is bigger than the workout

Running is not merely something you do.

It can become part of how you live, like walking or breathing: a capacity, a practice and a gift you were handed.

Run Development should leave more behind than completed sessions. The athlete should leave with applied knowledge, habits, ways of thinking and things they can recognize for themselves later.

The goal is not dependence on the coach.

The goal is a runner who sees more, feels more, understands more and owns more.

# What eight weeks should impart

Eight weeks is not valuable because eight calendar weeks passed.

The athlete should accumulate:
- repeated exposure;
- better movement habits;
- better judgment about effort;
- stronger mental habits;
- useful cues that have been practiced enough to mean something;
- knowledge they can apply without needing every answer supplied;
- greater confidence in the way they run;
- more ownership of their running.

Brice often hears later that a cue, phrase, habit or way of thinking from coaching continued to help long after the session where it was first introduced. That carryover is part of the product.

# The coaching loop

The underlying loop is simple:

**See → choose → cue → practice → feel → repeat → own.**

Numbers still matter. Athlete report still matters. But neither replaces judgment.

The runner is not a set of metrics and not a movement score.

# Editorial law

## Say it once

If a sentence makes the point, do not immediately explain the point again.

Avoid the pattern:
1. claim;
2. explanation of claim;
3. reassurance about explanation;
4. second explanation of what happens next.

Let layout, sequencing, imagery and the next interaction carry some of the meaning.

## Identity language must earn its place

Generic language is acceptable for utility copy such as buttons, prices, instructions and form labels.

Generic language is **not** good enough for the sections that explain what FORM believes or why Brice coaches differently.

If a sentence could sit unchanged on hundreds of coaching websites, it should not define FORM's identity.

## Reveal information when it becomes relevant

Do not front-load:
- reassurance about payment when nobody has tried to pay;
- assessment details before the person has started an inquiry;
- process narration the interface already makes obvious;
- repeated service/location labels after the page has established them.

The visitor should learn by moving through the page, not by reading an instruction manual before the first scroll.

## Proof has a place

Proof matters, but proof does not have to interrupt the philosophy immediately after the hero.

On the homepage, establish what FORM is and how it sees running first. Verified athlete evidence can appear later, where it supports an idea the visitor now understands.

# September 17 refinement — depth, ease and ownership

## Ease is not easy

Ease is not a synonym for low effort.

A hard workout can demand a great deal from the athlete and still look organized. FORM distinguishes effort from unnecessary resistance. The desired quality is confidence, connection and control inside the work, not the absence of work.

## The eye and the instrument

Brice describes the first recognition of friction as similar to hearing an out-of-tune instrument. Something stands out before the full explanation is available.

That first read is an invitation to investigate, not a diagnosis. Observation is tested against the runner, the task, athlete report and what happens after a change.

## Form is multiplied by every step

A useful movement change can matter because running repeats it thousands of times. The same principle is why FORM does not change movement merely for appearance. The intervention has to earn its place in repeated running.

The goal is not a universal visual model. The goal is less unnecessary cost in the runner in front of you.

## The autonomy standard

The work should stay with the athlete after coaching ends.

A successful engagement leaves behind judgment, not only completed sessions. The runner should increasingly recognize an out-of-tune note for themselves, distinguish effort from unnecessary struggle, understand why a cue or training decision matters, and need fewer reminders because the practice has become their own.

## Public architecture

Use three depths instead of repeating the manifesto everywhere:

1. **Homepage:** felt philosophy and enough method to understand the relationship. Keep the hero sparse. Establish ease, selective intervention, repetition and ownership through short observations.
2. **/the-method:** the deeper public explanation. This is the home for the out-of-tune instrument, the next-rep test, form multiplied by every step, frequency as exposure, internal state and the autonomy standard.
3. **Manifesto and coach-facing systems:** the full doctrine, evidence boundaries, raw language and operating rules.

The homepage should point to /the-method when a visitor wants the deeper thinking. A specific Lab study should not carry the burden of explaining the whole coaching philosophy.

## Identity language to avoid

Do not use generic phrases as FORM identity language merely because they are true:

- unlock your potential;
- reach the next level;
- transform your running;
- train smarter;
- holistic coaching;
- accountability and support;
- tailored to your goals;
- built around your lifestyle.

Utility copy can still be plain and generic when that is the clearest way to label a button, price, field or service.

## Observation-led programming

**Success is more useful running, not more suffering.** Brice judges progress against the athlete's goal and the changing challenge. The format is part of the question: maintaining continuous pace and repeatedly settling back into it need not feel the same. Preserve the athlete's account, choose a useful adjustment, and review execution plus recovery. One athlete's response improves how we observe the others; it does not automatically become their workout.

See [FORM observation-led progression](FORM_OBSERVATION_LED_PROGRESSION.md) for the current decision rules, threshold-program lineage and study-specific applications.


### Protect the question

A week is easier to coach when the athlete knows what matters most.

- Name the primary session.
- Protect the day before it.
- Do not add work because the athlete feels good.
- **Thursday / secondary quality is earned by recovery, not owed by the calendar.**
- Count hard strength, HYROX and races as real quality load.
- Missing a support session creates no debt.

For the current half-marathon studies, Tuesday often owns the first specific question. That is a common pattern, not a universal law. If the main question moves, the protection moves.

Every active athlete should have a lightweight study record using the shared observation grammar. Only the cases with enough evidence, permission and teaching value need a feature Labs story. See [Athlete study operating model](studies/ATHLETE-STUDY-OPERATING-MODEL-20261006.md).


## November 6, 2026 · One development thread across distances

The same FORM progression applies to 5K, 10K, half marathon, and marathon while changing emphasis: **raise the speed ceiling; establish sustainable threshold and race-pace control; lengthen continuity; reduce reliance on complete recoveries; place meaningful work later; keep the week absorbable.** The athlete's event determines the dose and test, not the other way around.

The sub-1:30 half-marathon group provides a coach-observed comparison: stable threshold-work pace has sometimes been **20–30 seconds per mile faster than evidenced half-marathon capability pace**. Treat that as a bounded field observation for athlete-by-athlete examination, not a universal calculator or a laboratory threshold determination.

For half-marathon development, build continuous threshold and HMP ownership before progressing HMP repeats from easy recovery toward shortened recovery and controlled floats, then late-run expressions. For marathon development, earn a comfortable two-hour long-run base, 60–70 minutes continuous at evidenced MP, and only then substantial 30-minute alternations or long MP repeats. A future marathon-pace-specific workout requires **at least 30 cumulative minutes at MP**; easy and taper days do not falsely claim to be MP workouts. Preserve the single-week recovery budget.

New FORM authoring must not use the word **“rhythm”** or its plural in athlete-facing programming or coach-authored guidance. Choose accurate alternatives such as pace, timing, cadence, composure or continuity; protect immutable quotations and filed results. New copy is blocked in the session-editorial guard; published legacy copy still needs a separate versioned migration.

Read the [Cross-Distance Development Thread](coaching/FORM_RUN_DEVELOPMENT_DISTANCE_THREAD_20261106.md), [5K and 10K Session Canon](coaching/FORM_5K_10K_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md), [Half-Marathon Session Canon](coaching/FORM_HALF_MARATHON_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md), and [Marathon Session Canon](coaching/FORM_MARATHON_SESSION_CANON_20261106.md). These are philosophy and session-authoring references, not live plan changes.


### Aerobic foundation and pace ownership · November 6 follow-up

The ordinary easy-running capacity must extend beyond a typical short workout's elapsed time including preparation and cooldown. Build genuinely easy standalone runs toward 60–75 minutes where appropriate, alongside the half's 75–90-minute easy long-run platform or a prepared marathoner's absorbable two-hour base. A short recovery run remains a legitimate separate purpose.

Our specific progression has **two axes**: longer uninterrupted pace ownership, and less complete recovery between substantial segments. Starter continuous `5 min pace / 5 min easy` or `1 mi pace / 1 mi easy` alternations can bridge toward sustained pace; they are not classic stopped mile-repeat workouts. Later controlled floats are a distinct higher-cost variant, not renamed easy running. Six continuous HMP miles may be a useful selective durability test; eight miles has more cost and is never compulsory.

If an Orlando half result supplies ~90 minutes at clock pace also suitable for Donna MP, it may answer the continuous-pace question without redoing an hour of MP for its own sake. It cannot answer whether the athlete can fuel and sustain the final marathon 10K. These links, progression doses and evidence boundaries are detailed in [Aerobic Base to Race-Pace Durability](coaching/FORM_AEROBIC_BASE_RACE_PACE_DURABILITY_BRIDGE_20261106.md).
