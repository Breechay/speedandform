## Evidence-to-design

Forge Sculpt Phase 1 (source: `forge-sculpt/train/data/forge-portal-programs.json`) was shoulder/upper-oriented: Shoulder A, Upper Push, Pull, Shoulder B, Arms + Core, Full Upper. Phase 2 added Glute Hinge, Glute Thrust, and Lower Pump/Core, while retaining Pull/Push/Full Upper. **Do not stack those 5–6 days on top of this running block.** Borrow the effective exercises and compress weekly strength into two distinct core workouts, with optional accessory work.

Published physiology: 2024 running-economy meta-analysis of 31 studies supports high-load and some plyometric approaches for running economy, with potentially different effects by speed (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11052887/). Concurrent-training review finds substantially overlapping endurance/strength sessions may impair lower-body maximal strength adaptation in trained people, with less effect when sessions are separated by more than two hours (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053170/). Neither licenses maximal lifts and jumping at any stage of fracture recovery. Keep hard running, heavy lower strength and high-impact plyometrics coordinated.

## Proposed workout bank

These are **complete standalone candidate workouts** using equipment available at Muscle Beach or existing Forge gym/home equipment. Select sessions as needed; the menu is NOT a directive to do every session every week. All listed sets are working sets after 5–10 min general warm-up plus progressively loaded rehearsal sets on the first barbell movement. Aim for about **2–3 repetitions in reserve** and clean mechanics, not grinders or testing a 1RM. Rest ~2 min on main barbell lifts; 60–90 seconds on accessory work. These are **initial draft loads and set counts**, to be reconciled against actual Forge loads and injury tolerance before formal assignment.

### A · Muscle Beach: The Frame (upper body, 45–60 min)

| Exercise | Working prescription | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pull-up, strict pronated or neutral | 3 × 4–8 | Lats and upper-back size; add quality reps before weight |
| Incline barbell press | 3 × 6–8 | Upper chest development |
| Barbell row, pronated | 3 × 6–8 | Mid-back and lat mass, strict torso |
| Military press | 2 × 6–8 | Shoulder strength; preserve current deltoid progress |
| Flat barbell press | 2 × 8–10 | Chest volume without more total heavy work |
| Plate lateral raises | 2 × 12–20 | Forge Phase 1 shoulder aesthetics |
| Hanging knee raise, posterior pelvic tilt | 2 × 8–12 | Abdominal control |

Pull-ups are a primary lift rather than a finish when overheated. Strict row position, no lumbar heaving. Do not add a separate shoulder sculpt workout to this day just because it feels fun; this already covers pressing and pulling. Mix row grips between weeks, not within the same set.

### B · Muscle Beach: The Chassis (lower + core, 45–55 min)

| Exercise | Working prescription | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Front squat | 3 × 5 to establish current repeatability | Quad, trunk, leg-drive strength |
| Barbell RDL | 2 × 6–8 | Posterior chain/hamstrings without heavy deadlift volume |
| Barbell hip thrust or floor bridge | 2 × 8–10 | Glutes without more speed work |

3 × 5 is deliberately a lower initial *working* dose than the athlete's familiar 5 × 5 front-squat preference. Preserve 5 × 5 as a known option after confirming compatibility with weeklong running and lower-leg recovery, not a mandatory first-week progression. Do not heavy deadlift and RDL and squat all at high volume on the same day. A full conventional deadlift may replace, not supplement, RDL in a future individually approved session.

### D · Elastic preparation (8–12 min; no bouncing required)

Initial candidate for now:
- Brisk 3-min walk and comfortable ankle/hip motions.
- Isometric straight-knee calf raise hold: 2 × 20 seconds per side.
- Isometric bent-knee calf raise hold: 2 × 20 seconds per side.
- Supported fast-but-controlled marching, low amplitude: 2 × 15–20 m.
- Gentle single-leg balance or low step-up: 2 × 20 seconds per side.

Later, only once cleared for impact progression and tolerating the running/lifting stress with no concerning left tibial response, create a *separate* low-contact plyometric progression. Start with small two-foot pogo contacts or skips at low volume rather than automatically adding depth jumps, single-leg bounds, sprint starts or cleans. Exposure progressed by symptoms, impact dose and 24–48h response; current 37+ mi week cannot by itself establish safe jumping tolerance.

## Progress without rigid escalation

- On main upper lifts: repeat the same load until reps are clean, then consider small incremental load gains while leaving reps in reserve.
- On front squat, RDL, glute and calf: hold the current work dose for at least 1–2 exposures and assess how running feels 24–48 hours later; do not add more sets, more speed minutes and more mileage simultaneously.
- Log: working load/reps/RIR; 24–48h tibia and general fatigue; voluntary speed exposures; Garmin VO₂ max as a *device estimate*; pace–HR relationship at reasonably matched conditions; contact/stride at matched paces; 7–10-mile comfort; and consistent bodyweight readings where available.
- Physique: better back definition requires both back muscle development and overall body composition change, not targeted fat burning. Start by observing comparable morning-weight averages and nutrition adequacy; aggressive deficits while returning to faster running and healing bone can undermine adaptation.

## Open clinical and program authority

Repeated plyometric or explosive Olympic-lifting exposure is not ratified without updated clinician/PT clearance for the surgically repaired tibia, especially high-impact loading. Stop and obtain assessment for new focal tibial pain, limping, persistent worsening swelling or pain that grows through the workout. The document is not a medical clearance.
