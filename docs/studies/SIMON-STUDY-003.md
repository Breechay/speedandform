## The question

Can faster running become continuous at an acceptable cost, and later remain available after prior running, while high-end field performance is developed?

Do not reduce this to fixing a failed half marathon. April's 1:26:13 was a successful, deliberately conservative race aimed at sub-1:30 according to Brice. Ambitions of 1:15-1:20 during preparation do not establish fitness at those times. Faster-long-run fading is a separate coach observation. An even race neither disproves that observation nor identifies its cause.

A stronger hold with an unchanged 5K is a successful half-marathon-development result. Neither curve is required to improve at every gate. Cost and recovery matter even if a headline result improves. This is a single-athlete coaching study, not a causal trial. Economy, sustainable aerobic speed, conditions, pacing, fueling and fatigue tolerance can all contribute; none has been isolated as the one limiter.

## Read, then choose

| Evidence | Coaching emphasis |
|---|---|
| Faster ceiling and better-controlled hold, normal recovery | Preserve useful faster work; extend sustained work selectively. |
| Better ceiling, but working pace expensive early while fresh | Reassess the working pace; favor controlled threshold-oriented accumulation over more speed. |
| Similar ceiling, but more controlled continuity | This counts as progress. Protect and extend the hold; do not chase a 5K result to validate it. |
| Neither improves and recovery is costly | Reduce demand and review conditions, training continuity, fueling, HYROX and health signals. |
| Unclear result, ordinary recovery | Check comparability and repeat a suitable observation; do not automatically add intensity or declare failure. |

Separate cost that is high from the start, cost that grows with fresh continuous duration, and cost that appears only after prior running. Those imply different next questions. For a later durability comparison, standardize the easy lead-in, pace, route, fuel/fluid and conditions. Observe when control changes if it does; do not require running to failure.

When Saturday becomes a substantial specific workout, reduce or replace quality elsewhere. A hard HYROX session is part of the quality budget. Do not classify it as free easy conditioning. No automatic peak mileage target. The working hypothesis can change without changing the study's name.

No automatic 80-90 km/week requirement. No claim that Saumur will be cool. No universal cadence or invented reserve index. No direct VO2max or threshold claim from field workouts or watch estimates.
