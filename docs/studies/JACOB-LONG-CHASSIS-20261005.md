# Jacob · The Long Chassis · FORM Human Performance Study 005

Owner: Brice Ikouebe  
Opened: 2026-10-05  
Public case-study consent: confirmed by coach from the athlete before publication.

## Question

**Can an experienced ultra runner preserve his aerobic engine while rebuilding the strength, stability and tissue tolerance needed to use it comfortably again — then extend that chassis toward a 100-mile adventure in roughly two years?**

## Known opening context

Athlete-reported:
- experienced endurance / ultra background;
- recent 30 km run completed, with the finish described as miserable and the right knee painful;
- knee and hip problems are currently more limiting than perceived aerobic fitness;
- Garmin-estimated VO2 max is currently about 50; athlete reports his historical device estimate never exceeded roughly 55;
- Palermo Marathon in Sicily is planned for the third week of November, with the stated goal to finish rather than compete;
- current preferred cross-training includes spin bike, elliptical and stair stepper;
- athlete believes he needs a consistent leg-strength, stability and core routine and reports inconsistent adherence to existing physio exercises;
- long horizon: a 100-mile race in about two years, plus 1–3 marathons per year and a larger event roughly every two years.

Treat all of the above as athlete report, not diagnosis or verified medical history.

## Working theory

The first problem is not to manufacture more cardiovascular intensity. The athlete appears able to create large endurance exposures already. The opening block should determine whether improved unilateral strength, trunk control, hip capacity and consistent rehab-compatible work make ordinary running cheaper and more durable.

FORM and FORGE should not compete:
- **FORM** owns running and low-impact aerobic prescription.
- **FORGE** owns strength / stability / chassis development.
- **Study 005** decides which system needs emphasis as the athlete responds.

## Opening phase

Do not freeze a strength prescription until the intake captures:
1. the current knee/hip symptom pattern and what changes it;
2. the actual PT/physio exercises and any restrictions;
3. ultra / marathon history and recent normal training volume;
4. current equipment and access;
5. Palermo constraints and what "finish" should mean for the build.

A 4–6 week chassis block is the working direction, not yet the final prescription.

## Evidence rule

Pain, PT instructions, race history, Garmin estimates and athlete impressions remain distinct. Do not infer a diagnosis from a training response. Persistent or worsening pain, swelling, instability, or gait change should be escalated to the athlete's clinician/PT rather than trained through.

## Long horizon

The 100-mile idea is a direction, not a current load target. The first success condition is much smaller:

**Can the athlete train consistently without the chassis becoming the reason the engine cannot be used?**
