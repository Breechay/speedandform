# Same Pace, Different Problem

Owner: Brice. Athletes: Elijah + Simon. Paired chapter opened October 4, 2026.
Public page: `/labs/same-pace-different-problem/`.

## The question

Two runners are developing toward a similar half-marathon pace neighborhood. Elijah has a November race; Simon has a longer development season. How do the coaching decisions differ, and what does each athlete's response teach us?

This is a paired observational coaching study, not a controlled comparison of training methods. Neither athlete's result proves what would have happened under the other's plan. A common aspirational pace does not establish equivalent fitness.

## The two cases

**Elijah:** Savannah Half Marathon, November 14, 2026. Sub-1:20 remains a conditional A-goal. Prioritize recoverable specific work, pacing, fueling rehearsal and race execution. The October 27 continuous six-mile session informs the opening strategy; it cannot certify the race outcome.

**Simon:** Semi-Marathon de la Loire, Saumur, May 16, 2027. The 1:19–1:23 range remains a development envelope, not a forecast. Establish fresh capability and recovery cost, account for HYROX, then develop race-specific distance ownership over the months.

Simon's later specific plan should borrow the distance-based logic of Race Pace Durability / Speed That Endures: broken work, longer continuous race-pace distance, then selected race-pace work after prior running. Short successful exposures do not resolve Brice's pacing-trust/durability hypothesis. A possible culminating twelve-mile continuous rehearsal remains conditional, not currently assigned and not a guarantee. Threshold/ceiling support may be timed; race-pace ownership is distance-based.
