How should I warm up before running?

Start gently. Use a few familiar movements if they help, then ease into the run. Before faster work, build gradually toward the effort your session asks for.

A simple sequence.

  1. 01

    Move gently

    Begin with walking or very easy jogging. Give yourself time to settle.

  2. 02

    Choose familiar movements

    Use one or two of the options below if they help. Skip unfamiliar drills.

  3. 03

    Build toward the day’s work

    Stay easy for an easy run. Before faster work, follow your session’s gradual warm-up and any prescribed accelerations.

There is no fixed routine you must perform before every run. Judge preparation by whether the movement feels ready and the session is appropriate. [1]

Use rehearsal, not a magic switch.

“Activation” is often used for short preparation exercises. It is a coaching label. A warm-up does not replace a strength program or enough recovery.

Choose one or two familiar movements.

Choose 1 or 2 familiar options. Do 1 gentle set, then check whether you feel ready to ease into the run.

01

Bridge

6–10 easy repetitions

Set up: Lie on your back with bent knees and feet on the floor.

Do: Lift and lower your hips through a comfortable range.

Cue: Lift the hips comfortably.

Feel: Light work in your hips and buttocks, without back strain.

Keep it gentle; stop if your back strains.

02

Dead bug

4–6 easy repetitions per side

Set up: Lie on your back with knees over hips and arms reaching up.

Do: Lower one heel and the opposite arm a little, then return. Alternate.

Cue: Keep the reach small.

Feel: Gentle abdominal work while your back stays comfortable.

Use a small reach and normal breathing.

03

Calf raise

8–10 easy repetitions

Set up: Hold stable support and stand on both feet.

Do: Raise your heels smoothly, then lower them.

Cue: Rise and lower smoothly.

Feel: Work in your calves without Achilles or joint pain.

Keep it easy enough that your calves still feel ready to run.

04

Band side steps

3–5 easy steps each way

Set up: Use a light band if familiar, or skip this option.

Do: Take small sideways steps, then return.

Cue: Take small easy steps.

Feel: Light work around your outer hips; it should not tire you for the run.

Avoid a deep squat or heavy band that makes this a tiring set.

These are options, not a required circuit. Finish before they feel like a workout. [2]

When is a different warm-up useful?

Faster work may need more gradual preparation than an easy run. Follow that session’s instruction. Keep the movements familiar; do not make the warm-up harder just to progress it.

What if I still feel flat?

Check sleep, food, illness, soreness and the week’s total work. You may need a shorter easy run or rest. A warm-up is not a test you must pass to keep the original workout. Decide what fits today →

When should you stop?

Stop the set if you lose control or must hold your breath. Reduce the amount or choose an easier version. Stop for sharp pain, joint pain or symptoms that change how you move; persistent or recurring symptoms need assessment.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Preparation should help the first part of the run.
  2. Use familiar movements, not a tiring circuit.
  3. Feeling heavy is a reason to check readiness too.
Your legs feel heavy. Should you add a longer activation routine until they feel ready?

Not automatically. Try gentle movement, then check. If fatigue remains, adjust the run. Extra preparation does not erase a recovery problem.
