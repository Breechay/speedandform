How easy should an easy run feel?

Easy enough to hold a comfortable conversation. You should finish with room to keep going, not feel as though you have completed a workout.

By Brice IkouebeUpdated September 17, 2026

Start with effort. Check the pace second.

On an easy run, speak a few full sentences without having to organize each one around a breath. You are still exercising; breathing faster than at rest is normal. The useful distinction is whether conversation feels comfortable. The talk test is a practical guide, not a laboratory measurement of your training zones. [1] [2]

Breathing

Full sentences, comfortably.

Movement

A rhythm you do not have to force.

Finish

You could continue, but the run is done.

A slower pace than your friend’s is not a problem to solve. Neither is a slower pace than last week’s. Use the effort you have today.

Easy running is training, not filler.

Lower-intensity running lets you accumulate endurance work without making every session a hard one. Research supports giving a substantial part of an endurance runner’s training to lower-intensity work. It does not establish one perfect percentage for every runner. [3]

In FORM, I use easy days to support the work around them. A run that leaves you too tired for the rest of the week has missed that job, even when the average pace looks good.

Easy and recovery are not synonyms. A long run can be easy in intensity and still be a substantial load because of its duration.

A simple easy run.

Thirty minutes is an example, not an entry requirement. Use a duration you already handle. Short runs and planned walking breaks are legitimate ways to build the practice.

When easy does not feel easy.

You can only say a few words.
Slow down or walk until conversation is comfortable. Do not defend the number on your watch.
It is hotter or more humid.
Choose a cooler time or shaded route, adjust the pace, and shorten the run when needed. Heat adds physiological strain; yesterday’s pace is not today’s obligation. [4]
Your legs are heavy from training.
Try a shorter, gentler run or rest. Easy running is not a requirement to run through accumulated fatigue.
Pain appears or changes your stride.
Stop the run rather than trying to coach yourself through it. Persistent or recurring pain needs assessment. [5]

Questions that come up.

Should I stay in Zone 2?

Use the definition behind the zone, not just its name. Zone systems and watch settings differ. Comfortable speech and manageable effort are useful checks alongside heart rate; neither is a substitute for an individualized threshold assessment. [1] [2]

Will running slowly make me slow?

Easy running is one part of a training week, not the whole prescription. Keep easy days easy, then put purposeful faster work where your training supports it. Read the threshold guide for that different job.

How do I know it is working?

Look for repeatable weeks and an effort you can manage, not a personal best on every easy run. Compare similar routes and conditions over time. A single unusually fast or low-heart-rate run does not establish a new fitness level.

Sources & context

The examples and decisions above are FORM coaching guidance. The research below supports the stated principles, not an individual prescription or a guaranteed result.

  3. Esteve-Lanao et al. (2007)Impact of training intensity distribution on performance in endurance athletesA small randomized study of subelite runners; supports substantial low-intensity work, not a universal weekly ratio.
