How do I practice keeping my trunk steady?

Practice keeping your trunk controlled while an arm, leg or resistance tries to move it. Start with a small amount you can do while breathing normally.

Follow the movements.

01

Dead bug

6–8 slow repetitions per side

Set up: Lie on your back. Bring your knees over your hips and reach your arms up.

Do: Lower one heel and the opposite arm a little, then return. Alternate sides. Use a smaller movement if needed.

Cue: Keep the reach small.

Feel: Gentle abdominal work while your back stays comfortable.

Keep breathing. Avoid a large back arch; shorten the reach if control changes.

02

Pallof press

6–10 slow presses per side

Set up: Secure a light band at chest height. Stand sideways to the anchor and hold it at your chest.

Do: Press your hands forward, pause, then bring them back. Try to face the same direction.

Cue: Face the same direction.

Feel: Your trunk working against the sideways pull; your shoulders stay comfortable.

The band wants to turn you. Reduce its pull if you must twist, lean or hold your breath. No secure anchor? Choose another exercise.

03

Side plank

10–20 seconds per side

Set up: Lie on your side with your elbow below your shoulder. Bend your knees for an easier version.

Do: Lift your hips into a comfortable line. Keep breathing, then lower slowly.

Cue: Lift through your side.

Feel: Work along the side of your trunk and supporting hip; no shoulder or back pain.

End the hold before your position collapses. Longer is not the goal when control is gone.

04

Supported single-leg hinge

6–8 slow repetitions per side

Set up: Stand beside a stable support. Rest one hand on it and keep the standing knee soft.

Do: Send your hips backward as your free leg moves behind you. Return to standing. Use a small range at first.

Cue: Send the hips back.

Feel: Work around the standing hip and back of the thigh, with your hand helping balance.

Balance, breathing and a comfortable movement. Add load only when this version is familiar.

Think of carrying a full cup.

Anti-rotation means resisting an unwanted turn. Running includes normal rotation. The goal is not to remove it.

These exercises give you practice with trunk control. A twist in a video does not diagnose weakness, and this routine is not a treatment for hip or back pain. Start with a real movement question →

Choose your starting amount.

EquipmentMat. Band or cable for the press. Hand support for the hinge.
StartOne easy set of two or three familiar exercises.
RestEnough to repeat with control and normal breathing.

The doses below are starting examples, not a rehabilitation plan or an instruction to add every exercise to every week. Repeat before adding more. Challenging strength work still counts toward your training. [1]

Where does it fit?

Use a small familiar dose alongside an easy day or a strength session. Avoid introducing a demanding version just before a key run. Judge it by how you move and how your following runs feel. [1]

Sharp pain, joint pain or symptoms that change your movement are reasons to stop. Persistent symptoms need assessment. [2]

Your checkboxes are a temporary checklist on this page. They do not save a workout to FORM or Forge.

When should you progress?

Repeat the small amount first. When the movement stays comfortable and your following runs feel manageable, change one thing: a little more range, resistance or work. Keep the cue and normal breathing.

When should you stop?

Stop the set if you lose control or must hold your breath. Reduce the amount or choose an easier version. Stop for sharp pain, joint pain or symptoms that change how you move; persistent or recurring symptoms need assessment.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Control the movement. Keep breathing.
  2. Use a version you can repeat comfortably.
  3. A steadier exercise is not proof that a running problem is fixed.
The band gets heavier, but you have to twist to press it. Is that a useful progression?

Reduce the resistance. The exercise’s job is to control the pull while moving your arms. A heavier band that changes the movement misses that job.
