How do I use mobility work around running?

Choose a movement that feels restricted, explore a comfortable range, then check again. You do not need to stretch every area or force a bigger range before every run.

Choose one or two options.

Choose 1 or 2 options for a movement that feels restricted. Try the small amount once, then compare the movement again.

01

Gentle hip-front stretch

20–30 comfortable seconds per side

Set up: Kneel with one knee cushioned and the other foot forward. Use support if needed.

Do: Shift forward a little while keeping your trunk comfortable. Avoid arching your back to reach farther.

Cue: Shift a little; keep the back comfortable.

Feel: A gentle stretch near the front of the hip, without knee or back pain.

A gentle stretch near the front of the hip. Reduce it or stop if the knee or back hurts.

02

Ankle circles

5–10 slow circles each way

Set up: Sit or hold support with the foot free to move.

Do: Draw small comfortable circles with your foot. Keep the movement gentle.

Cue: Draw a small smooth circle.

Feel: Comfortable movement at the ankle, without pushing into a painful spot.

Smooth movement without pushing into a painful spot.

03

Calf stretch

20–30 comfortable seconds per side

Set up: Face a wall with one foot behind you, heel resting on the ground.

Do: Lean gently forward. Try a straight or slightly bent back knee in a comfortable range.

Cue: Keep the heel down gently.

Feel: A mild calf stretch; stop if you feel Achilles pain.

A gentle calf stretch. Do not push into Achilles pain.

04

Seated upper-body turn

5 slow turns per side

Set up: Sit comfortably with feet on the floor. Cross your arms loosely.

Do: Turn gently to one side and return. Let the movement stay comfortable.

Cue: Turn a little, then return.

Feel: A comfortable turn through your upper body, without cranking the shoulders.

An easy turn. Do not crank your shoulders or chase the largest angle.

Open the door gently.

Tightness is a feeling to investigate. It does not tell you by itself which muscle is short or why running feels difficult. Running does not automatically shorten everyone’s hip flexors.

Try a small amount, then check whether your intended movement feels easier. Do not treat a temporary change as a fixed running problem.

Where does it fit?

Keep the amount small enough to serve the next activity.
TimeA useful choice
Before a runGentle movement, then your gradual warm-up. Skip deep or unfamiliar stretching just to complete a list.
After or between runsA comfortable stretch or movement if you find it useful. Review how you feel afterward.
Persistent pain or limitationStop forcing the movement and get assessment. [1]

Mobility, strength and running practice can serve different needs. A stretch does not replace progressive strength work, and a video does not tell you which you need. Start with what you notice →

When should you progress?

Repeat the small amount first. When the movement stays comfortable and your following runs feel manageable, change one thing: a little more range, resistance or work. Keep the cue and normal breathing.

When should you stop?

Stop the set if you lose control or must hold your breath. Reduce the amount or choose an easier version. Stop for sharp pain, joint pain or symptoms that change how you move; persistent or recurring symptoms need assessment.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Choose a real movement question.
  2. Explore gently, then compare.
  3. A stretch is not a diagnosis or an injury cure.
One ankle feels stiffer. Should you force a long stretch until it matches the other?

No. Use comfortable movement and compare the response. Pain or a persistent problem needs assessment, not a stronger push.
