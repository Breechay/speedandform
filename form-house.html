HOUSE.

Meet early. Leave your bag. Run the city. Come back for coffee, recovery, and the people you trained with.

Running already has the streets. What it rarely has is a place to belong before and after them.

The run is only part of the morning.

It is 5:50 AM. Your bag is down. Coffee is already on. Someone is tying a shoe. Someone else is checking the route.

At six, everyone leaves. Some are running easy. Some have work to do. The city is the training ground.

Then everyone comes back somewhere. Water. Food. A shower if there is one. Ten minutes on the floor. Twenty minutes talking.

That is FORM House.

MeetA dependable early door. Bags down. Route up. Nobody is waiting on a sidewalk wondering where to go.
TrainThe streets, bridges, and track stay the field. The House holds the bag and the return. It does not move the run indoors.
RecoverWater, a place to sit, a shower if the host has one. Enough to come down. Not a clinic.

BelongThe people do not disappear at the last mile. Same door next time. You can leave when you need to.
Why now

Running is becoming a place people go to find people.

New running clubs and club events have grown sharply. Race fields are up. In earlier group research, social connection was the most common reason people joined.

Start light

It can begin with one door opening early.

A residency uses a lobby, lounge, or café corner for the early window. An annex is a small zone built only after the mornings prove they belong there. A standalone house is the full version, if the site and the use ever justify it.

Pilot

Give us a few mornings.

See what the place becomes.

Four to eight Thursdays or scheduled Saturdays. Bags down. Water. A seat. Coffee if the host has it. Then we look at four things: who arrived, who came back inside, how long they stayed, and what got in the way.

The first version does not need to be perfect. It needs to make a 6 AM morning noticeably better.
