Run Development / The Method

Develop the runner.

I look for ease. Running should click: fluid, connected, almost meditative. When it doesn’t, I look for what is getting in the way.

The practice

See. Choose. Practice. Repeat. Own.

01

Hear the out-of-tune note.

I don’t begin with a checklist of faults. I watch rhythm, tension, control, and what looks like it is costing more than the task requires. The same visible pattern can matter in one runner and be harmless in another.

02

Change what matters most.

There is always more I could say. I don’t say all of it. I choose the change most likely to free the run now. Not five things at once.

03

Watch the next rep.

A cue has to do something. I give it, then watch what changes: rhythm, control, effort, and what the athlete feels. If it does not help, it does not earn a place just because it sounds technically correct.

04

Frequency teaches the body.

Sometimes the useful dose is only ten minutes. Sometimes 400s become 800s, then a mile. Those are examples of exposure, not rules. The point is enough useful repetition for the athlete to stop borrowing the cue and start owning it.

05

Form is multiplied by every step.

A small restriction repeated thousands of times stops being small. That is why I care about the things that cost you. Strength can support the movement when the limitation is control or capacity. The goal is not perfect form. It is less unnecessary resistance.

06

Coach the thinking underneath the running.

Pace, heart rate, distance, and splits tell part of the story. I also listen to choices, thoughts, confidence, restraint, and how the work felt. How the athlete absorbed the work helps decide what comes next.

07

The work should stay with you.

The best outcome is not an athlete who needs me forever. It is a runner who notices more, understands more, and can make better decisions without waiting for a cue.

Like a kite upon the wind.

Not every run is easy. Even hard work can feel organized: confidence without unnecessary resistance.

What this is not

No universal model.

Not every difference is a problem.

A visible difference earns attention when it creates a meaningful cost in this runner, in this work.

Software is an instrument.

Video, pace, heart rate, and analysis tools can sharpen the observation. They do not replace the coaching decision.

Speed is not the only outcome.

Better movement, better judgment, and more ownership matter even when a stopwatch is not the question.

Reveal what wants to be set free.
