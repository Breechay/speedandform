Before you begin.

You have run four days and about 18–22 miles a week for the past four weeks, including a comfortable six- to seven-mile long run. You recover well enough to repeat that routine.

Keep the easy running easy.

Run/walk counts. Keep the pattern you already use, and an effort where you can speak in full sentences. Slow down for heat, hills or fatigue. Begin and finish easy; the preparation and easing down are part of each distance.

Every training run is easy. There are no required speed sessions or fast finishes.

If ten miles would take you much longer than 2½ hours, ask me for a version built on time. Check your event’s cutoff too. This is not a return-from-injury plan.

Keep a rest day before and after the long run when moving days. Do not bunch four runs into consecutive days. Kilometers use the same miles, rounded to one decimal; totals use unrounded values. The exact race distance stays 21.0975 km. The race is separate from the training total.

Let the response change the week.

  • Record distance, time, how the run felt and your recovery.
  • Skip a missed short run. Do not double the next day or repay miles on the long run.
  • After a missed long run, work from the last distance you completed comfortably. Do not squeeze two long runs into a week.
  • If you remain unusually tired at the next run, shorten it, go easier or rest. Repeat or reduce the last manageable week.
  • Keep the taper. Do not compress missed weeks into the race date; reconsider the date when needed.

If pain worsens, changes your movement or persists between runs, stop progressing and seek appropriate clinical advice. Illness and time away need an individual return decision.

Keep existing strength work familiar and manageable. Do not introduce new heavy leg work before the long run.

Practice what you will use.

Use earlier long runs to practice your race-day food, drink, equipment and walk breaks. Start the race at a restrained, familiar effort. A missed split is not a debt to recover.

Race week has two short easy runs before a Sunday race. Do not add a confidence workout. Adjust for conditions; illness or pain may mean not starting. The plan ends after the race and does not prescribe a next-day run.
