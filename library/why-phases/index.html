Why does a training program have phases?

A phase gives one priority more attention for a while. You keep practicing other abilities, then move the emphasis when it serves the goal.

Think of preparing a school performance.

A phase is a block of time with a main job. It could emphasize learning movements, building strength, adding muscle in a chosen area, or preparing for a race. There is no single sequence that every runner needs.

What changes, and what stays?

An illustration of changing emphasis, not an assigned program.
PartEarlier emphasisLater emphasis
Strength practiceLearn and repeat manageable movements.Make familiar work more challenging when ready.
A physique priorityGive one area more useful work.Move attention while maintaining earlier work.
Race preparationBuild a repeatable running routine.Practice more of the work the race asks for.

Different goals can need different orders. Keep the total work manageable. A new phase is not permission to increase every demand together. [1]

How do you use this as an athlete?

  1. 01

    Know today’s priority

    Ask what this phase is trying to develop.

  2. 02

    Follow the amount

    Do not restore old sets or miles just because they were familiar.

  3. 03

    Report the response

    Tell your coach how the work and recovery are going.

For an assigned FORM or Forge program, the current session is the instruction. This page explains the idea; it does not change that assignment.

Use it this week.

For your current training block, write 1 main priority and 2 things you are keeping in practice. Ask your coach if the reason for the current amount is unclear.

Three mistakes to catch.

  1. Changing phases only because the calendar changed.
  2. Restoring old sets without checking the new plan.
  3. Expecting every ability to be the main priority at once.

Where does it fit?

Phases organize priorities over time. The daily session still tells you what to do now.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. One clear priority for this phase.
  2. Keep the rest in practice.
  3. Move the emphasis for a reason, not novelty.
Your favorite exercise has fewer sets this phase. Does that mean it has been abandoned?

No. It may be maintained while another priority gets more attention. Check the whole program before adding back the old volume.
