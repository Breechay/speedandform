Race preparation

How to set your first half-marathon goal

The goal sets the paces, and the paces set the week. So the goal has to be honest.

A goal time is not a wish. It is the number every training pace in your plan gets derived from. Set it right and the whole build feels demanding but possible. Set it from ambition alone and the plan quietly breaks — starting with your easy days.

Start from evidence, not identity

The most honest starting point is a recent race — a 10K, a 5K, even a hard parkrun. A recent result carries information a goal pulled from the air never will. If you have no race, an honest current effort over a known distance is the next best thing.

FORM turns that starting point into a goal conversation: the campaign, the paces, and the week come from it together.

An ambitious goal breaks the easy days first

Here is the failure pattern: the goal is too fast, so threshold is set too fast, so every hard day is a small race, so you unconsciously slow the hard days and speed the easy ones until the week is one grey effort. Nothing in that week is training a specific thing anymore.

A right-sized goal keeps the contrast: hard days that are genuinely hard, easy days that are genuinely easy.

The goal can move — with evidence

Your first goal is a starting hypothesis, not a contract. As the weeks accumulate, FORM reads what you actually ran and tells you plainly what it sees — including when there is not enough evidence yet. If the fitness shows up early, the goal earns its upgrade.

Pick the number you can defend with a result, not the one you want to say out loud. The plan will take you from there.
