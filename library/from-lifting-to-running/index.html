Training both

How to start running when you already lift

You do not need to stop lifting to become a runner. You need a running dose that your current training can absorb.

The mistake is usually not starting to run. It is adding too much running, too much intensity, and too many new demands in the same week.

Begin with frequency you can repeat

Two or three short, easy runs can be enough to establish rhythm. The first goal is to make running normal—not to prove fitness in the first week.

Keep most of the early running controlled. Add duration gradually, and let the longest run grow only when the rest of the week is being handled well.

Protect the lower-body work that matters

Place demanding runs away from the heaviest lower-body sessions when possible. A hard interval session and a heavy hinge day may both be useful, but stacking them carelessly can make both worse.

You may need to reduce lower-body volume while running grows. That is not losing discipline. It is giving the current goal enough room to work.

Do not add running on top of the old week. Rebuild the week so lifting and running have places.

Choose the first challenge

A first 5K, 10K, or half marathon gives the running a destination. The distance matters less than having enough runway to build gradually and arrive prepared.

Let the identity follow the work

You do not have to decide whether you are “a lifter” or “a runner” before you begin. Start with the goal in front of you. Train it well. The next challenge can come later.
