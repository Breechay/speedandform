Race preparation

How a half-marathon week fits together

A race plan is not a pile of runs. Each day has a job, and the week works because those jobs support one another.

The first question is not, “How many hard workouts can I fit in?” It is, “What does this runner need next, and what can they recover from?”

Start with the race and the real week

A useful plan begins with the race date, current running, available training days, and the longest work the runner can handle now. A parent, physician, or shift worker may have the same goal as someone with a wide-open schedule, but they should not receive the same week.

The plan has to survive the life around it. Four well-placed runs can beat six runs that are constantly skipped, moved, or forced through fatigue.

Give every run one clear job

  • Easy running builds repeatable volume without turning every day into a test.
  • Threshold work develops the ability to hold a strong, controlled effort for longer.
  • Intervals or speed add measured intensity without swallowing the whole week.
  • The long run extends durability and gradually carries more race-specific work.
  • Protected or rest days keep the important sessions worth doing.
The week is successful when the hard work is hard enough, the easy work is easy enough, and the runner can return next week ready to progress.

You do not need to run 13.1 miles in training

For many first-time half-marathoners, the longest run stays shorter than race distance. The goal is not to prove the race can be completed in advance. The goal is to arrive healthy, conditioned, and familiar with sustained effort.

How far the long run reaches depends on pace, experience, weekly volume, and recovery. The number should come from the runner’s context—not a rule copied from someone else’s plan.

Progress one demand at a time

A good build does not increase mileage, long-run duration, threshold volume, and interval difficulty all at once. One demand moves while the rest of the week supports it. That is what makes the plan feel clear instead of chaotic.
