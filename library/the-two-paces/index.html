Running

The two numbers that organize the whole plan

Threshold and the easy ceiling. Once those two paces are set, every run in the week has a place.

Runners collect paces the way lifters collect exercises — race pace, tempo pace, interval pace, recovery pace. Most of them are noise. Two numbers do the organizing, and everything else hangs off them.

Threshold: the pace you can hold, extended

Threshold is controlled hard. Not a sprint, not comfortable — the effort you could sustain for close to an hour on a good day. Training slightly below and at this line is what moves your race times, because it raises the pace you can hold without falling apart.

In FORM, threshold is a band, not a single number. You sit inside it. If you are constantly at the fast edge, you are racing your training, and the plan will tell on you.

The easy ceiling: the line that protects tomorrow

The easy ceiling is the pace you stay under on easy days. It is not a target to reach — it is a limit to respect. Its whole job is to make sure today's run does not tax what tomorrow's session needs.

Most self-coached runners break their week here, not on the hard days. Easy creeps ten seconds faster each week until nothing in the plan is actually easy, and then the hard days flatten.

Everything else is placement

Once those two numbers are honest, the week almost writes itself. Hard days sit at or near threshold. Easy days sit under the ceiling. The long run borrows from both. That is why FORM shows your two numbers on the profile and on every session — you should never have to guess where today belongs.

Your two numbers are set from real evidence, and they move when your running says they should — not when motivation does.
