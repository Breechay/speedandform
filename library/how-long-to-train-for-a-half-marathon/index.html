More weeks are useful when they give you room.

Room to establish the routine. Room to repeat a week. Room to recover without trying to repay missed running. More calendar time is not a requirement to make every week harder.

Ordinary walking, cycling, and gym fitness can support your activity, but they do not tell you how much running you can currently repeat. Look at the actual outings.

For a run/walk goal, review the whole outing, including the planned walking. Continuous running is not the price of entry.

Check the practical fit.

  • Can your week hold the scheduled outings and rest?
  • Does the opening week resemble something you can already manage?
  • Does the longest outing fit your available time? If the ten-mile peak would take substantially more than 2½ hours, use a plan adapted by time.
  • Does your expected race duration fit the event’s actual cutoff?
  • Are you recovering well enough to continue, without symptoms restricting normal training?

These are questions for choosing a plan, not automatic medical clearance or a prediction of your finish time.
