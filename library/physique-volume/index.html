Should every muscle get the same amount of work?

The amount of work should fit your goal, current training and recovery. A priority can get more attention while other areas keep training.

Think of a study timetable.

Training volume is the amount of work. In the gym, sets per exercise or muscle group are one way to count it. Sets are not identical: load, effort and exercise choice matter too.

Build, maintain, then review.

Three decisions in a focused strength phase.
DecisionWhat it meansWhat to check
Build a priorityGive a chosen area more useful practice.Repetitions, loads, control and progress over time.
Maintain other areasKeep enough familiar work to hold what you built.Whether strength and movement remain stable.
Review the combinationCount lifting, running and life demands.Energy, recovery and whether the work is repeatable.

No fixed number of sets fits every person. You do not need to change exercises every week to make progress. Start with a manageable structure and review the response. [1]

When should the emphasis move?

When the goal, progress or recovery asks for it. A phase gives a priority time to develop; it is not a calendar rule that you must move on before the work has done its job.

If you follow an assigned FORM or Forge program, use that prescription. This explanation does not add sets to your account.

Use it this week.

Look at your current 7-day plan. Name 1 strength priority and count the sets assigned to it. Review the rest of the work before proposing any extra sets.

Three mistakes to catch.

  1. Adding priority work without reviewing the total.
  2. Treating maintenance as doing nothing.
  3. Changing exercises every week just for novelty.

Where does it fit?

Volume is the amount of strength work within the wider week. A priority needs room, recovery and a clear reason.

The part to take with you

Remember this.

  1. Give the priority a clear share of the work.
  2. Maintenance is still training.
  3. More sets are useful only if you can build on them.
You add more shoulder work but keep every other set unchanged. What else has changed?

The total training load has grown. Review the whole week and recovery, rather than treating the new priority as a free extra.
