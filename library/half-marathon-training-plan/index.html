Before you start.

This plan is for finishing a half marathon. It does not promise a finish time or begin from no running. Everyday walking alone does not meet the starting point.

If 10 mi would take substantially more than 2½ hours, ask for a progression adapted by time. That is a reason to adapt this plan, not a universal danger threshold or an instruction to replace the long outing with 150 minutes.

Check your event’s actual cutoff. This is not a return-from-injury plan. If symptoms are restricting your normal training, use an individual return plan first.

Keep the effort easy.

Start gently. Finish with reserve.

Begin with five minutes of comfortable walking or easy jogging. Ease down during the final five minutes. Both are included in the distance shown.

In the middle, keep an effort where you can speak in full sentences. Slow down when heat, hills, or fatigue make that harder. There are no required fast finishes or speed sessions.

Walk breaks belong.

Use the run/walk pattern that already works for you, from the start of each outing. Keep it familiar as the distance grows.

Do not remove walk breaks and extend distance at the same time. Shorten the running portions if needed, without trying to make up time afterward.

The full plan.

Weeks 4 and 7 step back. Week 10 is the longest outing, followed by two lighter training weeks. Advance when the work stays manageable and you recover well.

Two easy outings, then the race. No extra confidence workout. With race distance, this week is 17.1 mi; the training total above excludes the race.

Week 12 contains four training miles plus the race, approximately 17.1094 miles in all. The table assumes a Sunday race. Keep the rest day before and after the long outing when changing days. Do not bunch four outings into four consecutive days. If those days do not fit, adapt the schedule.

Kilometers are converted from the same miles and rounded to one decimal. Totals use unrounded distances, so displayed cells can differ slightly from their displayed total. Race distance is exactly 21.0975 km, approximately 13.1094 miles.

When the week changes.

  1. Check the response. Keep a note of distance, elapsed time, how the outing felt, and recovery. Finishing the row alone is not the reason to move on.
  2. Skip missed short outings. Do not double the next day, use the rest day before the long outing, or add the missed miles to Sunday.
  3. Missed the long outing? Reassess from the last one you completed comfortably. Do not squeeze two long outings into a week.
  4. Still unusually tired at the next outing? Shorten it, go easier, or rest. Repeat or reduce the last manageable week instead of automatically extending.
  5. Do not compress the plan to meet the date. A disrupted week, illness, or time away can change what is reasonable. There is no single return dose. Keep the taper and reconsider the race date if needed.

If pain worsens, changes your movement, or persists between outings, stop the progression and seek appropriate clinical advice.

Strength can stay familiar.

If you already have a manageable strength routine, you can keep 15–20 minutes after Tuesday and Friday when recovery allows. It is optional and separate from the running total.

You do not need to start lifting to use this plan. Avoid introducing unfamiliar heavy leg work before the long outing.

Arrive with something left.

Race week has only two short easy outings before the race. Keep your familiar meals, equipment, and sleep routine. Leave the confidence workout behind.

Practice your race-day food, drink, and any walk breaks during earlier long outings. Begin the race at a restrained, familiar effort. A missed split is not a debt to recover.

Adjust your approach if the weather or your condition changes. Illness or pain may mean not starting. After the race, the plan ends; it does not prescribe a next-day run or an immediate return to peak mileage.

Not at this starting point yet?

Build a repeatable running or run/walk routine first. The free NHS Couch to 5K program is one option: three outings a week over nine weeks or longer, working toward 30 minutes of running.

Finishing that program does not automatically prepare you for this plan. Reassess the four-outing routine, weekly distance, and comfortable long outing before beginning these twelve weeks.
